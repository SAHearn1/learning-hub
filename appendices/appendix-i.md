# Appendix I: Educator Self-Care and Regulation Plan

## Purpose

Educators working with trauma-affected students are at significant risk for secondary traumatic stress (STS), compassion fatigue, and burnout. The FG2G curriculum recognizes that educator well-being is not a luxury — it is a prerequisite for effective facilitation. This appendix provides structured tools for educators to monitor, maintain, and restore their own regulation, using the same 5Rs framework they facilitate for students.

> *"You cannot pour from an empty watering can. Educator self-care in FG2G is not selfish — it is the most generous thing you can do for your students, because a regulated educator is the foundation of a regulated classroom."*

## Educator Regulation Self-Assessment

### Daily Check-In (30 seconds)

Rate yourself on the same 0-100 scale used for students:

| Score Range | State | Signs | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80-100 | Thriving | Energized, present, creative, patient | Maintain current practices; share energy with colleagues |
| 60-79 | Regulated | Functional, focused, generally positive | Continue with awareness; use preventive strategies |
| 40-59 | Coping | Fatigued, reactive, going through motions | Activate self-care plan; seek peer support; modify schedule if possible |
| 20-39 | Struggling | Overwhelmed, irritable, dreading work, detached | Seek immediate support; activate emergency self-care; consider mental health consultation |
| 0-19 | Crisis | Unable to function effectively; emotional flooding | Step back from direct student contact if possible; contact supervisor; access professional support |

### Weekly Reflection Prompts

Complete these in your garden journal or professional growth portfolio:

1. What was my average regulation level this week?
2. What moments brought me into my highest regulation?
3. What moments challenged my regulation the most?
4. Which self-care practices did I actually use this week?
5. What do I need to adjust for next week?

## The 5Rs for Educator Self-Care

### Root: Grounding Practices for Educators

| Practice | Description | When to Use | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning garden moment | Spend 5 minutes in the garden before students arrive; observe, breathe, ground | Daily, before school | 5 min |
| Sensory reset | Keep a personal sensory toolkit (smooth stone, essential oil, textured fabric) at your desk | During transitions, after difficult moments | 1-2 min |
| Threshold ritual | Before entering your classroom, pause at the door; take three breaths; set an intention | Before each class period | 30 sec |
| Grounding walk | Walk the garden perimeter slowly, noticing five sensory details | During planning period or lunch | 10 min |
| End-of-day release | Before leaving school, spend 3 minutes in the garden; mentally leave work stress there | Daily, after school | 3 min |

### Regulate: Maintaining Your Window of Tolerance

| Strategy | Description | Activation Level |
|---|---|---|
| Box breathing | Inhale 4 counts, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4 | Slightly elevated; in the moment |
| Progressive muscle release | Systematically tense and release muscle groups | Moderate tension; during a break |
| Garden digging | 10 minutes of vigorous garden work (digging, weeding, turning compost) | High energy; need physical discharge |
| Cold water reset | Run cold water over wrists for 30 seconds | Acute stress; immediate reset needed |
| Peer co-regulation | Sit with a regulated colleague; match their breathing pace | Sustained dysregulation; need relational support |
| Movement break | Walk, stretch, or do light exercise | Low energy or afternoon fatigue |

### Reflect: Processing Your Experience

| Practice | Description | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Garden journaling | Write or sketch in your garden journal; process the day's experiences | Daily (5-10 min) |
| Peer reflection pairs | Meet with a trusted colleague to share and process | Weekly (20-30 min) |
| Supervision / consultation | Meet with a clinical consultant or mentor to discuss challenging situations | Monthly (60 min) |
| PLC processing time | Dedicated time in PLC meetings for emotional processing, not just logistics | Monthly (15-20 min) |
| Personal therapy | Professional support for your own processing and growth | As needed / ongoing |

### Restore: Recovering from Difficult Moments

| Situation | Recovery Strategy |
|---|---|
| After a student crisis | Debrief with a colleague within 24 hours; journal the experience; assess your own regulation before returning to teaching |
| After an emotionally demanding day | Extended garden time; avoid screen time; engage in physical activity; connect with a supportive person |
| After witnessing student trauma disclosure | Access clinical consultation; practice self-compassion; remind yourself of professional boundaries |
| During a period of sustained stress | Audit your schedule for recovery time; reduce non-essential commitments; communicate needs to supervisor |
| When you notice compassion fatigue signs | Take a personal day if possible; reconnect with your "why"; engage in activities that bring joy outside of work |

### Reconnect: Finding Purpose and Connection

| Practice | Description | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose journaling | Write about why you chose this work; what impact you're making | Monthly |
| Celebration documentation | Keep a file of student growth moments, thank-you notes, and success stories | Ongoing |
| Colleague appreciation | Express genuine appreciation to a colleague | Weekly |
| Community connection | Participate in community garden events or partner activities as a participant, not a facilitator | Quarterly |
| Professional growth | Engage in learning that excites you, not just required PD | Ongoing |
| Mentorship | Mentor a newer educator; sharing your experience reinforces your own growth | As available |

## Secondary Traumatic Stress Warning Signs

### Know Your Signs

| Domain | Warning Signs |
|---|---|
| Physical | Chronic fatigue; headaches; gastrointestinal issues; insomnia; appetite changes |
| Emotional | Irritability; numbness; anxiety; sadness; helplessness; decreased empathy |
| Cognitive | Difficulty concentrating; intrusive thoughts about students; hypervigilance; cynicism |
| Behavioral | Social withdrawal; increased substance use; absenteeism; avoidance of certain students or tasks |
| Professional | Reduced effectiveness; boundary violations; loss of motivation; questioning career choice |
| Relational | Difficulty in personal relationships; social withdrawal; overprotectiveness of own children |

### When to Seek Professional Help

Seek professional support if you experience:
- Symptoms lasting more than 2 weeks
- Significant impairment in daily functioning
- Intrusive thoughts or nightmares about student experiences
- Increasing use of alcohol or other substances to cope
- Thoughts of self-harm or hopelessness
- Inability to maintain professional boundaries
