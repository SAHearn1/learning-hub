# Appendix A: Glossary of Key Terms

## 5Rs Framework Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **5Rs Framework** | The pedagogical backbone of the FG2G curriculum: Root, Regulate, Reflect, Restore, Reconnect. A structured sequence that guides every learning interaction. |
| **Root Phase** | The entry point for every learning session. Establishes safety, assesses readiness, and sets learning intentions through grounding activities. |
| **Regulate Phase** | The co-regulation and stabilization phase. Activated when a student's regulation level falls below the threshold for productive learning. |
| **Reflect Phase** | The active learning and metacognition phase. Standards-aligned academic content is delivered through the TRACE protocol and reasoning moves. |
| **Restore Phase** | The error recovery phase. Errors are reframed as "brain growth moments" and analyzed systematically rather than simply corrected. |
| **Reconnect Phase** | The application and integration phase. Learning is connected to real-world contexts, community, and the student's own life. |
| **Phase Transition** | The movement from one 5Rs phase to another, governed by regulation levels and learning progress. Transitions are always communicated transparently. |

## TRACE Protocol Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **TRACE Protocol** | Think, Reason, Articulate, Check, Extend -- the cognitive engagement framework embedded within the Reflect phase. |
| **Think** | The first TRACE step: "What do you notice first?" Activates observation and prior knowledge. |
| **Reason** | The second TRACE step: "What's your plan of attack?" Develops strategic thinking. |
| **Articulate** | The third TRACE step: "Talk me through your thinking." Builds mathematical and scientific discourse. |
| **Check** | The fourth TRACE step: "How do you know you're right?" Develops self-verification habits. |
| **Extend** | The fifth TRACE step: "What else could you do with this?" Promotes transfer and generalization. |

## Reasoning Moves

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **DECOMPOSE** | Break complex problems into manageable parts. |
| **COMPARE** | Identify similarities and differences between concepts. |
| **QUESTION** | Challenge assumptions and probe deeper. |
| **VERIFY** | Check work through alternative methods. |
| **GENERALIZE** | Identify patterns and form rules. |
| **TRANSFORM** | Reframe or represent a problem differently. |
| **CONNECT** | Link ideas across domains and to real-world contexts. |

## Regulation and Trauma-Informed Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs)** | Potentially traumatic events occurring before age 18, including abuse, neglect, and household dysfunction. Originally identified by Felitti and Anda (1998). |
| **Co-regulation** | The process by which a calm, attuned adult helps a dysregulated individual return to their window of tolerance through relational and sensory support. |
| **Dysregulation** | A state in which the autonomic nervous system is outside the window of tolerance, manifesting as hyperarousal (fight/flight) or hypoarousal (freeze/shutdown). |
| **Grounding** | Techniques that anchor a person to the present moment through sensory engagement, reducing anxiety and promoting safety. |
| **Hyperarousal** | A state above the window of tolerance characterized by anxiety, agitation, impulsivity, aggression, or rapid thinking. |
| **Hypoarousal** | A state below the window of tolerance characterized by numbness, withdrawal, flat affect, dissociation, or apparent apathy. |
| **Neuroception** | The unconscious process by which the nervous system evaluates risk and safety in the environment (Porges, 2011). |
| **Non-anxious Presence** | An educator's capacity to remain calm and warm regardless of student behavior, providing a stable relational anchor. |
| **Polyvagal Theory** | Stephen Porges' theory describing three hierarchical states of the autonomic nervous system: ventral vagal (social engagement), sympathetic (fight/flight), dorsal vagal (freeze/collapse). |
| **Regulation Level** | A 0-100 scale used in the FG2G curriculum to assess a student's current state of autonomic regulation. Above 60 permits learning; below 40 triggers the Regulate phase. |
| **Self-regulation** | The independent capacity to monitor and manage one's own emotional and physiological state. Develops through repeated experiences of co-regulation. |
| **Toxic Stress** | Prolonged activation of the stress response system without adequate adult support, leading to disrupted brain development and impaired learning (Shonkoff et al., 2012). |
| **Trauma-informed** | An approach that recognizes the widespread impact of trauma, integrates knowledge of trauma into practices and policies, and actively resists re-traumatization. |
| **Window of Tolerance** | The zone of arousal within which a person can function effectively -- alert, present, and able to think clearly (Siegel, 1999). |

## Garden and Curriculum Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Dual-Purpose Pedagogy** | The FG2G principle that every instructional activity simultaneously serves academic learning goals and social-emotional development goals. |
| **Living Learning Lab** | The garden-based outdoor learning environment designed according to FG2G specifications, with zones aligned to the 5Rs framework. |
| **Regulation Pod** | A semi-enclosed space within the Living Learning Lab designed for active co-regulation and self-regulation. |
| **RootGuide** | The AI-powered tutoring system that implements the 5Rs and TRACE protocol in digital learning sessions with dysregulation detection. |
| **RootWork** | The overarching term for the FG2G curriculum's approach to trauma-informed, garden-based education. |
| **Sensory Garden** | A dedicated space within the Living Learning Lab planted with aromatic, tactile, and visually calming plants for regulation support. |
| **Therapeutic Horticulture** | Garden-based activities designed to promote psychological and physiological well-being, informed by clinical research but delivered in educational settings. |
