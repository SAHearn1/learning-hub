# Appendix D: Co-Regulation Strategy Menu

## Overview

This appendix provides a comprehensive menu of co-regulation strategies organized by tier, arousal state, and grade band. Educators should select strategies based on the student's current presentation and developmental level.

## Tier 1: In-Context Supports

These strategies can be deployed without interrupting the learning activity. Use when regulation level is 41-60 (borderline).

### For Hyperarousal (Above the Window)

| Strategy | Description | Grade Bands |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Acknowledgment** | "I can see this is hard. You're working through something." | All |
| **Normalization** | "Lots of people find this part tricky. That's normal." | All |
| **Slow your own pace** | Deliberately slow your speech, lower your volume, relax your posture | All |
| **Proximity** | Move closer without crowding; sit at the student's level | All |
| **Offer choice** | "Would you like to keep going or switch to a different part?" | All |
| **Reduce stimulation** | Lower voice, reduce visual clutter, move to quieter area | All |
| **Physical task** | Redirect to a calming garden task: watering, weeding, sorting seeds | All |
| **Breathing cue** | "Let's take three slow breaths together. In through the nose, out through the mouth." | All |

### For Hypoarousal (Below the Window)

| Strategy | Description | Grade Bands |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Gentle activation** | "Let's stand up and stretch for a moment." | All |
| **Sensory input** | Offer a mint, cold water, or a textured object to hold | All |
| **Movement** | Walk to the garden, carry supplies, water plants | All |
| **Direct engagement** | Ask a concrete, answerable question: "What color is that leaf?" | All |
| **Peer connection** | Partner with an engaged peer for a brief collaborative task | 3-5, 6-8, 9-12 |
| **Temperature change** | Offer cold water; move to a sunny or shaded area | All |
| **Strong scent** | Offer rosemary, mint, or lemon balm from the sensory garden | All |

## Tier 2: Active Interventions

These strategies require a brief pause in the learning activity. Use when regulation level is 21-40 (dysregulated).

### Breathing Exercises

| Exercise | Instructions | Grade Bands |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Balloon breathing** | "Breathe in slowly like you're filling a balloon in your belly. Now let the air out slowly." | K-2 |
| **Square breathing** | "Breathe in for 4 counts, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4." Draw a square in the air. | 3-5, 6-8 |
| **5-4-3-2-1 grounding** | Name 5 things you see, 4 you hear, 3 you can touch, 2 you smell, 1 you taste. | All |
| **Breathing roots** | "Imagine roots growing from your feet into the earth as you breathe in. They spread wide as you breathe out." | All |
| **Garden breathing** | Hold a fragrant herb. "Breathe in the smell slowly. Now breathe out like you're blowing a dandelion." | All |

### Physical Regulation Activities

| Activity | Instructions | Grade Bands |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Garden walk** | Walk the perimeter of the garden slowly, noticing one new thing at each corner. | All |
| **Heavy work** | Carry soil bags, push a wheelbarrow, dig with a shovel -- activities that provide proprioceptive input. | All |
| **Water play** | Wash produce, water plants with a watering can, clean tools at the wash station. | K-2, 3-5 |
| **Soil contact** | Hands directly in soil -- mixing compost, filling pots, preparing beds. | All |
| **Seed sorting** | Sort seeds by size, color, or type -- a fine motor task that channels focus. | All |
| **Progressive muscle relaxation** | Tense and release muscle groups, starting from feet and moving upward. | 6-8, 9-12 |

## Tier 3: Extended Regulation

These strategies involve leaving the primary learning activity. Use when Tier 1 and Tier 2 strategies are insufficient or when the student needs more than 5 minutes of regulation support.

### Regulation Pod Activities

| Activity | Duration | Description |
|----------|----------|-------------|
| **Sensory garden visit** | 5-10 min | Walk through the sensory garden, touching textured plants, smelling herbs |
| **Regulation pod retreat** | 5-15 min | Use the semi-enclosed regulation pod with calming tools (weighted blanket, fidgets, calming visuals) |
| **Journaling** | 5-10 min | Write or draw in growth journal about current feelings and what might help |
| **Guided imagery** | 5-8 min | Listen to a brief guided visualization (audio recording available) |
| **One-on-one co-regulation** | 5-15 min | Educator sits with student in a calm space, maintaining non-anxious presence; no demands for verbal processing |

### Return Protocol

After Tier 3 regulation:

1. **Reassess regulation level** -- "Where are you now on the scale?"
2. **Do not require explanation** -- "You don't have to tell me what happened. I just want to make sure you're okay."
3. **Offer re-entry choices** -- "Would you like to rejoin the group, work independently, or start with a garden task?"
4. **Brief Root phase** -- Repeat a shortened grounding check-in before returning to Reflect.
5. **No penalty** -- Time spent in regulation is never treated as lost instructional time or punished in any way.

## Educator Self-Regulation

Educators must maintain their own regulation to co-regulate students effectively:

### Quick Self-Check
- Am I breathing slowly and deeply?
- Is my voice calm and low?
- Is my body relaxed (uncrossed arms, soft face)?
- Am I reacting to the behavior or responding to the need?

### When You Feel Dysregulated
- Take three slow breaths before responding.
- Lower your voice rather than raising it.
- If possible, tag in a colleague for a brief break.
- Use the garden -- a few minutes of weeding or watering can reset your own nervous system.
- Remember: the student's behavior is communication, not defiance.
