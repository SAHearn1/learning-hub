# Appendix C: TRACE Protocol Quick Reference Cards

## Overview

These quick reference cards are designed for educators to keep accessible during instruction. Each card provides the TRACE prompt, the cognitive skill it develops, sample questions for each grade band, and the reasoning moves it activates.

## THINK Card

**Core Prompt**: "What do you notice first?"

**Cognitive Skill**: Observation, prior knowledge activation

| Grade Band | Sample Questions |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **K-2** | "What do you see? What does it remind you of? What colors/shapes do you notice?" |
| **3-5** | "What stands out to you? What do you already know about this? What's familiar?" |
| **6-8** | "What are the key features here? What prior knowledge is relevant? What patterns do you see?" |
| **9-12** | "What assumptions are embedded in this problem? What contextual factors matter? What's the scope?" |

**Reasoning Moves Activated**: DECOMPOSE, COMPARE

**Garden Connection**: "Look at our garden bed. What do you notice today that's different from last week?"

**If the student is stuck**: Do not answer for them. Instead: "Take your time. Start with one thing you notice." Or: "What if you looked at just this part?"

---

## REASON Card

**Core Prompt**: "What's your plan of attack?"

**Cognitive Skill**: Strategic thinking, planning

| Grade Band | Sample Questions |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **K-2** | "What should we do first? Then what? How will we start?" |
| **3-5** | "What strategy could you try? What are your options? Which tool might help?" |
| **6-8** | "What approach makes the most sense here? Why? What's your first step and why?" |
| **9-12** | "What method will you use and what's your justification? What alternatives did you consider?" |

**Reasoning Moves Activated**: DECOMPOSE, TRANSFORM, QUESTION

**Garden Connection**: "We need to transplant these seedlings. What's our plan? What do we need to think about before we start?"

**If the student is stuck**: "What's one thing you could try, even if you're not sure it's right?" Or: "How did we solve something similar before?"

---

## ARTICULATE Card

**Core Prompt**: "Talk me through your thinking."

**Cognitive Skill**: Explanation, discourse, metacognitive verbalization

| Grade Band | Sample Questions |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **K-2** | "Tell me what you did. Show me how. Why did you do it that way?" |
| **3-5** | "Explain your thinking to me. Why does that make sense? How would you teach this to a friend?" |
| **6-8** | "Walk me through your reasoning step by step. What's the logic connecting each step? Where did you make a choice?" |
| **9-12** | "Justify your approach. What evidence supports your reasoning? How would you explain this to someone who disagrees?" |

**Reasoning Moves Activated**: GENERALIZE, CONNECT, COMPARE

**Garden Connection**: "Explain to me why you planted those seeds at that depth. What's your reasoning?"

**If the student is stuck**: "Start from the beginning. What was the first thing you thought?" Or: "Pretend I don't know anything about this. Teach me."

---

## CHECK Card

**Core Prompt**: "How do you know you're right?"

**Cognitive Skill**: Self-verification, error detection

| Grade Band | Sample Questions |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **K-2** | "Let's check! Does that look right? Can we count again to make sure?" |
| **3-5** | "How can you check your work? Is there another way to solve it? Does your answer make sense?" |
| **6-8** | "What would convince you this is correct? Can you verify using a different method? What could disprove your answer?" |
| **9-12** | "What's the strongest test of your reasoning? Where are the potential weaknesses? What would a skeptic ask?" |

**Reasoning Moves Activated**: VERIFY, QUESTION, TRANSFORM

**Garden Connection**: "We predicted these seeds would germinate in 7 days. How will we check? What will we do if our prediction was wrong?"

**If the student is stuck**: "What if you tried it with an easier example first?" Or: "Does the answer seem reasonable? Too big? Too small?"

---

## EXTEND Card

**Core Prompt**: "What else could you do with this?"

**Cognitive Skill**: Transfer, generalization, creative application

| Grade Band | Sample Questions |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **K-2** | "Where else do you see this? What if we changed one thing? Can you make up your own?" |
| **3-5** | "What other problems could you solve this way? How is this like something in your life? What if the numbers were bigger?" |
| **6-8** | "How does this connect to other things we've learned? Where would you use this outside school? What new questions does this raise?" |
| **9-12** | "How does this principle apply across domains? What are the broader implications? Can you pose a new problem based on what you've learned?" |

**Reasoning Moves Activated**: GENERALIZE, CONNECT, TRANSFORM

**Garden Connection**: "We measured the growth rate of our tomato plants. Where else could we use this kind of measurement? What if we wanted to predict next month's growth?"

**If the student is stuck**: "Think about your life outside school. Where might this show up?" Or: "What if you changed one part of the problem?"

## Monitoring for Dysregulation During TRACE

At any point during the TRACE protocol, watch for dysregulation signals:

| Signal | Response |
|--------|----------|
| Frustration escalating (clenched fists, raised voice) | Pause TRACE. Acknowledge: "This is challenging. That means your brain is working hard." Offer a brief grounding activity. |
| Shutdown (flat affect, head down, not responding) | Pause TRACE. Soften voice: "Let's take a breath together." Offer choice: "Would you like to keep going or take a garden break?" |
| Shame response ("I'm stupid," "I can't do this") | Stop immediately. Reframe: "This is a brain growth moment. Your brain is building new connections right now." Move to Restore phase if needed. |
| Avoidance (changing subject, asking to leave) | Acknowledge: "It makes sense to want to step away from something hard." Offer scaffolding: "What if we tried just the first part together?" |

**Key Principle**: The TRACE protocol is never worth a student's regulation. If engagement with TRACE is causing dysregulation, transition to the Regulate phase. The learning can resume when the student is ready.
