# Family Engagement Strategies

## Overview

Family engagement in FG2G extends far beyond traditional parent involvement models. Rather than viewing families as recipients of information or volunteers for school events, FG2G positions families as essential partners in the therapeutic and educational ecosystem. The garden serves as a uniquely accessible bridge between school and home — a shared space where diverse families can contribute their knowledge, culture, and care.

> *"The garden doesn't ask for credentials. It welcomes every hand that's willing to tend it. Family engagement in FG2G follows the same principle — every family has something to grow."*

## Family Engagement Framework

### Epstein's Six Types of Involvement — FG2G Adaptations

| Involvement Type | Traditional Approach | FG2G Garden-Based Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Parenting** | Information sessions on child development | Garden-at-home kits with family regulation activities; 5Rs parent workshops |
| **Communicating** | Report cards and newsletters | Regulation trend sharing via Family Portal; garden photo updates; harvest celebrations |
| **Volunteering** | Classroom helpers, field trip chaperones | Garden workdays; harvest preparation; cultural planting events; mentorship |
| **Learning at Home** | Homework help tips | Family garden projects; home regulation practice; seasonal activities |
| **Decision-Making** | PTA/PTO participation | Family advisory council for garden design; curriculum input sessions |
| **Community Collaboration** | Resource referrals | Family-led community garden events; intergenerational planting projects |

## Engagement Strategies by Phase

### Root Phase: Building Trust and Safety

Before families can engage deeply, they must feel safe and welcome. Many families of trauma-affected children have their own complicated relationships with schools.

**Strategies:**

| Strategy | Description | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome garden visit | Invite families to experience the garden before the academic year | Schedule open garden hours during enrollment/orientation |
| Home language communication | All FG2G materials available in family's home language | Translation services for key documents; bilingual garden signage |
| Cultural garden space | Include plants and garden practices from families' cultural traditions | Survey families about cultural planting traditions; incorporate into garden design |
| No-barrier entry | Remove barriers to participation (childcare, food, transportation, timing) | Provide childcare at events; serve food; vary event times; offer transport |
| Relationship-first contact | Initial family contact focuses on listening, not informing | Home visits or informal meetings where educator learns about family |

### Regulate Phase: Supporting Home Regulation

**Strategies:**

| Strategy | Description | Materials |
|---|---|---|
| 5Rs at Home Guide | Family-friendly explanation of each phase with home activities | Printed and digital guide in multiple languages |
| Family regulation toolkit | Garden-based regulation strategies families can use at home | Toolkit with sensory items, activity cards, garden seeds |
| Co-regulation workshops | Interactive sessions where families practice co-regulation together | Workshop curriculum (2 hours); childcare provided |
| Garden mindfulness series | Monthly family garden sessions focused on regulation through gardening | Session plans for guided garden meditation and sensory activities |
| Home garden starter kits | Seeds, soil, and containers for families to begin home gardens | Seasonal kits distributed at family events |

### Reflect Phase: Engaging Families as Knowledge Partners

**Strategies:**

| Strategy | Description | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Family knowledge harvest | Invite families to share gardening expertise, cultural food practices, and traditional knowledge | Structured interview process; classroom presentations; garden design input |
| Family TRACE nights | Families experience the TRACE protocol through garden investigations together | Evening events with garden stations and guided inquiry activities |
| Multilingual garden labels | Families create garden labels in their home languages | Label-making workshops; multilingual plant identification guides |
| Family science nights | Families and students investigate garden science together | Hands-on stations: soil testing, seed germination, composting science |
| Storytelling circles | Families share stories connecting gardening, food, and cultural identity | Facilitated storytelling events in the garden |

### Restore Phase: Normalizing Struggle Together

**Strategies:**

| Strategy | Description | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Family garden troubleshooting | Families help diagnose and solve garden problems alongside students | Problem-solving sessions during garden workdays |
| Growth story sharing | Families share stories of overcoming challenges (not trauma disclosure) | Structured sharing protocols at family events |
| Home garden check-ins | Families and students problem-solve home garden challenges together | Photo-sharing via Family Portal; virtual troubleshooting sessions |
| Family-student error analysis | Families experience the Restore phase by analyzing shared cooking or garden mistakes | Activity guides for family error analysis conversations |

### Reconnect Phase: Families as Community Partners

**Strategies:**

| Strategy | Description | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Harvest celebrations | Families gather to celebrate garden harvests with shared meals | Seasonal harvest events (3-4 per year) |
| Community garden projects | Families participate in expanding garden impact to the broader community | Weekend community garden workdays |
| Family mentorship | Experienced families mentor new FG2G families | Formal mentorship matching; orientation support |
| Cultural food events | Families prepare and share traditional dishes using garden produce | Cultural food festivals; recipe collection and sharing |
| Intergenerational planting | Grandparents and extended family members participate in garden activities | Dedicated intergenerational garden sessions |

## Communication Strategy

### Communication Channels

| Channel | Content | Frequency | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| Family Portal | Regulation trends, academic progress, garden updates | Continuous access | Individual families |
| Garden newsletter | Seasonal garden news, upcoming events, home activity suggestions | Monthly | All families |
| Photo/video updates | Garden activities, student work, celebration highlights | Weekly | All families (via app or text) |
| Personal outreach | Individual student updates, concerns, celebrations | As needed | Individual families |
| Family events | In-person engagement, relationship building | Monthly | All families invited |

### Communication Principles

1. **Strength-based** — Lead with what's going well before addressing challenges
2. **Jargon-free** — Use plain language; avoid educational terminology without explanation
3. **Culturally responsive** — Respect diverse communication norms and preferences
4. **Bidirectional** — Always include mechanisms for families to respond and share
5. **Trauma-informed** — Never disclose student regulation data publicly; respect family privacy

## Addressing Barriers to Engagement

### Common Barriers and Solutions

| Barrier | Root Cause | FG2G Solution |
|---|---|---|
| "I don't have time" | Work schedules, caregiving responsibilities | Vary event times; offer weekend and evening options; provide virtual alternatives |
| "I don't speak English" | Language barriers | Multilingual materials; interpreter services; bilingual staff; home language signage |
| "School wasn't good for me" | Negative school experiences | Build relationship outside school building (garden); family-first approach |
| "I don't know about gardening" | Perceived expertise gap | Everyone is a learner in the garden; value all knowledge; provide accessible entry points |
| "I can't get there" | Transportation challenges | Neighborhood garden events; virtual options; transportation assistance |
| "I don't feel welcome" | Cultural or social marginalization | Intentional inclusion; diverse representation; cultural garden integration |
| "I have younger children" | Childcare needs | Provide childcare at all events; design family-inclusive activities |

## Assessment of Family Engagement

### Engagement Metrics

| Metric | Data Source | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Event attendance | Sign-in sheets; portal registration | 60% of families attend at least 2 events per year |
| Portal usage | Platform analytics | 50% of families log in monthly |
| Volunteer participation | Volunteer tracking system | 30% of families contribute volunteer hours annually |
| Communication responsiveness | Response tracking | 70% of families respond to direct communications |
| Family satisfaction | Annual survey | 80% rate engagement experience as positive |
| Home garden participation | Self-report via portal | 40% of families maintain home garden connections |

### Qualitative Assessment

- Annual family focus groups (diverse representation required)
- Family story collection (with consent) for program documentation
- Educator reflection on family partnership quality
- Student reports on family involvement in garden/learning activities

## Family Advisory Council

### Purpose and Structure

The Family Advisory Council ensures authentic family voice in FG2G implementation:

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| Membership | 6-10 family members representing diverse backgrounds, grade bands, and experience levels |
| Selection | Open application with intentional recruitment for demographic representation |
| Meeting schedule | Quarterly meetings + annual retreat |
| Scope | Advise on garden design, family programming, communication, cultural responsiveness |
| Compensation | Childcare, food, and stipend provided for meetings; recognition at community events |
| Term | 2-year terms with staggered rotation |

### Council Responsibilities

- Review and provide feedback on family communication materials
- Advise on cultural responsiveness of garden design and activities
- Recommend family programming priorities
- Serve as liaisons between FG2G team and broader parent community
- Participate in annual program evaluation
