# Community Partnership Models

## Overview

FG2G implementation is strengthened — and in many cases made possible — by strategic partnerships with community organizations. These partnerships are not supplementary; they are integral to the curriculum's Reconnect phase and to the sustainability of garden-based programming. This document outlines partnership models, development processes, and management frameworks that support mutually beneficial relationships between FG2G schools and their communities.

> *"A garden never grows in isolation. It depends on the broader ecosystem — the pollinators, the water cycle, the soil microbiome. FG2G depends on its community ecosystem in the same way. Partnership is not an add-on; it is the root system."*

## Partnership Continuum

### Levels of Partnership Engagement

```
LEVEL 1: AWARENESS          LEVEL 2: COOPERATION        LEVEL 3: COORDINATION
──────────────────          ──────────────────          ────────────────────
Partners know about    →    Partners share              Partners align
each other's work           resources occasionally      activities and goals

LEVEL 4: COLLABORATION      LEVEL 5: INTEGRATION
──────────────────────      ────────────────────
Partners co-design     →    Partners share
and co-deliver              governance, resources,
programs                    and accountability
```

### Partnership Types by Function

| Partnership Type | Description | Example Partners | 5Rs Connection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Garden resource partners | Provide materials, expertise, or land for garden programs | Master gardener programs, nurseries, agricultural extensions | Root: Physical garden infrastructure |
| Mental health partners | Support trauma-informed practices and student wellness | Counseling centers, trauma specialists, social services | Regulate: Professional clinical support |
| Academic enrichment partners | Extend learning opportunities beyond the classroom | Universities, museums, science centers, libraries | Reflect: Deepened inquiry |
| Workforce development partners | Connect garden learning to career pathways | Agricultural businesses, environmental organizations, food industry | Restore/Reconnect: Real-world application |
| Community engagement partners | Facilitate student-community connections | Neighborhood associations, faith communities, civic organizations | Reconnect: Community impact |
| Funding and sustainability partners | Provide financial support and resource development | Foundations, corporate sponsors, government programs | All phases: Program sustainability |

## Partnership Development Process

### Phase 1: Community Mapping (Months 1-2)

Before seeking partners, conduct a thorough community asset mapping:

| Asset Category | Guiding Questions | Documentation Method |
|---|---|---|
| Organizational assets | What organizations serve youth, families, or food systems in our community? | Organization directory with contact information |
| Physical assets | What gardens, farms, parks, or green spaces exist near our school? | Map with locations and access notes |
| Human assets | What individuals have gardening, therapy, or education expertise? | Skills inventory database |
| Institutional assets | What universities, hospitals, or government agencies could support FG2G? | Institutional contact list |
| Cultural assets | What cultural traditions around gardening and food exist in our community? | Community interview summaries |
| Economic assets | What businesses relate to food, agriculture, or environmental services? | Business directory with partnership potential rating |

### Phase 2: Partner Identification and Outreach (Months 2-3)

**Priority Partner Matrix:**

| Partner Characteristic | High Priority | Medium Priority | Lower Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mission alignment | Directly aligned with trauma-informed or garden-based education | Generally aligned with youth development | Tangentially related to education |
| Resource contribution | Can provide sustained, significant resources | Can provide periodic resources | Can provide one-time resources |
| Geographic proximity | Within walking distance of school | Within the district | Within the region |
| Track record | Established, proven community presence | Growing organization with potential | New or untested in community |
| Reciprocal benefit | Clear mutual benefit | Benefit primarily to school | Benefit primarily to partner |

### Phase 3: Relationship Building (Months 3-6)

Effective partnerships begin with relationship, not transactions:

1. **Introductory meeting** — Share FG2G vision; learn about partner's mission and needs
2. **Site visits** — Partner visits school garden; school team visits partner's facility
3. **Pilot activity** — Small, low-stakes collaborative activity to build trust
4. **Debrief and reflect** — Honest conversation about what worked and what to improve
5. **Formal agreement** — Develop MOU only after relationship foundation is established

### Phase 4: Formalization (Month 6+)

**Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) Components:**

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Shared goals and alignment with FG2G mission |
| Roles and responsibilities | Specific commitments from each partner |
| Resources | What each partner contributes (time, materials, expertise, space, funding) |
| Timeline | Duration of agreement, key milestones, renewal process |
| Communication | Designated contacts, meeting frequency, reporting expectations |
| Data sharing | What information will be shared; privacy protections |
| Evaluation | How partnership effectiveness will be measured |
| Modification | Process for adjusting the agreement as needs change |
| Termination | Respectful exit process if partnership ends |

## Model Partnership Structures

### Model 1: Master Gardener Partnership

**Partners:** County agricultural extension / Master gardener program

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| Partner commitment | 1 master gardener per class, 2 hours/week during growing season |
| School commitment | Garden space, integrated lesson plans, scheduling coordination |
| Student impact | Expert horticultural guidance; intergenerational mentorship |
| Partner benefit | Volunteer hours toward certification; meaningful community engagement |
| 5Rs alignment | Root (trusted adult), Reflect (expert knowledge), Reconnect (community bond) |

### Model 2: University Research Partnership

**Partners:** University education, psychology, or agriculture department

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| Partner commitment | Faculty advisor, 2-3 graduate assistants, research design support |
| School commitment | Research participation consent, data access, classroom access |
| Student impact | Enhanced assessment; evidence-based improvements |
| Partner benefit | Research publications; graduate field placements |
| 5Rs alignment | Reflect (research methodology), Restore (data-driven improvement) |

### Model 3: Community Food System Partnership

**Partners:** Food banks, community kitchens, farmers markets

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| Partner commitment | Accept and distribute produce; provide nutrition education resources |
| School commitment | Grow designated crops; coordinate harvest and delivery |
| Student impact | Real-world application; food systems understanding; community service |
| Partner benefit | Fresh, local produce; community engagement stories |
| 5Rs alignment | Reconnect (community impact), Reflect (food systems analysis) |

### Model 4: Mental Health Agency Partnership

**Partners:** Community mental health centers, trauma-informed care organizations

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| Partner commitment | Monthly consultation; annual training; crisis referral pathway |
| School commitment | Implement trauma-informed protocols; refer as appropriate |
| Student impact | Enhanced trauma-informed support; clinical access when needed |
| Partner benefit | Prevention-focused programming reduces crisis referrals |
| 5Rs alignment | Root (safety expertise), Regulate (clinical co-regulation guidance) |

### Model 5: Business Mentorship Partnership

**Partners:** Local businesses in agriculture, food service, environmental services

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| Partner commitment | Monthly mentorship; workplace tours; internship opportunities |
| School commitment | Prepare students for professional interactions; align enterprise projects |
| Student impact | Career exposure; professional skills; real-world business knowledge |
| Partner benefit | Talent pipeline; community visibility; CSR outcomes |
| 5Rs alignment | Reconnect (career connection), Reflect (professional inquiry) |

## Partnership Management

### Communication Cadence

| Stakeholder | Communication Type | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Primary partner contacts | Check-in call or meeting | Monthly |
| All partners | Newsletter or update | Quarterly |
| Partner advisory group | Advisory meeting | Twice yearly |
| Community-wide | Annual report and celebration event | Annually |

### Partnership Health Assessment

Evaluate each partnership annually:

| Indicator | Healthy | Needs Attention | At Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Communication | Regular, open, responsive | Occasional gaps | Infrequent or one-directional |
| Mutual benefit | Both partners clearly benefiting | One partner benefiting more | One partner not benefiting |
| Reliability | Commitments consistently met | Occasional missed commitments | Frequent missed commitments |
| Alignment | Shared goals actively pursued | Goals aligned but not coordinated | Goals diverging |
| Growth | Partnership deepening over time | Stable but not growing | Declining |
| Student impact | Clear evidence of positive impact | Some evidence | No evidence |

## Sustainability Planning

| Strategy | Description |
|---|---|
| Diversify partnerships | Maintain relationships across all types to avoid over-dependence |
| Build personal relationships | Ensure multiple contacts at each partner organization |
| Demonstrate impact | Regularly share evidence of positive outcomes with partners |
| Celebrate publicly | Recognize partner contributions in visible, meaningful ways |
| Plan for transitions | Document processes to survive staff turnover |
| Cultivate reciprocity | Continuously ask "What can we offer our partners?" |
