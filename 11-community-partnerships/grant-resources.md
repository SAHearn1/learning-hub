# Grant Resources and Funding Guide

## Overview

Sustainable FG2G implementation requires diverse funding streams that support garden infrastructure, professional development, technology, and community programming. This guide identifies funding categories, provides grant-writing guidance specific to FG2G programs, and outlines a sustainability strategy that moves beyond year-to-year grant dependence toward long-term financial health.

> *"A garden that depends on a single water source is vulnerable to drought. FG2G programs need diversified funding — multiple streams that ensure growth continues even when one source dries up."*

## Grant Writing Guide for FG2G

### Framing Your FG2G Proposal

The key to successful FG2G grant writing is matching the program's multi-dimensional impact to each funder's specific priorities:

| If the Funder Prioritizes | Emphasize This Aspect of FG2G |
|---|---|
| Academic achievement | Dual-purpose pedagogy; standards alignment; TRACE protocol; academic outcome data |
| Mental health / trauma | 5Rs framework; regulation data; ACEs research foundation; therapeutic horticulture evidence |
| Environmental education | Garden-based learning; ecological literacy; stewardship; sustainability |
| Equity and inclusion | Trauma-informed approach; culturally responsive garden design; family engagement; access |
| Innovation | AI-powered tutoring; dysregulation detection; dual-purpose design; technology integration |
| Community impact | Partnership model; family engagement; community food production; Reconnect phase |
| Workforce development | Agricultural career connections; entrepreneurship; professional skills; enterprise projects |

### Proposal Components

#### Needs Statement

A strong FG2G needs statement includes:

1. **Local ACEs data** — Prevalence of adverse childhood experiences in your community
2. **School-specific indicators** — Discipline rates, attendance, academic performance, SEL gaps
3. **Resource gaps** — Limited access to mental health services, green space, or experiential learning
4. **Community context** — Food insecurity, environmental health, economic challenges
5. **Evidence of demand** — Educator interest, family support, administrator commitment

#### Program Description

Structure your program description around the 5Rs:

| Section | Content to Include |
|---|---|
| Theoretical foundation | ACEs research, polyvagal theory, therapeutic horticulture, dual-purpose pedagogy |
| Program model | 5Rs framework with specific activities and implementation plan |
| Target population | Grade bands, number of students, demographics, need indicators |
| Implementation timeline | Phased rollout with specific milestones |
| Staffing | FG2G coordinator, trained educators, garden staff, community partners |
| Technology | RootGuide platform, AI tools, data collection systems |
| Evaluation plan | Regulation data, academic outcomes, SEL measures, fidelity indicators |

#### Evaluation Plan

Funders want to know how you'll measure impact:

| Outcome Domain | Measure | Data Source | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student regulation | Average regulation scores; trend improvement | Regulation Tracker | Monthly |
| Academic achievement | Standards mastery; grade-level performance | Assessment engine; report cards | Quarterly |
| SEL competency | CASEL competency ratings | SEL measures; educator observation | Semester |
| Behavioral outcomes | Discipline referrals; attendance; suspension rates | School information system | Semester |
| Garden outcomes | Produce harvested; garden usage frequency | Garden Manager tool | Seasonal |
| Educator development | PD completion; credentialing advancement; self-efficacy | PD Tracker; surveys | Annual |
| Family engagement | Event attendance; portal usage; satisfaction | Engagement metrics; surveys | Annual |
| Program fidelity | Implementation checklist scores | Fidelity tool | Monthly |

## Sustainability Strategy

### The Sustainability Spectrum

```
YEAR 1-2: STARTUP                YEAR 3-4: GROWTH              YEAR 5+: SUSTAINABILITY
──────────────────               ────────────────              ─────────────────────
Grant-dependent              →   Mixed funding              →  Institutionalized
External funding primary         Grant + district budget        District budget primary
Building evidence                Demonstrating impact           Sustaining and expanding
Pilot scale                      Expanding reach                Full implementation
```

### Long-Term Sustainability Strategies

| Strategy | Description | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Budget integration | Work with district to embed FG2G costs into operational budgets | Begin Year 2; achieve by Year 4 |
| Demonstrate ROI | Document cost savings from reduced discipline, improved attendance, decreased referrals | Collect data Year 1; present analysis Year 2-3 |
| Build political will | Engage school board, superintendent, and community leaders as champions | Ongoing from Year 1 |
| Diversify funding | Maintain 3+ funding sources at all times | Ongoing |
| Revenue generation | Develop fee-for-service PD offerings and garden product sales | Begin Year 3 |
| Community ownership | Build deep community investment so garden programs have broad support base | Ongoing from Year 1 |
| Policy advocacy | Advocate for state-level support for garden-based and trauma-informed education | Begin Year 2 |
