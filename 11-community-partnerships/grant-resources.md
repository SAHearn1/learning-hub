# Grant Resources

## Overview

Securing sustainable funding is essential for establishing and maintaining a robust garden-based, trauma-informed curriculum. The RootWork Learning Hub integrates multiple funding streams—federal grants, state programs, foundation support, corporate partnerships, and community fundraising—to build a financial foundation that supports the garden infrastructure, professional development, technology integration, and community partnership activities described throughout this curriculum.

This guide provides educators, administrators, and community partners with practical resources for identifying, applying for, and managing grant funding for garden-based education programs.

## Corporate Partnerships

### Developing Corporate Partnerships

Corporate partnerships can provide funding, in-kind support, volunteer labor, and expertise. Successful partnerships align corporate social responsibility goals with garden program needs.

**Partnership Development Process:**

| Step | Action | Timeline |
|------|--------|----------|
| 1. Research | Identify local and national companies with education, food, sustainability, or community CSR goals | 1-2 months |
| 2. Cultivate | Build relationships through networking, community events, and introductions | 2-6 months |
| 3. Propose | Present a partnership proposal with clear mutual benefits, deliverables, and recognition | 1 month |
| 4. Formalize | Execute a partnership agreement with scope, timeline, deliverables, and evaluation | 1 month |
| 5. Implement | Deliver on commitments; provide regular updates; recognize partner publicly | Ongoing |
| 6. Evaluate | Assess partnership outcomes; share impact data; plan renewal or expansion | Annually |

**Potential Corporate Partners by Sector:**

| Sector | Companies | Partnership Type |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| Home Improvement | Home Depot, Lowe's | Materials donation; volunteer labor for garden construction; grant programs |
| Food/Agriculture | Local farms, Whole Foods, Sprouts | Produce donation; market partnerships; educational visits; funding |
| Technology | Google, Microsoft, local tech companies | Technology equipment; coding/data science mentorship; funding |
| Healthcare | Local hospitals, health systems | Wellness program partnership; mental health resources; funding |
| Financial Services | Local banks, credit unions | Financial literacy programming; enterprise mentorship; funding |
| Nurseries/Garden Centers | Local nurseries | Plant and supply donation; expert volunteers; educational workshops |

## Grant Writing Guide

### Pre-Application Preparation

Before writing any grant application, complete this preparation:

1. **Needs Assessment:** Document the specific needs your garden program addresses with data (student demographics, academic outcomes, food access statistics, mental health data, community input)
2. **Logic Model:** Develop a clear logic model connecting inputs, activities, outputs, and outcomes
3. **Budget Development:** Create a detailed, realistic budget with justification for each line item
4. **Partnerships Documentation:** Secure letters of support from community partners, families, administrators, and district leadership
5. **Evidence Base:** Compile research evidence supporting garden-based education, trauma-informed practice, and SEL integration
6. **Capacity Statement:** Document your organization's capacity to implement and sustain the program (staff qualifications, track record, infrastructure)

### Logic Model Template

| Component | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Inputs** | Resources invested | Funding, staff, garden space, curriculum materials, technology, community partnerships |
| **Activities** | What the program does | Garden-based instruction, TRACE protocol, SEL integration, professional development, family engagement |
| **Outputs** | Direct products of activities | Number of students served, garden beds established, lessons delivered, families engaged, PD hours provided |
| **Short-term Outcomes** | Changes in knowledge and skills (1 year) | Improved academic performance, increased science engagement, enhanced regulation skills, stronger school-family connection |
| **Medium-term Outcomes** | Changes in behavior and practice (2-3 years) | Sustained academic growth, reduced disciplinary incidents, improved school climate, increased family engagement |
| **Long-term Outcomes** | Systemic impact (3-5 years) | Narrowed achievement gaps, improved community food access, trauma-informed school culture, replicable model for other schools |

### Grant Writing Best Practices

| Element | Best Practice | Common Mistake |
|---------|--------------|----------------|
| **Need Statement** | Use local data; tell a compelling story; connect to funder's priorities | Using only national statistics; being too general; deficit-only language |
| **Goals and Objectives** | SMART objectives (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) | Vague goals; confusing outputs with outcomes; unrealistic timelines |
| **Methods** | Clearly describe what you will do, who will do it, and when | Too vague; not connecting activities to outcomes; ignoring implementation challenges |
| **Evaluation Plan** | Mix of quantitative and qualitative measures; include process and outcome evaluation | No evaluation plan; only quantitative; no baseline data collection plan |
| **Budget** | Detailed, justified, realistic; include in-kind contributions | Vague budget categories; over-requesting personnel; no budget narrative |
| **Sustainability Plan** | Describe how the program will continue after grant funding ends | No sustainability plan; unrealistic assumptions about future funding |

### Sample Budget Categories

| Category | Line Items | Justification |
|----------|-----------|---------------|
| Personnel | Program coordinator (0.5 FTE); garden educator (1.0 FTE); evaluation consultant | Staff needed for implementation, instruction, and assessment |
| Fringe Benefits | FICA, health insurance, retirement | Standard benefit rate per organizational policy |
| Garden Infrastructure | Raised beds, soil, irrigation system, greenhouse materials, tools | Initial garden establishment (one-time costs) |
| Supplies | Seeds, amendments, containers, art supplies, science equipment | Recurring annual supply costs |
| Curriculum Materials | Textbooks, journals, assessment tools, TRACE protocol materials | Instructional materials for all grade bands |
| Technology | Tablets, sensors, weather station, RootGuide AI access licenses | Technology for data collection, research, and AI tutoring |
| Professional Development | Workshop facilitation, conference attendance, coaching | Educator training for garden-based, trauma-informed instruction |
| Family Engagement | Event food, translation services, transportation, childcare | Removing barriers to family participation |
| Travel | Site visits, conference attendance, community partner meetings | Professional learning and partnership development |
| Indirect Costs | Administrative support, facilities, utilities | Per negotiated indirect cost rate |

## Grant Management

### Post-Award Requirements

| Task | Frequency | Responsible Party |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| Financial tracking | Ongoing | Business office with program coordinator oversight |
| Progress reporting | Quarterly or as specified | Program coordinator with educator input |
| Data collection | Ongoing per evaluation plan | Evaluation consultant with educator support |
| Budget modifications | As needed (with funder approval) | Program coordinator + business office |
| Funder communication | Regular updates; immediate for significant changes | Program coordinator |
| Final report | End of grant period | Program coordinator + evaluation consultant |
| Audit compliance | As required | Business office |

### Sustainability Planning

Grant funding is temporary. A sustainable garden program requires diversified funding:

| Revenue Source | Strategy | Timeline |
|---------------|----------|----------|
| District budget integration | Demonstrate outcomes; advocate for line-item funding; align with district priorities | Years 2-3 |
| Enterprise revenue | Garden enterprise generates self-sustaining revenue through produce sales, workshops, and value-added products | Years 1-3 (building) |
| Endowment development | Seek major gifts or planned giving for garden endowment fund | Years 3-5 |
| Annual fundraising | Annual garden gala, plant sale, or community event | Annual |
| Multiple grants | Diversify across federal, state, foundation, and corporate sources; stagger grant timelines | Ongoing |
| In-kind support | Maintain corporate partnerships for materials, labor, and expertise | Ongoing |
| Community contributions | Volunteer hours; donated materials; expertise sharing | Ongoing |
