# K-2 Root Phase: Grounding and Safety

## Overview

The Root phase for K-2 learners (ages 5-8) establishes felt safety and readiness before any academic content is introduced. At this developmental stage, children depend heavily on adults to help them assess safety and transition between activities. The Root phase uses predictable rituals, picture-based check-ins, sensory grounding, and garden entry routines to create the neurological conditions for learning.

Young children communicate their regulation state primarily through behavior and body language rather than verbal self-report. Educators must therefore rely on careful observation, warm invitation, and concrete tools such as picture cards to gauge each child's readiness.

> **Garden Metaphor**: Before a seed can sprout, the soil must be prepared -- softened, cleared of rocks, and watered. The Root phase prepares the child's internal "soil" so learning can take hold.

## Garden Entry Ritual

Every session begins with a consistent, multisensory garden entry ritual. Predictability is essential for children who may lack consistent routines outside school.

### Ritual Sequence (5-8 minutes)

1. **Gather at the Garden Gate** -- Children line up at the designated entry point (physical garden gate, classroom door, or taped line on the floor). The educator greets each child by name with a warm, calm voice.
2. **Transition Song** -- The group sings a short entry song together (see below). Singing activates the vagus nerve and supports co-regulation.
3. **Grounding Touch** -- Each child touches something from the garden: a handful of soil, a smooth stone, a leaf, or a sprig of rosemary. The educator narrates: "Feel the soil in your hands. Notice if it is cool or warm."
4. **Picture Card Check-In** -- Children select a card that matches how they feel (see detailed guidance below).
5. **Ready Signal** -- Children show a physical "ready signal" (hands on knees, thumbs up, or feet planted on the ground) to indicate they are prepared to begin.

### Sample Transition Songs

**"Roots Go Down" (sung to the tune of "London Bridge")**
> Roots go down into the ground, into the ground, into the ground.
> Roots go down into the ground -- we are ready.

**"Hello Garden" (call and response)**
> Educator: "Hello garden, here we are!"
> Children: "Here we are, here we are!"
> Educator: "Ready to grow and learn today!"
> Children: "Learn today, learn today!"

## Picture Card Check-In

Picture cards are the primary self-report tool for K-2 learners. Each child selects one card that represents their current feeling state. Cards use simple images rather than words, making them accessible to pre-readers and English language learners.

### Recommended Card Sets

| Card Image | Feeling State | Regulation Implication |
|------------|--------------|----------------------|
| Bright sunshine | Happy, energized, ready | Optimal -- proceed to learning |
| Partly cloudy | Okay, a little unsure | Adequate -- brief grounding, then proceed |
| Rainy cloud | Sad, tired, low energy | Borderline -- extended grounding or gentle check-in |
| Lightning storm | Angry, frustrated, upset | Dysregulated -- transition to Regulate phase |
| Foggy/misty | Confused, "don't know" | May indicate hypoarousal -- observe and gently engage |

### Educator Facilitation Language

- "Pick the weather card that matches how your inside feels right now."
- "There's no wrong answer. Every kind of weather is okay."
- "Thank you for showing me. I see you picked the rainy cloud. I'm glad you told me."
- "You can change your card any time today if your weather changes."

**Important**: Never require children to explain their card choice publicly. A child who selects the storm card should receive a quiet, private check-in rather than a group discussion.

## Sensory Grounding Activities

When the picture card check-in reveals that several children are outside their window of tolerance, or when the group is transitioning from a high-energy activity (recess, lunch, arrival), use one or more of these sensory grounding activities.

### Physical Grounding (Body-Based)

| Activity | Duration | Description |
|----------|----------|-------------|
| Tree pose | 1-2 min | Stand tall, press feet into the ground, arms reach up like branches. "Feel your roots growing into the floor." |
| Seed-to-sprout | 2 min | Curl into a tiny ball (seed), slowly grow taller as the educator counts to ten, stretch arms out (leaves open). |
| Garden stomps | 1 min | Stomp feet firmly on the ground ten times. "We are pressing our roots into the earth." |
| Wiggle and freeze | 1-2 min | Shake bodies loosely, then freeze when the educator says "Roots!" Practice three times. |
| Hand press | 30 sec | Press palms together firmly for five seconds, release. Repeat three times. "Feel the warm tingle in your hands." |

### Sensory Grounding (Five Senses)

**Adapted 5-4-3-2-1 for Young Learners ("Garden Senses")**

The educator leads the group through a simplified version:

- "Look around. Tell me TWO things you can see in the garden." (visual)
- "Close your eyes. Tell me ONE thing you can hear." (auditory)
- "Touch the soil (or stone, or leaf). How does it feel?" (tactile)
- "Smell this herb. What does it remind you of?" (olfactory)

This shortened version (2-1-1-1) is more accessible than the full 5-4-3-2-1 for children under age 8.

### Garden-Specific Grounding

- **Watering ritual**: Each child waters their personal plant or a designated section. The repetitive motion and sound of water are naturally calming.
- **Soil sifting**: Children run dry soil or sand through their fingers into a bin. Narrate the sensory experience: "Notice how the soil feels. Is it soft? Gritty? Cool?"
- **Herb sniffing station**: Set out small pots of mint, rosemary, lavender, and basil. Children choose one to smell and describe.

## Regulation Level Assessment for K-2

Educators assess regulation using observable indicators rather than asking children to self-rate on a numerical scale. The following observation guide supports the assessment.

| Regulation Level | Observable Indicators | Educator Response |
|-----------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Optimal (Green) | Eye contact, calm body, following directions, smiling or neutral expression | Proceed to Reflect phase |
| Adequate (Yellow-Green) | Mild fidgeting, slightly distracted, but responsive when redirected | Brief grounding activity, then proceed |
| Borderline (Yellow) | Difficulty sitting still, looking away, not responding to first prompt, flat affect | Extended grounding; one-on-one check-in |
| Dysregulated (Orange) | Crying, refusing to participate, aggressive body language, hiding | Transition to Regulate phase with warmth |
| Crisis (Red) | Inconsolable distress, running away, harming self or others, complete shutdown | Follow school safety protocol; calm adult presence |

### Documentation

Record each child's check-in card selection and observed regulation level in a simple daily tracking sheet. Over time, patterns emerge (e.g., "Jaylen consistently arrives dysregulated on Mondays") that inform proactive planning.

## Educator Role and Language Models

During the Root phase, the educator serves as a **non-anxious, warm presence**. Voice should be calm, low, and steady. Body language should be open and relaxed, at the child's eye level whenever possible.

### Language to Use

- "Good morning, Amara. I'm happy to see you today."
- "Let's get our bodies ready to learn. Feet on the floor, hands in your lap."
- "Take a deep breath with me. Smell the flower -- blow out the candle." (Inhale through nose, exhale through mouth.)
- "Your job right now is just to be here. That's enough."
- "I notice your body has a lot of energy today. Let's do some stomps together."

### Language to Avoid

- "Why are you upset?" (demands explanation the child may not be able to give)
- "Calm down." (dismissive and ineffective)
- "Everyone else is ready. Why aren't you?" (shaming comparison)
- "What happened at home?" (invasive, potentially re-traumatizing)
- "You need to sit still right now." (punitive tone)

## Assessment Indicators

Educators observe the following indicators to determine whether the Root phase is effective for each child over time.

| Indicator | Beginning | Developing | Secure |
|-----------|-----------|------------|--------|
| Participates in entry ritual | Needs individual prompting each session | Joins with group after reminder | Independently follows routine |
| Selects picture card | Refuses or selects randomly | Selects with adult support; choice roughly matches observed state | Independently selects card matching internal state |
| Engages in grounding activity | Resistant or distracted | Participates with prompting | Engages willingly; body visibly calms |
| Shows ready signal | Does not show readiness | Shows signal with delay or after peers | Shows signal promptly and independently |
| Transitions to learning | Requires extended time or moves to Regulate | Transitions with brief additional support | Transitions smoothly within 5-8 minutes |

## Transition to Next Phase

After the Root ritual is complete, the educator uses a brief transition cue:

- **To Reflect** (regulation level green/yellow-green): "Our roots are planted. Let's grow some learning!"
- **To Regulate** (regulation level orange/red): "I can see you might need a little more time to get settled. Let's visit our calm corner together." (Said privately, warmly, without drawing group attention.)

The transition is always communicated with warmth, never as a consequence. Every child's pace is respected.
