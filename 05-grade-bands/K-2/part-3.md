# K-2 Reflect Phase: Guided Discovery and Early Thinking

## Overview

The Reflect phase is where core academic learning takes place. For K-2 learners, this phase emphasizes concrete, hands-on discovery through the garden environment. Young children learn through direct sensory experience -- touching seeds, counting petals, measuring stems, observing insects, and describing what they see. Abstract reasoning is emerging but not yet dominant, so all learning is anchored in manipulatives, visuals, and physical activity.

The TRACE protocol (Think, Reason, Articulate, Check, Extend) is simplified for this age group into shorter, educator-guided sequences using accessible language and garden-based materials. Academic content in mathematics, science, and language arts is woven naturally into garden activities rather than presented as separate subjects.

> **Garden Metaphor**: The Reflect phase is the growing season -- the time when the seed, now rooted in safe soil, pushes its first leaves toward the light. The educator provides the sunlight and water (scaffolding and support) while the child does the growing.

---

## Purpose

1. **Engage in standards-aligned academic content** -- Mathematics, science, and language arts instruction connected to Georgia Standards of Excellence, Common Core, and NGSS at the K-2 level.
2. **Develop early metacognitive awareness** -- Help children begin to notice and name their own thinking through guided questioning.
3. **Build foundational reasoning skills** -- Use the simplified TRACE protocol to develop observation, description, and early logical thinking.
4. **Maintain emotional safety during learning** -- Ensure that academic challenge never exceeds the child's regulation capacity.

---

## Simplified TRACE Protocol for K-2

The full TRACE protocol is adapted for young learners with simpler language, shorter durations, and more educator guidance at each step.

| TRACE Step | Full Framework | K-2 Adaptation | Educator Prompt |
|------------|---------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Think** | "What do you notice first?" | "Look and wonder" | "What do you see? What do you notice?" |
| **Reason** | "What's your plan of attack?" | "What could we try?" | "What do you think we should do? Why?" |
| **Articulate** | "Talk me through your thinking." | "Tell me about it" | "Tell me what you did. How did you figure that out?" |
| **Check** | "How do you know you're right?" | "Let's make sure" | "Let's check. Can we count again? Does that match?" |
| **Extend** | "What else could you do with this?" | "What else?" | "What else could we try? Where else do you see this?" |

### Sample TRACE Sequence: Counting Seeds

1. **Think**: The educator places a handful of sunflower seeds on a tray. "Look at these seeds. What do you notice about them?"
   - Children observe: "There are a lot." "They're stripy." "Some are bigger."
2. **Reason**: "How could we figure out how many seeds there are?"
   - Children suggest: "Count them!" "Put them in groups."
3. **Articulate**: Children count the seeds (with support as needed). "Tell me how you counted. What did you do?"
   - Child: "I put them in piles of two and then I counted the piles."
4. **Check**: "Let's make sure. Can you count them a different way to check?"
   - Child counts one-by-one and compares to the grouped count.
5. **Extend**: "If we planted all these seeds, how many sunflowers could we grow? What if we shared half with another class -- how many would each class get?"

**Sample Lesson: Measuring Our Sunflowers**

- Each child measures their sunflower using linking cubes, placing cubes end-to-end along the stem.
- They record the number of cubes on a simple bar graph posted on the wall.
- Educator prompts: "How tall is your sunflower? How many cubes? Is yours taller or shorter than Maya's? How many cubes different?"
- Over weeks, children track growth and compare: "Last week it was 8 cubes. This week it is 12 cubes. How much did it grow?"

**Sample Lesson: What Do Seeds Need?**

- Children plant the same type of seed in four cups: (1) soil + water + light, (2) soil + water + no light, (3) soil + no water + light, (4) no soil + water + light.
- Each day, children observe and draw what they see in their garden journal.
- Educator guides the TRACE protocol: "What do you notice about Cup 1 compared to Cup 2? What do you think happened? Why?"
- After two weeks, the class discusses: "What does a seed need to grow? How do you know?"

## Concrete Manipulatives and Learning Tools

K-2 learners need physical objects to support emerging abstract thinking. The following garden-connected manipulatives should be available during every Reflect session.

| Manipulative | Academic Use | Garden Connection |
|-------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Seeds (variety of sizes) | Counting, sorting, grouping, estimating | Directly from the garden seed supply |
| Linking cubes | Measuring plant height, building number representations | Measuring stems, spacing plants |
| Balance scale | Comparing weights of produce, soil amounts | Weighing harvested vegetables |
| Magnifying glasses | Observation, detail, scientific inquiry | Examining leaves, insects, soil |
| Measuring cups and spoons | Volume, capacity, fractions | Measuring water for plants, mixing soil |
| Garden journals (blank books) | Writing, drawing, recording data | Personal plant observation records |
| Picture word wall | Vocabulary, labeling, reading | Garden vocabulary posted at child height |

## Educator Role During the Reflect Phase

### Facilitation Over Instruction

The educator guides learning through questions rather than direct answers. This develops the child's own thinking rather than dependence on adult knowledge.

**Instead of**: "This is a root. Roots grow underground and soak up water."
**Use**: "What part of the plant is this? Where do you think it goes? What do you think it does?"

**Instead of**: "You have seven seeds. That's correct."
**Use**: "You counted seven. How did you count? Can you show me? Let's check by counting together."

**Instead of**: "Tomatoes are red when they are ripe."
**Use**: "Look at these two tomatoes. What is different about them? Why do you think that might be?"

### Monitoring Regulation During Learning

Even during engaged learning, the educator watches for signs that a child is leaving their window of tolerance:
- Sudden increase in errors after a period of success
- Putting head down or pushing materials away
- Negative self-talk: "I can't do this," "This is too hard," "I'm stupid"
- Fidgeting, getting up, bothering peers

If dysregulation is detected, transition to the Regulate phase with warmth: "I can see your brain is working really hard. Want to take a garden break? We can come back to this."

## Lesson Structure for K-2 Reflect Phase

A typical Reflect session follows this structure within the 15-25 minute timeframe.

| Segment | Duration | Activity |
|---------|----------|----------|
| Warm-Up | 2-3 min | Brief review of prior learning or quick retrieval activity ("Who remembers what we learned about seeds last time?") |
| Introduction | 3-5 min | Present the day's question or challenge using a garden object or picture book. Activate the THINK step. |
| Guided Exploration | 8-12 min | Hands-on activity using garden materials. Educator circulates, asks TRACE questions, scaffolds as needed. |
| Sharing | 3-5 min | Two or three children share what they discovered. Educator models the ARTICULATE step. |
| Transition | 1-2 min | Bridge to Restore (if errors arose) or Reconnect (if learning objective is met). |

## Assessment Indicators

| Indicator | Beginning | Developing | Secure |
|-----------|-----------|------------|--------|
| Engages in THINK step (observation) | Does not look at or engage with materials | Looks at materials when prompted; makes one observation | Independently observes and offers multiple noticings |
| Attempts REASON step (strategy) | Waits for adult to tell them what to do | Suggests a strategy with prompting ("What could you try?") | Proposes a strategy independently before starting |
| Demonstrates ARTICULATE step (explanation) | Cannot describe what they did | Describes actions with single words or short phrases | Explains thinking in complete sentences: "I counted by twos because..." |
| Participates in CHECK step (verification) | Does not check work | Checks when asked by educator | Begins to self-check ("Wait, let me count again") |
| Explores EXTEND step (transfer) | Does not connect to other contexts | Responds to educator extension prompts | Independently asks "What if...?" or makes connections to prior learning |
| Maintains regulation during academic work | Frequently needs Regulate phase breaks | Occasional dysregulation; recovers with Tier 1 support | Sustains engagement for 15-25 minutes with minimal support |

## Transition to Next Phase

- **To Restore**: When a child makes an error that presents a learning opportunity. The educator transitions with warmth: "Something really interesting happened here. Let's look at it together -- your brain is about to learn something new!"
- **To Regulate**: When dysregulation signals are observed. Transition without shame: "Let's take a garden break. Your brain has been working hard."
- **To Reconnect**: When the learning objective has been met. "You figured it out! Let's share what you learned and see how it connects to our garden."
