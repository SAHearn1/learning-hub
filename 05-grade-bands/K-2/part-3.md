# K-2 Grade Band: Science & Literacy Integration

## Science Curriculum Through the Garden

### Overview

Science in the K-2 RootWork curriculum is taught through direct engagement with living systems. The garden is not a supplement to science instruction -- it is the science classroom. Students develop the practices of observation, questioning, investigation, and explanation through sustained interaction with plants, soil, water, weather, and the organisms that share the garden ecosystem.

At the K-2 level, the emphasis is on observation-based science that builds a foundation for later experimental design. Students learn to notice details, record what they see, ask questions about the natural world, and begin to develop explanations based on evidence rather than assumption. These practices align directly with the NGSS Science and Engineering Practices and the Georgia Standards of Excellence for science.

### Unit 1: Seeds and Plant Life Cycles (Weeks 1-8)

#### Essential Questions
- What does a seed need to grow?
- How does a plant change over time?
- What are the parts of a plant and what do they do?

#### Key Vocabulary
Root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit, seed, germinate, sprout, seedling, soil, sunlight, water, nutrients, life cycle, observe, predict, record

#### Lesson Sequence

**Week 1-2: What Is a Seed?**

*Root Phase:* Students receive mystery seeds in small envelopes. Sensory exploration -- feel the texture, observe the size and color, smell (if safe). "If you were a seed, what kind of plant would you want to become?"

*Reflect Phase Activities:*
1. **Seed Dissection** (large seeds like lima beans, soaked overnight):
   - Students carefully open seeds to find the embryo plant inside
   - Identify the seed coat (protection), cotyledon (food storage), and embryo (baby plant)
   - Draw and label in science journals
   - "The seed carries everything the baby plant needs to start growing -- food and protection, just like how we need to feel safe and nourished to learn"

2. **Seed Comparison:**
   - Sort a collection of seeds by observable properties: size, shape, color, texture
   - Create a class chart: "Seeds come in many different shapes and sizes, just like people"
   - Predict: "Do you think the biggest seed will make the biggest plant?"

3. **Planting Investigation:**
   - Each student plants 3 bean seeds in clear cups against the side so roots and stems are visible
   - Set up the "What does a seed need?" experiment:
     - Cup A: Soil + water + sunlight (control)
     - Cup B: Soil + water + no sunlight (dark cupboard)
     - Cup C: Soil + sunlight + no water
     - Cup D: No soil + water + sunlight (wet paper towel)
   - Students predict which will grow best and record predictions in journals

*Reconnect Phase:* "Seeds travel in amazing ways -- on the wind, in animal fur, through water. Look for seeds on your way home today!"

**Week 3-4: Germination and Growth**

*Reflect Phase Activities:*
1. **Daily Observations:**
   - Students check their seed cups each day and record observations
   - K: Draw what they see; teacher scribes descriptions
   - 1st: Draw and write one sentence: "Today I see ___"
   - 2nd: Record date, drawing, measurement, and written observation

2. **Time-Lapse Documentation:**
   - Photograph each cup weekly (or use a classroom time-lapse camera)
   - Arrange photos in sequence to show change over time
   - "What happened first? What happened next? What happened last?"

3. **Experiment Results Discussion:**
   - Revisit the "What does a seed need?" cups after 2 weeks
   - Compare results to predictions: "Were you right? Were you surprised?"
   - TRACE protocol: "What do you notice about the cups? (Think) Why do you think Cup B looks different from Cup A? (Reason) Tell your partner what you think seeds need. (Articulate) How can we check if we're right? (Check) What would happen if we changed something else? (Extend)"
   - Class conclusion: "Seeds need water, sunlight, and soil to grow their best"

**Week 5-6: Plant Parts and Functions**

*Reflect Phase Activities:*
1. **Plant Part Scavenger Hunt:**
   - In the garden, find examples of roots, stems, leaves, and flowers
   - Collect samples (where appropriate) and create a labeled display
   - Song: "Roots, stems, leaves, and flowers, leaves and flowers! Roots, stems, leaves, and flowers -- that's what plants are made of!"

2. **Function Investigation:**
   - **Roots:** Uproot a weed gently. Observe the root system. "What do you think roots do?" Place a celery stalk in colored water to demonstrate water uptake.
   - **Stems:** Compare a thin flower stem to a thick sunflower stalk. "The stem is like a highway for water and food."
   - **Leaves:** Cover one leaf with foil for a week. Compare to uncovered leaves. "Leaves need sunlight to make food for the plant."
   - **Flowers:** Observe a flower with a magnifying glass. Find the pollen. "Flowers help make seeds for new plants."

3. **Edible Plant Parts:**
   - Classify garden vegetables by which part we eat:
     - Roots: carrots, radishes, beets
     - Stems: celery, asparagus
     - Leaves: lettuce, spinach, kale
     - Flowers: broccoli, cauliflower
     - Fruits: tomatoes, peppers, squash
     - Seeds: corn, peas, sunflower seeds
   - Taste test from the garden (where safe and available)

**Week 7-8: Complete Life Cycle**

*Reflect Phase Activities:*
1. **Life Cycle Sequencing:**
   - Using photos from the class time-lapse or seed cup observations
   - Sequence cards: seed → sprout → seedling → mature plant → flower → fruit/seed → seed
   - "It's a circle! The plant makes seeds that grow into new plants!"

2. **Life Cycle Comparison:**
   - Compare plant life cycles to animal life cycles (butterfly, frog)
   - Identify similarities: birth/germination → growth → reproduction → death
   - "All living things have life cycles. Even us!"

3. **Seed Saving:**
   - Collect seeds from mature garden plants
   - Dry and package seeds for next year's garden
   - "We're helping the life cycle continue!"

### Unit 2: Soil and Earth Materials (Weeks 9-14)

#### Essential Questions
- What is soil made of?
- Why do different soils look and feel different?
- How do living things change the soil?

#### Key Vocabulary
Soil, sand, clay, silt, humus, compost, decompose, organism, earthworm, nutrient, texture, moisture

#### Core Lessons

**Lesson: Soil Exploration**

*Root Phase:* Each student receives a cup of garden soil. "Close your eyes. Feel the soil. What does it feel like? Smell it. What does it smell like? Now open your eyes. What do you see?"

*Reflect Phase Activities:*
1. **Soil Layers:**
   - Shake a jar of soil with water. Let it settle overnight.
   - Observe layers: sand (bottom), silt (middle), clay (top), organic matter (floating)
   - "Soil is a mixture of many things!"

2. **Soil Comparison:**
   - Collect soil samples from different locations: garden, playground, forest, sandbox
   - Compare color, texture, moisture, and smell
   - Which holds water best? Pour water through each in a funnel with filter paper

3. **Living Soil:**
   - Set up a worm observation bin
   - Observe earthworms processing soil: "Worms eat dead leaves and turn them into rich soil. They're nature's recyclers!"
   - Count and identify organisms in a soil sample using magnifying glasses

**Lesson: Composting**

*Reflect Phase Activities:*
1. **What Decomposes?**
   - Bury items in a compost investigation: banana peel, apple core, paper, plastic bag, leaf, bread crust
   - Predict: "Which will break down? Which won't?"
   - Check every two weeks and record observations
   - Conclusion: "Natural things decompose. Plastic doesn't. That's why composting is important!"

2. **Build a Class Compost:**
   - Layer greens (food scraps, grass clippings) and browns (dry leaves, cardboard)
   - Assign composting jobs: adding scraps, turning, watering, temperature checking
   - "We're feeding the soil so it can feed our plants!"

### Unit 3: Weather, Seasons & the Garden (Weeks 15-22)

#### Essential Questions
- How does weather affect our garden?
- What patterns do we see in weather over time?
- How do gardens change with the seasons?

#### Core Lessons

**Lesson: Garden Weather Station**

*Reflect Phase Activities:*
1. **Weather Instruments:**
   - Thermometer: Read daily temperature (2nd grade reads the number; K-1 identifies "hot," "warm," "cool," "cold")
   - Rain gauge: Measure rainfall in inches
   - Wind sock: Observe wind direction and strength
   - Record daily weather data on class chart

2. **Weather and Plant Connection:**
   - "Our tomatoes drooped after three hot days with no rain. What do you think happened?"
   - "The lettuce grew really well during the cool week. Why might that be?"
   - Correlate weather data with plant growth data from garden journals

3. **Seasonal Garden Calendar:**
   - Create a class calendar showing what to plant in each season
   - Fall: garlic, lettuce, peas (cool weather crops)
   - Spring: tomatoes, peppers, beans (warm weather crops)
   - "Why can't we plant tomatoes in winter?"
   - Connect to Earth's patterns: "The sun gives us more warmth in summer, and plants that like warmth grow best then"

### Unit 4: Animals in the Garden Ecosystem (Weeks 23-30)

#### Essential Questions
- What animals live in or visit our garden?
- How do animals help plants?
- How do plants help animals?

#### Core Lessons

**Lesson: Garden Biodiversity Survey**

*Reflect Phase Activities:*
1. **Insect Count:**
   - Sit quietly in the garden for 10 minutes. Record every animal observed.
   - Classify: insects, spiders, birds, worms, mammals
   - "Our garden is a community of living things!"

2. **Beneficial Insects:**
   - Observe pollinators (bees, butterflies) visiting flowers
   - "Without bees, we wouldn't have most of our fruits and vegetables!"
   - Build a simple bee hotel from bamboo tubes
   - Observe ladybugs eating aphids: "Ladybugs are garden helpers"

3. **Food Chains in the Garden:**
   - Build a simple food chain: sun → plant → caterpillar → bird
   - Act it out: students become links in the chain
   - "What would happen if we removed the plants?"
   - Connect to the importance of healthy gardens for the whole ecosystem

**Lesson: Plant and Animal Interdependence**

*Reflect Phase Activities:*
1. **Pollination Investigation:**
   - Use cotton swabs to "pollinate" flowers like a bee
   - Transfer pollen from one flower to another
   - "Bees don't know they're helping plants make seeds. They just want nectar! But they carry pollen on their fuzzy bodies."

2. **Seed Dispersal Methods:**
   - Observe seeds with hooks (burrs), wings (maple), parachutes (dandelion), and tasty fruit (berries)
   - Match the seed type to its dispersal method: wind, water, animal fur, animal eating
   - "Plants can't walk, so they have clever ways to send their seeds to new places!"

## Literacy Integration

### Reading in the Garden

#### Anchor Texts by Unit

| Unit | Read-Aloud Texts | Text Type |
|------|-----------------|-----------|
| Seeds & Life Cycles | *The Tiny Seed* (Eric Carle), *From Seed to Plant* (Gail Gibbons), *A Seed Is Sleepy* (Dianna Hutts Aston) | Narrative, Informational, Poetic |
| Soil & Earth | *Diary of a Worm* (Doreen Cronin), *Jump into Science: Dirt* (Steve Tomecek) | Narrative, Informational |
| Weather & Seasons | *The Reason for Seasons* (Gail Gibbons), *Planting a Rainbow* (Lois Ehlert) | Informational, Narrative |
| Animals & Ecosystem | *The Very Hungry Caterpillar* (Eric Carle), *Are You a Ladybug?* (Judy Allen) | Narrative, Informational |

#### Reading Strategies in Garden Context

**Before Reading:**
- Picture walk: "What do you think this book will be about?"
- Activate prior knowledge: "What do we already know about seeds from our garden?"
- Set a purpose: "Let's read to find out if seeds really need darkness to germinate"

**During Reading:**
- Stop and observe: Compare book illustrations to real garden specimens
- Vocabulary in context: "The book says 'germinate.' We saw that happen when our seeds sprouted!"
- Think-aloud: Teacher models comprehension strategies

**After Reading:**
- Retelling with garden props: Use real seeds, plants, or garden tools to retell the story
- Compare book to experience: "Was our experience with seeds the same as the book described?"
- Extend: "The book talked about seeds traveling on the wind. Can we test that in the garden?"

### Writing Through the Garden

#### Growth Journals

The growth journal is the central literacy artifact of the K-2 RootWork curriculum. Students write in their journals at least three times per week, documenting observations, reflections, and learning.

**Kindergarten Journal Expectations:**
- Draw a detailed picture of an observation
- Label with beginning sounds or known words (teacher assists with spelling)
- Dictate a sentence for the teacher to scribe
- By year's end: Write a simple sentence independently ("I see a red flower.")

**1st Grade Journal Expectations:**
- Draw and write 1-3 sentences about an observation
- Use vocabulary from the word wall
- Include a date and the question they investigated
- Begin to include measurements and data

**2nd Grade Journal Expectations:**
- Write 3-5 sentences describing observations, procedures, and conclusions
- Use transition words: first, then, next, finally
- Include labeled diagrams with measurements
- Write questions for further investigation
- Respond to prompts that connect garden learning to broader themes

#### Writing Genres Through Garden Contexts

| Genre | Garden Application | Example Prompt |
|-------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Narrative** | Garden stories | "Write about the day we found a caterpillar in our garden" |
| **Informational** | How-to guides, reports | "How to Plant a Seed" (procedural); "All About Earthworms" (report) |
| **Opinion** | Persuasive writing | "I think our garden should grow ___ because ___" |
| **Poetry** | Sensory poems | "Write a poem using words that describe how the garden looks, sounds, smells, and feels" |
| **Descriptive** | Observation logs | "Describe your plant using as many details as you can" |

#### Word Study and Vocabulary

**Garden Word Wall Categories:**
- Plant parts: root, stem, leaf, flower, seed, fruit, bark, branch, petal, pollen
- Garden actions: plant, water, dig, weed, harvest, prune, mulch, compost, observe, measure
- Science words: germinate, decompose, pollinate, nutrient, organism, habitat, predict, investigate
- Describing words: tiny, enormous, rough, smooth, fragrant, wilted, vibrant, lush, dormant
- Feeling words (connecting to SEL): calm, excited, curious, frustrated, proud, peaceful, energized, tired

**Phonics Connections:**
- Word families: seed/weed/need, grow/flow/show, plant/can't/ant, root/hoot/boot
- Compound words: sunflower, earthworm, raindrop, butterfly, greenhouse
- Blends and digraphs: stem (st-), sprout (spr-), branch (br-), earth (th), flower (fl-)

### Speaking and Listening

**Structured Talk Routines:**

1. **Garden Partner Share (daily):**
   - "Turn to your garden buddy and tell them what you noticed today"
   - Sentence stems provided: "I noticed ___." "I wonder ___." "I think ___ because ___."
   - Listening partner practices: eye contact, nodding, asking one question

2. **Garden Reports (weekly):**
   - Each student or pair shares one observation with the class
   - Practice speaking clearly, making eye contact, and using a "presenter voice"
   - Audience practices asking questions: "Can you tell me more about ___?"

3. **Science Discussions (during investigations):**
   - Teacher facilitates whole-group discussions using accountable talk:
     - "I agree with ___ because ___"
     - "I want to add to what ___ said..."
     - "I respectfully disagree because ___"
     - "Can you explain what you mean by ___?"

4. **Garden Vocabulary Practice:**
   - "Word of the week" displayed with picture, definition, and sentence
   - Students practice using the word in different contexts throughout the week
   - Garden charades: act out vocabulary words for classmates to guess

## Cross-Curricular Connections

### Social Studies Through the Garden

- **Community helpers**: Visit from a local farmer, master gardener, or nutritionist
- **Maps and geography**: Create a map of the school garden; identify where different foods are grown around the world
- **Economics**: Garden store dramatic play; understanding where food comes from and why it costs money
- **Cultural traditions**: Explore how different cultures use gardens (Three Sisters planting from Indigenous traditions, Japanese zen gardens, English cottage gardens)
- **Responsibility and citizenship**: Caring for shared garden spaces; understanding our responsibility to the environment

### Art Through the Garden

- **Botanical illustration**: Detailed drawings of plants using pencils, watercolors, or pastels
- **Nature printing**: Use leaves, flowers, and vegetables to create prints
- **Sculpture**: Clay models of garden creatures and plants
- **Color mixing**: Use flowers and berries as natural dyes; explore the color wheel through garden colors
- **Photography**: Document the garden through student photography projects

### Music and Movement Through the Garden

- **Garden songs**: Original and traditional songs about planting, growing, and seasons
- **Rhythm and patterns**: Clapping patterns that mirror growth patterns; drumming with seed pods
- **Movement**: "Grow like a plant" movement sequence -- curl up like a seed, push through the soil, stretch toward the sun, sway in the wind
- **Listening**: Sit quietly in the garden and identify all sounds: birds, insects, wind, water
