# K-2 Regulate Phase: Co-Regulation and Calming

## Overview

The Regulate phase for K-2 learners activates whenever a child moves outside their window of tolerance -- whether into hyperarousal (hitting, yelling, running, crying) or hypoarousal (shutting down, going silent, putting head on desk, staring blankly). At ages 5-8, children are still developing the neural circuitry for self-regulation. They rely almost entirely on co-regulation with a calm, attuned adult to return to a state of readiness.

This phase is never a punishment. It is an essential part of the learning process -- teaching children that big feelings are normal and manageable, and that a trusted adult will help them through.

> **Garden Metaphor**: When a young seedling is battered by wind or heavy rain, the gardener provides a stake or cover to support it until the storm passes. The gardener does not scold the seedling for bending. Co-regulation is the stake that holds the child steady.

---

## Purpose

1. **Detect dysregulation** -- Recognize behavioral signals that a child has left their window of tolerance.
2. **Co-regulate** -- Provide relational warmth and environmental supports so the child's nervous system can settle.
3. **Restore readiness** -- Guide the child back to a regulation level where learning engagement is possible.
4. **Build foundational self-regulation skills** -- Over time, help children begin to recognize their own body signals and ask for help.

---

## Recognizing Dysregulation in Young Children

Young children express dysregulation through their bodies rather than their words. Educators should monitor for the following signals continuously, including during the Root and Reflect phases.

### Hyperarousal Signals (Fight/Flight)

| Signal Category | Observable Behaviors |
|----------------|---------------------|
| Physical | Hitting, kicking, throwing objects, running around the room, climbing on furniture |
| Vocal | Screaming, crying loudly, yelling "No!", making repetitive loud sounds |
| Facial/Postural | Clenched fists, rigid body, flushed face, wide eyes, rapid breathing |
| Relational | Refusing all adult contact, pushing peers, defiant or oppositional stance |

### Hypoarousal Signals (Freeze/Shutdown)

| Signal Category | Observable Behaviors |
|----------------|---------------------|
| Physical | Head on desk, curling into a ball, going limp, sucking thumb, rocking |
| Vocal | Complete silence, whispering, monotone responses, "I don't know" to everything |
| Facial/Postural | Blank stare, glassy eyes, slack jaw, no facial expression, avoiding eye contact |
| Relational | Withdrawing from the group, hiding under a table or behind furniture, turning away |

### Masked Dysregulation in Young Children

Some children mask their distress with behaviors that may appear compliant:
- Excessive people-pleasing ("I'll do whatever you say")
- Perfectionism and erasing repeatedly until paper tears
- Nervous laughter or silliness that does not match the context
- Clinging to the educator and refusing to engage independently

## Co-Regulation Strategies: Tiered Approach

### Tier 1: In-Context Supports (No Phase Transition)

These light-touch strategies can be offered without interrupting the group activity. Use when a child shows early signs of leaving their window of tolerance.

**Proximity and Touch (with consent)**
- Move closer to the child without speaking. Sit beside them.
- Place a gentle hand on their shoulder or back (only if the child has previously consented to touch).
- Offer a weighted lap pad or stuffed animal.

**Verbal Co-Regulation**
- "I'm right here with you."
- "You're safe. I can see this is hard right now."
- "Let's take a breath together. Watch me." (Educator models slow breathing.)
- "I notice your hands are squeezing tight. Can we open them up like a flower?"

**Environmental Adjustment**
- Dim the lights slightly or turn off overhead fluorescents.
- Lower the volume of your own voice.
- Reduce visual clutter near the child.
- Offer noise-reducing headphones.

### Tier 2: Active Regulation Interventions (Brief Pause from Learning)

When Tier 1 supports are not enough and the child's regulation level drops to orange on the observation scale, use active interventions.

**Breathing Exercises with Visual Aids**

| Exercise | Visual Aid | Educator Script |
|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Flower and Candle | Hold up a real or paper flower and a (unlit) birthday candle | "Smell the flower -- big sniff in through your nose. Now blow out the candle -- slow breath out through your mouth. Let's do it three times." |
| Balloon Belly | Place hands on belly | "Put your hands on your tummy. Breathe in and make your balloon get BIG. Now let the air out slowly -- sssssss -- and feel the balloon get small." |
| Square Breathing | Trace a square drawn on paper or in the air | "Breathe in while I trace up. Hold while I trace across. Breathe out while I trace down. Hold while I trace back. Let's go around the square together." |
| Bunny Breaths | Wiggle nose like a bunny | "Take three quick little sniffs through your nose like a bunny, then one long breath out. Sniff-sniff-sniff -- whoooosh." |

**Movement Breaks**
- Walk to the garden together to water a plant.
- Do five jumping jacks, then freeze in tree pose.
- "Shake out your sillies" -- shake hands, arms, legs, whole body, then slowly settle.
- Animal walks down the hallway: bear walk, crab walk, frog jumps (channels hyperarousal energy through structured movement).

**Sensory Garden Activities**
- Dig in the sensory soil bin with hands or tools.
- Pick and smell fresh herbs (lavender, mint, rosemary).
- Hold a smooth garden stone in each hand and squeeze, then release.
- Listen to wind chimes or running water in the garden space.
- Sort seeds by size, color, or texture into cups.

### Tier 3: Calm Corner / Extended Regulation

When a child needs more than a brief intervention, they transition to the designated calm corner or regulation space. This is always presented as a supportive choice, never a consequence.

**Calm Corner Setup for K-2**

Essential items:
- Soft seating (bean bag, floor cushion, small rocking chair)
- Weighted blanket or lap pad
- Sensory bin with garden materials (smooth stones, dried lavender, soft moss)
- Picture menu of calming choices (see below)
- Sand timer (3-minute and 5-minute) so the child can see time passing
- Soft lighting (lamp rather than overhead fluorescent)
- One or two garden-themed picture books

**Calming Choices Picture Menu**

Display a simple visual board with pictures and labels:
1. Take deep breaths (picture of child breathing)
2. Squeeze a stress ball (picture of hands squeezing)
3. Sit in the bean bag (picture of cozy corner)
4. Look at a book (picture of book)
5. Draw how I feel (picture of crayons and paper)
6. Visit my plant (picture of garden)

The child points to their choice. The educator facilitates without academic demands.

**Educator Protocol During Calm Corner Use**
- Stay nearby but do not hover. Check in every 2-3 minutes with brief, warm statements.
- "I'm right here when you're ready."
- "Take all the time you need."
- Do not ask questions or demand explanations while the child is in the calm corner.
- The child signals readiness to return (stands up, makes eye contact, says "I'm ready," or approaches the group).

## Building Early Self-Regulation Skills

The long-term developmental goal is to move from adult-led co-regulation toward the child's emerging self-regulation. This progression unfolds gradually across the K-2 years.

| Stage | Description | K-2 Indicators | Educator Role |
|-------|-------------|----------------|---------------|
| 1. Full Co-Regulation | Adult identifies dysregulation and initiates all strategies | Child accepts comfort; calms with adult support | Detect, intervene, narrate: "Your body is telling you it needs a break." |
| 2. Cued Recognition | Child begins to notice body signals with adult prompting | Child nods when asked "Is your body feeling tight?" | Prompt awareness: "What is your tummy telling you right now?" |
| 3. Assisted Choice | Child selects a strategy from offered options | Child points to calming choice on the picture menu | Offer choices: "Would you like deep breaths or the calm corner?" |
| 4. Prompted Self-Initiation | Child asks for help or a break with adult encouragement | Child says "I need a break" after being reminded that is an option | Reinforce: "Thank you for telling me what you need. That was very brave." |
| 5. Independent Self-Regulation | Child recognizes own dysregulation and initiates a strategy | Child walks to calm corner or begins breathing on own | Celebrate: "You noticed your body needed something and you took care of it. That is real growing." |

Most K-2 children will operate in stages 1-3. Reaching stages 4-5 by second grade represents strong development.

## Educator Language Models for the Regulate Phase

### Entering the Regulate Phase

- "I can see your body is having some big feelings right now. That's okay. I'm going to help."
- "Let's take a break from our work. The learning will be right here when we come back."
- "Your brain needs a rest. Just like plants need water, your brain needs a calm moment."

### During Regulation

- "You are safe. I am right here."
- "Let's breathe together. In... and out... You're doing it."
- "It's okay to feel mad (sad, scared). Feelings are like weather -- they come and go."
- "Your body is working hard to feel better. I can see it."

### Transitioning Out of the Regulate Phase

- "Your body looks more settled now. How does the inside feel?"
- "I think you're ready. Want to pick a new weather card?"
- "You did something really important -- you took care of your feelings. Now let's see what we can learn."

## Garden-Based Regulation Activities

The garden provides uniquely effective regulation opportunities for young children because it engages the body, the senses, and the nurturing instinct simultaneously.

| Activity | Regulation Mechanism | Materials Needed |
|----------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Digging and turning soil | Rhythmic heavy work calms the proprioceptive system | Child-sized shovels, garden bed |
| Watering with a gentle hose or watering can | Repetitive motion; sound of water is calming | Watering cans, accessible water source |
| Picking herbs and smelling them | Olfactory input activates the parasympathetic nervous system | Herb garden (lavender, mint, basil) |
| Pulling weeds | Provides an outlet for frustration through purposeful physical work | Garden gloves, weed-filled area |
| Sitting quietly in the garden observation spot | Nature exposure reduces cortisol levels | Bench or blanket in a shaded garden area |
| Sorting seeds or stones | Repetitive fine motor task; focuses attention | Seed collection, sorting cups |

## Assessment Indicators

| Indicator | Beginning | Developing | Secure |
|-----------|-----------|------------|--------|
| Accepts co-regulation from adult | Resists or escalates when adult intervenes | Accepts comfort after initial resistance | Readily accepts adult support; calms within 3-5 minutes |
| Identifies body signals (with prompting) | Cannot identify any body signals | Can point to where feeling is (e.g., "my tummy") when asked | Names body signal without prompting: "My hands are tight" |
| Uses calming strategy (with support) | Does not engage with offered strategies | Engages with one preferred strategy when offered | Selects from calming menu; engages until feeling settles |
| Returns to learning after regulation | Cannot return within the session | Returns with extended transition support (10+ minutes) | Returns to learning within 5-8 minutes with brief re-grounding |
| Begins to self-advocate | Does not communicate needs | Communicates need non-verbally (points to calm corner, pulls educator's hand) | Uses words: "I need a break" or "I need help" |

## Transition Out of the Regulate Phase

When the child's body shows signs of settling (breathing slows, muscles relax, eye contact returns, child speaks or engages), the educator transitions the child back to a brief Root re-check before rejoining learning.

- "Let's pick a new weather card. How does your inside weather feel now?"
- If the child selects sunshine or partly cloudy: "Wonderful. You took care of yourself. Let's go back and see what the group is growing."
- If the child still selects storm or rain: "That's okay. Let's stay here a little longer. There's no rush."

The child is never forced to return before they are ready. Learning can be adapted or abbreviated for the remainder of the session as needed.
