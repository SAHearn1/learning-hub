# K-2 Grade Band: Core Academic Units — Mathematics

## Overview

Mathematics instruction in the K-2 RootWork curriculum is inseparable from the garden experience. Young children develop number sense, measurement concepts, data literacy, and early algebraic thinking through hands-on interactions with seeds, plants, soil, water, and the living garden ecosystem. Every mathematical concept is introduced through concrete garden experiences, developed through guided practice with manipulatives, and extended through growth journal documentation.

The garden provides what traditional classrooms often lack for early learners: authentic, meaningful contexts where mathematics is a tool for understanding the real world rather than an abstract exercise. When a child counts seeds, they are not performing a school task -- they are preparing to grow something alive. This authenticity is particularly powerful for students with trauma histories, for whom school-based tasks may carry associations with failure or shame.

## Unit 1: Counting, Cardinality & Number Sense (Weeks 1-6)

### Essential Questions
- How many seeds do we need?
- How can we count things in our garden accurately?
- How do numbers help gardeners?

### Learning Objectives
- Count objects to 20 (K) / 120 (1st) / 1000 (2nd) with one-to-one correspondence
- Compare quantities using the language of more, fewer, and equal
- Understand that the last number counted tells the total (cardinality)
- Write numerals to represent garden quantities
- Use counting to solve real garden problems

### Week-by-Week Progression

#### Week 1: Seeds and Counting (Kindergarten Focus)

**Root Phase:** Students arrive at the garden and receive a small bag of mixed seeds (sunflower, bean, corn, pumpkin). They hold the seeds, feel the textures, and smell them. Emotion check-in: "If you were a seed today, would you be a tiny seed or a big seed? A seed that feels ready to grow, or one that needs a little more rest?"

**Reflect Phase Activities:**
1. **Seed Sort and Count**: Students sort seeds by type onto plates. Count each group using one-to-one correspondence. Teacher models touching each seed while counting aloud.
   - Scaffold: Place seeds on a number line mat to reinforce correspondence
   - Extension: "How many seeds do we have altogether?"
2. **Seed Estimation Jar**: Display a jar of seeds. Students estimate how many. Count together to verify.
   - Use TRACE: "What do you notice about the jar? (Think) How could we figure out how many? (Reason) Tell your partner your guess. (Articulate) Let's count and check! (Check) What if we used a bigger jar? (Extend)"
3. **Growth Journal**: Draw the seeds and write the numeral for how many of each type.

**Reconnect Phase:** "How many seeds does your family use when they cook? Ask someone at home!"

#### Week 2: Garden Measurement Introduction

**Reflect Phase Activities:**
1. **How Tall Is It?**: Measure classroom plants using linking cubes. Stack cubes next to the plant and count. Record in growth journals.
   - Language: "The plant is ___ cubes tall."
   - Compare: "Which plant is taller? Which is shorter? How do you know?"
2. **Hand-Span Measurement**: Measure the length of garden beds using hand-spans. Discover that different students get different measurements (building toward the need for standard units).
3. **Number Line Garden Walk**: Place number cards along the garden path. Students walk the "number line" stepping on each number in order.

#### Week 3: Comparing and Ordering

**Reflect Phase Activities:**
1. **More or Fewer?**: Compare seed collections. "Maria has 7 sunflower seeds. James has 5. Who has more? How many more?"
   - Use concrete materials: line up seeds side by side for visual comparison
2. **Garden Lineup**: Arrange 5 plants from shortest to tallest. Use comparison language: taller than, shorter than, the same height as.
3. **Greater Than Caterpillar**: Introduce the > and < symbols using a caterpillar character who always eats the bigger pile of seeds.

#### Weeks 4-6: Building Toward Fluency

**Activities spiral and deepen:**
- Counting collections of garden harvest items (cherry tomatoes, beans, herbs by the leaf)
- Skip counting by 2s (pairs of leaves), 5s (fingers used in planting), 10s (seed packets)
- Writing numbers in garden journals with increasing independence
- Solving "how many altogether" and "how many are left" story problems using garden contexts

### Differentiation

| Readiness Level | Modification |
|----------------|-------------|
| **Approaching** | Count to 10 with physical objects; use number mats with dots; adult counts alongside child |
| **On Level** | Count to 20-50; compare groups; write numerals independently |
| **Advanced** | Count beyond 100; create own comparison problems; estimate before counting and evaluate accuracy |

### Restore Phase Integration

When counting errors occur:
- "You counted 8, and there are actually 9. Let's count together and find where the sneaky one was hiding! Seeds are good at hiding."
- Provide a counting mat where each seed has a designated space
- Celebrate recounting: "You checked your work just like a real scientist!"

## Unit 2: Addition & Subtraction (Weeks 7-14)

### Essential Questions
- What happens when we plant more seeds?
- What happens when we harvest vegetables?
- How can we use numbers to tell the story of our garden?

### Learning Objectives
- Represent addition as putting together and adding to
- Represent subtraction as taking apart and taking from
- Solve word problems within 10 (K) / 20 (1st) / 100 (2nd) using garden contexts
- Use drawings, manipulatives, and equations to represent solutions
- Understand the relationship between addition and subtraction

### Core Lesson Sequence

#### Lesson: Planting Day Addition (1st Grade Focus)

**Root Phase:** Planting day ritual -- students put on garden gloves, choose their seed packets, and do three deep "gardener breaths" before heading outside.

**Reflect Phase:**

*Setup:* Each student has a small planting tray with 10 cells.

**Problem 1 (Joining):** "Yesterday we planted 4 bean seeds. Today we're planting 3 more. How many bean seeds will we have in all?"
- Students physically place 4 seeds in the tray, then 3 more
- Count total: "4 and 3 makes 7!"
- Record: Draw the seeds. Write 4 + 3 = 7
- TRACE: "What did you notice about the groups? (Think) How did you figure out the total? (Reason) Explain to your garden buddy. (Articulate) Count all the seeds to check. (Check) What if we planted 5 more tomorrow? (Extend)"

**Problem 2 (Result Unknown):** "Our garden had 8 tomatoes. The birds ate 2. How many tomatoes do we have now?"
- Act it out with manipulatives (counters shaped like tomatoes, or actual cherry tomatoes)
- Cross out the ones the birds took. Count what's left.
- Record: 8 - 2 = 6
- Garden connection: "This is why we might need nets or scarecrows!"

**Problem 3 (Change Unknown):** "We need 10 lettuce plants for our salad garden. We already planted 6. How many more do we need to plant?"
- Use the 10-cell tray. Place 6 seeds. How many empty cells?
- Students count on from 6 to 10: "7, 8, 9, 10 -- we need 4 more!"
- Record: 6 + ___ = 10

**Reconnect Phase:** "If we grow 10 heads of lettuce and share them equally with 2 classrooms, how many does each class get?" (Preview of future division concepts)

#### Lesson: The Garden Store (2nd Grade Focus)

**Setup:** Classroom "garden store" with items labeled with prices in cents (seed packets: 5¢, 10¢, 15¢; small tools: 20¢, 25¢; pots: 30¢, 35¢). Students receive play money.

**Reflect Phase Activities:**

1. **Shopping Problems:**
   - "You have 50¢. You buy a seed packet for 15¢ and a pot for 30¢. How much did you spend? How much change do you get?"
   - Students use number lines, hundreds charts, or base-ten blocks to compute
   - Multiple strategies encouraged: counting on, making tens, using known facts

2. **Inventory Counting:**
   - "We sold 23 seed packets in the morning and 18 in the afternoon. How many total?"
   - Students model with base-ten blocks: 2 tens + 1 ten = 3 tens; 3 ones + 8 ones = 11 ones = 1 ten and 1 one. Total: 4 tens and 1 one = 41.
   - Connect to real garden inventory: How many pots do we need for the class?

3. **Making Change:**
   - Practice subtraction within 100 using the garden store context
   - "A family bought herbs for 47¢ and paid with two quarters (50¢). What's their change?"

### Problem Types Progression

| Problem Type | Garden Context | Example |
|-------------|---------------|---------|
| **Join (Result Unknown)** | Planting more seeds | 5 seeds + 3 seeds = ? |
| **Separate (Result Unknown)** | Harvesting vegetables | 12 tomatoes - 4 picked = ? |
| **Join (Change Unknown)** | How many more to plant | 6 planted + ? = 10 needed |
| **Separate (Change Unknown)** | How many were eaten | 9 strawberries - ? = 5 left |
| **Compare (Difference Unknown)** | Comparing garden rows | Row A has 14, Row B has 9. How many more in A? |
| **Part-Part-Whole** | Sorting harvest | We picked 20 vegetables: some peppers and some squash. 13 were peppers. How many squash? |

## Unit 3: Measurement & Data (Weeks 15-22)

### Essential Questions
- How do we measure things in our garden?
- How can we record what we observe?
- What can data tell us about our garden?

### Learning Objectives
- Measure lengths using non-standard and standard units
- Compare and order objects by measurable attributes
- Collect, organize, and represent data using tally charts, picture graphs, and bar graphs
- Tell and write time related to garden schedules
- Identify and count coins related to garden economics

### Core Lesson Sequence

#### Lesson: Tracking Plant Growth (Ongoing Project)

**Setup:** Each student or pair has a designated plant to track over multiple weeks.

**Week 1 -- Baseline Measurement:**
- Measure initial plant height using linking cubes
- Record in growth journal with date, drawing, and measurement
- Introduce data table: Date | Height in Cubes | Observations

**Week 2 -- Standard Units Introduction (1st-2nd Grade):**
- "Different students got different measurements with cubes because the cubes are different sizes. What if we all used the same tool?"
- Introduce rulers. Practice measuring the same plants with rulers.
- Compare: "When we used cubes, we said 8 cubes. With a ruler, we say 6 inches. Same plant, different tools!"

**Weeks 3-6 -- Ongoing Data Collection:**
- Measure weekly. Record in data tables.
- Create picture graphs showing growth over time (one picture = 1 inch of growth)
- Bar graphs comparing different students' plants
- Class discussion: "Whose plant grew the most this week? Whose plant is tallest overall? Are those the same thing?" (Distinguishing between growth rate and total height)

**TRACE Application:**
- Think: "What do you notice about your data table?"
- Reason: "Why do you think Jasmine's plant grew more than Tyler's?"
- Articulate: "Explain to the class what your graph shows."
- Check: "Let's remeasure to make sure our data is accurate."
- Extend: "What would happen to our data if we forgot to water for a week?"

#### Lesson: Garden Survey (Data Collection & Representation)

**Reflect Phase Activities:**

1. **Class Survey:** "What vegetable should we plant next?" Students vote. Tally the results.
   - K: Create a picture graph using stamps or stickers
   - 1st: Create a bar graph with a scale of 1
   - 2nd: Create a bar graph with a scale of 2; write questions that can be answered from the graph

2. **Garden Census:** Go to the garden and count different types of organisms.
   - Record: How many earthworms? Butterflies? Ladybugs? Ants?
   - Organize data in a tally chart
   - Represent as a picture graph: one picture = 1 organism (K-1) or 2 organisms (2nd)

3. **Weather Data:** Track daily temperature and rainfall for two weeks.
   - Compare to plant growth data: "Do plants grow more when it rains?"
   - 2nd grade: Line plots showing daily temperatures

#### Lesson: Garden Geometry (Shape Recognition and Spatial Reasoning)

**Reflect Phase Activities:**

1. **Shape Hunt:** Walk through the garden identifying shapes.
   - Circles: sunflower heads, pot rims, stepping stones
   - Rectangles: garden beds, labels, bricks
   - Triangles: trellises, A-frame supports
   - Record findings with drawings and labels

2. **Garden Bed Design** (2nd Grade):
   - Use grid paper to design a garden bed
   - Partition rectangles into rows and columns
   - Count total squares (building toward area concepts)
   - "If each square is one plant, how many plants can we fit?"

3. **3D Shape Exploration:**
   - Spheres: round fruits (tomatoes, melons)
   - Cylinders: pots, cans, stems
   - Cones: carrots, radishes
   - Cubes: compost bins, block planters

## Unit 4: Patterns, Place Value & Problem Solving (Weeks 23-30)

### Essential Questions
- What patterns can we find in the garden?
- How do big numbers work?
- How can we solve garden problems that seem hard at first?

### Learning Objectives
- Identify, describe, and extend patterns found in nature
- Understand place value within 100 (1st) / 1000 (2nd)
- Use place value understanding to add and subtract
- Apply multiple strategies to solve multi-step garden problems

### Core Lesson Sequence

#### Lesson: Patterns in Nature

**Reflect Phase Activities:**

1. **Fibonacci in the Garden:**
   - Count petals on different flowers: lily (3), buttercup (5), daisy (8, 13, 21)
   - Observe spiral patterns in sunflower seed heads
   - K: "Some flowers have a few petals, some have a lot. Let's count!"
   - 1-2: "Do you notice a pattern in the numbers? 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8..."

2. **Leaf Pattern Symmetry:**
   - Fold a leaf in half. Does it match? This is symmetry!
   - Create symmetrical leaf prints using paint
   - Draw the line of symmetry on leaf rubbings

3. **Growth Patterns:**
   - Track the number of leaves on a plant each week: 2, 4, 6, 8...
   - "What pattern do you see? How many leaves will there be next week?"
   - Create growing patterns with seeds: 1, 2, 3... or 2, 4, 6...

#### Lesson: Place Value Garden (1st-2nd Grade)

**Setup:** Large collection of seeds (hundreds). Small cups (ones), bags of 10 (tens), boxes of 100 (hundreds, 2nd grade only).

**Reflect Phase Activities:**

1. **Bundling Seeds:**
   - Count a large pile of seeds by putting 10 in each cup
   - "We have 7 cups of 10 and 3 left over. That's 73 seeds!"
   - 2nd grade: 10 cups of 10 make a bag of 100

2. **Garden Orders:**
   - "The school garden needs to order seeds. We need 234 sunflower seeds. Can you show that with our cups and bags?"
   - 2 boxes of 100, 3 cups of 10, 4 single seeds
   - Expanded form: 200 + 30 + 4

3. **Comparison Shopping:**
   - Compare seed quantities: "Package A has 156 seeds. Package B has 198 seeds. Which has more? How do you know?"
   - Use place value understanding: compare hundreds first, then tens, then ones

### Reasoning Moves in Practice (K-2 Adapted)

| Reasoning Move | K-2 Language | Garden Example |
|---------------|-------------|----------------|
| **Decompose** | "Break it into parts" | "12 tomatoes = 10 in the basket + 2 in my hand" |
| **Compare** | "Same or different?" | "How is a seed the same as a bulb? How is it different?" |
| **Question** | "I wonder..." | "I wonder why this plant grew taller than that one?" |
| **Verify** | "Let's check" | "Let's count again to make sure we have enough pots" |
| **Generalize** | "It always..." | "Big seeds always make big plants... or do they?" |
| **Transform** | "Show it another way" | "You drew 5 + 3. Can you show it with seeds too?" |
| **Connect** | "This is like..." | "Counting seeds is like counting money -- we group by tens!" |

## Unit 5: Year-End Integration & Celebration (Weeks 31-36)

### Essential Questions
- How has our garden grown this year?
- How have we grown as mathematicians?
- How can we share what we've learned?

### Learning Objectives
- Apply year-long mathematical skills to a culminating project
- Analyze a full year of garden data
- Communicate mathematical thinking through multiple representations
- Celebrate growth and effort

### Culminating Project: Our Garden Story (in Numbers)

Students create a "Garden Math Book" that documents their mathematical journey:

**Page 1: "In the beginning..."**
- How many seeds we planted at the start of the year (counting)
- Drawing and numeral

**Page 2: "Our garden grew..."**
- Graph of plant growth over the year (measurement, data)
- "My plant grew from ___ inches to ___ inches"

**Page 3: "We solved garden problems..."**
- A favorite word problem with solution and illustration
- Multiple representations (drawing, equation, words)

**Page 4: "Garden math facts..."**
- "We harvested ___ vegetables this year" (addition of harvest records)
- "The tallest plant was ___ inches" (measurement)
- "The class favorite vegetable was ___" (data from surveys)

**Page 5: "Next year's garden..."**
- Students plan: "I want to grow ___. I will need ___ seeds, ___ pots, and ___ weeks."
- Applying math skills to a future plan

### Assessment Through Celebration

The Garden Math Book serves as both a portfolio assessment and a celebration of learning. Families are invited to a "Garden Gallery" where students present their books and share their mathematical growth.
