# K-2 Reconnect Phase: Sharing and Celebrating Growth

## Overview

The Reconnect phase completes the 5Rs learning cycle by helping K-2 learners apply what they have learned to their own lives, share their discoveries with others, and celebrate the effort and thinking they invested in the session. For young children, this phase is concrete and joyful -- it centers on show-and-tell, garden walks, simple drawings, harvest sharing, and family connections.

At ages 5-8, children learn best when new knowledge is immediately connected to something familiar and personally meaningful. The Reconnect phase ensures that academic content does not remain abstract or confined to the lesson -- it becomes part of the child's story, their garden, their family, and their community.

> **Garden Metaphor**: The Reconnect phase is the harvest -- the time when we gather what has grown, share it with others, and save seeds for the next planting. Just as a garden's harvest feeds the community, a child's learning enriches everyone around them.

---

## Purpose

1. **Apply learning** -- Connect new concepts to the child's own life, garden, and experiences.
2. **Share discoveries** -- Practice oral language and build confidence through show-and-tell and partner sharing.
3. **Celebrate growth** -- Acknowledge effort, thinking, and persistence (not just correct answers).
4. **Create documentation** -- Record learning through drawings, photos, and simple portfolio entries.
5. **Set forward intentions** -- Establish a simple goal or wondering for the next session.
6. **Strengthen family connections** -- Send learning home through garden produce, drawings, and take-home activities.

---

## Key Activities

### Show-and-Tell: Sharing Learning with a Partner

After the Reflect phase, each child shares one thing they learned or discovered with a partner. This is a structured, brief activity (2-3 minutes) that develops oral language, builds confidence, and reinforces learning through explanation.

**Educator Facilitation**:
1. "Turn to your garden partner. Tell them one thing you learned today."
2. Provide a sentence starter for children who need support: "Today I learned that..." or "I noticed that..."
3. After partner sharing, invite two or three volunteers to share with the whole group.
4. The educator restates and validates each child's sharing: "Malik noticed that the roots go down into the soil. That's important -- roots hold the plant steady, just like our grounding holds us steady."

**Sentence Starters for K-2 Sharing**:

| Grade | Sentence Starters |
|-------|------------------|
| Kindergarten | "I saw..." / "I learned..." / "My plant..." |
| First Grade | "Today I learned that..." / "I noticed that... because..." / "My favorite part was..." |
| Second Grade | "Something interesting I discovered was..." / "I figured out that... by..." / "This connects to... because..." |

### Garden Walk: Connecting Learning to the Living Garden

After the sharing, the class takes a brief walk through the garden (or reviews garden photos/materials if outdoors is not accessible). The educator connects the day's learning to something visible in the garden.

**Sample Garden Walk Connections**:

| Lesson Topic | Garden Walk Connection | Educator Prompt |
|-------------|----------------------|-----------------|
| Counting and numbers | Count the tomatoes on a vine, petals on a flower | "Let's count together. How many tomatoes are on this plant?" |
| Measurement | Compare the height of two different plants | "Which plant is taller? How could we measure to be sure?" |
| Plant needs (sun, water, soil) | Observe which plants are in sun vs. shade | "Look at this sunny spot and this shady spot. What differences do you notice in the plants?" |
| Life cycles | Find plants at different stages (seed, sprout, mature, flowering) | "Can you find a baby plant and a grown-up plant? What's different?" |
| Weather and seasons | Notice how the garden changes with the season | "What does our garden look like in fall? How is it different from spring?" |

### Drawing and Portfolio Documentation

Young children document their learning primarily through drawing, with emergent writing added as developmentally appropriate. Each child maintains a **Garden Growth Journal** that serves as a simple portfolio.

**Garden Growth Journal Entry Structure**:

Each entry includes:
1. **Date** (written by educator or child, depending on ability)
2. **Drawing** of something learned, observed, or created during the session
3. **Label or sentence** (dictated to educator if needed; independently written by older K-2 students)
4. **Spark count** (optional: number of "brain sparks" from the Restore phase)

**Educator Facilitation**:
- "Draw one thing you learned today in your garden journal."
- "Can you label your drawing? What is this part called?"
- "Tell me about your drawing. I'll write your words next to it." (For pre-writers, the educator scribes.)
- "Would you like to add a spark to your journal for the mistake you learned from today?"

**Portfolio Review**: At the end of each month, the educator and child review the journal together, looking at growth over time. "Look at your drawing from the first week and your drawing today. What do you notice? You have grown so much!"

### Photo Documentation

When available, take photographs of:
- Children working in the garden
- Completed activities and projects
- Plant growth over time (same plant photographed weekly)
- Children sharing their learning with partners

Photos can be printed and added to the portfolio, displayed on a classroom "Growing Wall," or sent home to families.

## Celebration of Growth

The Reconnect phase celebrates effort, thinking, and persistence -- not just correct answers. Celebration is specific, process-oriented, and connected to the 5Rs framework.

### Celebration Language Models

| Type of Celebration | Educator Language |
|--------------------|-------------------|
| Effort | "You worked so hard on that today. Your brain was really growing." |
| Persistence | "You didn't give up even when it was tricky. That's what gardeners do -- they keep tending." |
| Thinking | "I heard you say 'Let me try a different way.' That is exactly what a scientist does." |
| Kindness/Community | "You helped your garden partner count the seeds. That's how we grow together." |
| Growth from error | "Remember when you said 'Oops and grow'? Look at what you figured out after that!" |
| Self-regulation | "You noticed your body needed a break and you took one. That takes real strength." |

### Avoid Empty Praise

| Instead of... | Say... |
|--------------|--------|
| "Good job!" | "You counted every seed carefully. That took real focus." |
| "You're so smart!" | "You figured that out by trying two different ways. That's clever thinking." |
| "Perfect!" | "You checked your answer and it matched. That's what careful scientists do." |
| "You're the best!" | "Your brain grew so much today. I can see it in your work." |

### Group Celebrations

At the end of each week (or at the educator's discretion), the class celebrates collective growth:

- **Growth Vine**: A paper vine on the wall where each child adds a leaf with one thing they learned that week. Over the semester, the vine grows across the wall.
- **Harvest Bell**: Ring a small bell (or chime) when the class reaches a group milestone (e.g., "We planted 50 seeds this month!" or "Everyone in the class drew in their journal today!").
- **Garden Party**: Monthly celebration where children share their favorite journal entry or garden accomplishment with the class. Serve simple garden snacks (cherry tomatoes, snap peas, herbs on crackers) when produce is available.

## Family and Community Connections

### Sending Learning Home

The Reconnect phase extends beyond the classroom by connecting learning to the child's family and home life.

**Weekly Take-Home Activities**:

| Activity | Description | Family Connection |
|----------|------------|-------------------|
| Seed packet with instructions | Children bring home seeds to plant with family members | Growing something together; observing at home |
| "Ask Me About..." card | A small card that says "Ask me about [topic]!" so families can prompt conversation | Oral language practice; family engagement with curriculum |
| Garden journal page (photocopy) | Copy of the week's journal entry to share | Families see learning progress; conversation starter |
| Recipe card with garden produce | When harvest is available, send home produce with a simple recipe | Nutrition, family cooking, math (measuring) |
| "I Can Teach You" challenge | Child teaches a family member one thing from the garden lesson | Deepens learning through teaching; family bonding |

### Harvest Sharing

When the garden produces a harvest, children participate in picking, counting, sorting, and distributing the produce. This is a powerful Reconnect activity because it makes the entire 5Rs cycle tangible -- from planting (Root) through growing (Reflect) to sharing the results.

**Harvest Sharing Protocol**:
1. Children help pick ripe produce (fine motor skills, observation).
2. Count and sort the harvest (math: counting, classification).
3. Divide equally among the children to take home (math: fair sharing, early division).
4. Wash and package the produce together (life skills, community care).
5. Each child takes home their share with an "Ask Me About..." card explaining what they grew.

### Family Garden Events

Schedule one or two family events per semester:
- **Family Planting Day**: Families come to the school garden to plant together. Children lead their family members through the garden, showing them what they have grown and learned.
- **Harvest Celebration**: End-of-season event where families visit the garden, see portfolio displays, taste garden produce, and hear children share their learning.

## Intention Setting for K-2

Each session ends with a brief, simple intention or wondering for the next time. This gives children a sense of continuity and ownership over their learning.

**Educator Facilitation**:
- "Next time, I wonder if we could... What do you think?"
- "Is there something you want to look at more closely in the garden?"
- "What do you want to try next time?"

For younger K-2 students, the intention may be set by the educator with the group: "Next time, we are going to measure our plants again and see how much they grew. I can't wait!"

For older K-2 students (second graders), individual intentions can be recorded: "Write or draw one thing you want to do or learn next time."

## Lesson Closing Ritual

Just as the Root phase begins with a consistent entry ritual, the Reconnect phase ends with a consistent closing ritual that signals the end of the learning cycle and creates a sense of completion.

### Closing Ritual Sequence

1. **Journal time** (2-3 min): Children draw or write in their garden journal.
2. **Partner share** (1-2 min): Turn and tell your partner one thing you learned.
3. **Growth cheer** (30 sec): The class says together: "We grew today! Roots down, leaves up, growing strong!"
4. **Closing song** (optional, 1 min): Sing the closing verse of the garden song.

**"Growing Strong" Closing Song (sung to the tune of "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star")**:
> We are growing, watch us bloom,
> In our garden, in our room.
> Roots go deep and leaves reach high,
> Growing, learning, you and I.
> We are growing, watch us bloom,
> See you next time -- back here soon!

## Assessment Indicators

| Indicator | Beginning | Developing | Secure |
|-----------|-----------|------------|--------|
| Shares learning with partner | Does not share or says "I don't know" | Shares one idea with prompting and sentence starter | Shares independently using descriptive language |
| Connects learning to the garden | Cannot identify a garden connection | Points to a garden connection when prompted by educator | Independently names how the lesson connects to the garden |
| Creates journal entry | Does not draw or write | Draws with adult prompting; drawing loosely relates to lesson | Draws and labels/writes independently; entry clearly reflects learning |
| Uses celebration language | Does not acknowledge own growth | Responds positively when educator celebrates their effort | Self-celebrates: "I learned something new!" or "My brain grew!" |
| Sets forward intention | Cannot identify a next step | Responds to educator's suggested intention ("Yes, I want to do that") | Independently names something they want to learn or try next |
| Engages in family connection | Does not bring home materials or share with family | Brings home materials; family reports some conversation | Teaches family member about garden learning; family participates in events |

## Transition to the Next 5Rs Cycle

The Reconnect phase completes the current learning cycle. When the child returns for the next session, the cycle begins again with the Root phase -- grounding, checking in, and establishing readiness. This predictable rhythm (Root, Regulate, Reflect, Restore, Reconnect) creates the structured consistency that is essential for children who have experienced trauma.

Over time, children internalize this rhythm:
- "First we ground ourselves."
- "Then we check how our body feels."
- "Then we learn and discover."
- "If we make a mistake, we grow from it."
- "Then we share what we learned and celebrate."

This is the cycle of the garden, and the cycle of growth.
