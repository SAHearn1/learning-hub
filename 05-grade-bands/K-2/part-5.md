# K-2 Grade Band: Assessment, Family Engagement & Garden Activities

## Assessment Framework for K-2

### Assessment Philosophy

Assessment in the K-2 RootWork curriculum serves learning rather than ranking. Young children are developing at dramatically different rates across cognitive, social-emotional, physical, and linguistic domains -- and children with trauma histories may show particularly uneven developmental profiles, with strengths in some areas and significant gaps in others. Our assessment approach is designed to:

1. **Capture growth over time** rather than snapshot performance
2. **Use authentic garden-based tasks** rather than decontextualized tests
3. **Include multiple modes of expression** (drawing, speaking, building, writing) rather than privileging written language
4. **Document social-emotional development** alongside academic progress
5. **Inform instruction** rather than produce grades
6. **Celebrate effort and reasoning** rather than correct answers alone

### Diagnostic Assessment (Beginning of Year)

#### Garden Readiness Assessment

Administered individually during the first two weeks, this diagnostic assessment uses garden-based tasks to evaluate students' incoming skills:

**Mathematical Thinking:**

| Task | What It Measures | Materials |
|------|-----------------|-----------|
| "Can you count these seeds for me?" (provide 15-25 seeds) | Counting, one-to-one correspondence, cardinality | Mixed seeds |
| "Can you put these seeds in groups?" | Classification, sorting by attributes | Seeds of different types/sizes |
| "Which group has more? How do you know?" | Comparison, beginning quantitative reasoning | Two unequal seed groups |
| "We need to share these 10 seeds equally between 2 pots. Can you do that?" | Fair sharing, early division concepts | 10 seeds, 2 pots |
| "This plant is ___ tall" (provide linking cubes) | Measurement concept, units | Plant and linking cubes |
| "What shape is this garden bed? This stepping stone?" | Shape recognition | Garden structures or photos |

**Scientific Thinking:**

| Task | What It Measures | Materials |
|------|-----------------|-----------|
| "What do you think a seed needs to grow?" | Prior knowledge, scientific reasoning | Seed |
| "Look at this plant. What do you notice?" | Observation skills, vocabulary | Potted plant |
| "What do you think will happen if we don't water this plant?" | Prediction, cause-and-effect thinking | Photo of a healthy plant |
| "Can you draw what you see?" | Observation and recording skills | Magnifying glass, journal |

**Literacy:**

| Task | What It Measures | Materials |
|------|-----------------|-----------|
| "Can you read these garden words?" (sight words in garden context) | Word recognition | Word cards: seed, plant, grow, water, sun |
| "What sound does 'seed' start with?" | Phonemic awareness | Verbal prompt |
| "Can you write your name on your garden journal?" | Writing development | Journal, pencil |
| "Tell me about this picture" (garden scene) | Oral language, narrative skills | Illustrated garden scene |

**Social-Emotional:**

| Task | What It Measures | Materials |
|------|-----------------|-----------|
| How does the child enter the room? | Comfort with transitions, attachment style | Observation |
| Does the child make eye contact with adults? Peers? | Social engagement, potential hypervigilance | Observation |
| How does the child respond to a novel task? | Risk tolerance, self-efficacy | New activity |
| Can the child share materials? Take turns? | Social skills development | Group activity |
| How does the child respond when something goes wrong? | Frustration tolerance, self-regulation | Naturally occurring challenge |

Results are recorded on a developmental continuum (not a score) and used to plan initial instruction and identify students who may need additional support or evaluation.

### Formative Assessment (Ongoing)

#### Growth Journal Analysis

Teachers review growth journals weekly, noting progression in:

| Domain | What to Look For | Evidence of Growth |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Drawing** | Detail, accuracy of observation, labeling | From scribbles to recognizable shapes to detailed, labeled diagrams |
| **Writing** | Letter formation, spelling development, sentence structure | From marks to letters to words to phrases to sentences to multi-sentence entries |
| **Math** | Number representation, measurement recording, data representation | From random marks to accurate numerals to organized data tables |
| **Science** | Observation vocabulary, question quality, evidence use | From "I see a plant" to "The leaves are turning yellow and curling at the edges, maybe because we watered too much" |
| **SEL** | Feeling vocabulary, self-reflection, empathy expression | From single emotion words to nuanced feeling descriptions to connection to strategies |

#### Observation-Based Checks

**Quick Checks During Garden Activities:**
- Clipboard with class roster and skill checklist
- Note specific observations during activities: "Marcus counted to 23 accurately during seed sorting" or "Aisha used the term 'germinate' correctly in conversation"
- Focus on 4-5 students per session for detailed observation (rotating through the class)

**TRACE Protocol Observation:**
- Can the student Think (observe and activate prior knowledge)?
- Can the student Reason (develop a plan or strategy)?
- Can the student Articulate (explain their thinking to others)?
- Can the student Check (verify their own work)?
- Can the student Extend (apply learning to new contexts)?

Record on a simple rubric: Not Yet Observed / Emerging / Developing / Consistent

#### Performance Tasks (Monthly)

**Example: "The Planting Problem" (1st Grade, mid-year)**

*Task:* "Our class wants to plant a garden bed with 3 rows. We want 5 plants in each row. How many plants do we need? Show your thinking in at least two ways."

*Assessment Criteria:*

| Criterion | Emerging | Developing | Proficient | Advanced |
|-----------|----------|------------|------------|----------|
| **Understanding** | Attempts the problem but may misinterpret | Understands the problem with support | Understands independently | Identifies additional constraints or questions |
| **Strategy** | Uses one strategy with errors | Uses one strategy correctly | Uses two strategies correctly | Creates an efficient strategy and explains why it works |
| **Representation** | Drawing or manipulatives only | Drawing with some numerical representation | Drawing, equation, and words | Multiple representations with clear connections between them |
| **Communication** | Cannot explain thinking | Partial explanation | Clear explanation of process and reasoning | Explains and justifies, considers alternatives |
| **Growth Mindset** | Gives up when challenged | Persists with encouragement | Persists independently and self-corrects | Seeks challenge and reflects on the learning process |

### Summative Assessment (End of Unit/Trimester)

#### Garden Portfolio Review

Each trimester, students compile a portfolio that includes:

1. **Selected growth journal entries** (3-5 best entries, chosen by the student with teacher guidance)
2. **Photos of garden work** with student-dictated or written captions
3. **One completed performance task** with scoring rubric
4. **Self-assessment page:** "Things I can do now that I couldn't do before" (drawn or written)
5. **SEL reflection:** "The kind of garden friend I am" (drawing and words)
6. **Teacher observation summary:** Brief narrative of growth and areas for development

#### Student-Led Portfolio Conferences

Twice per year, students present their portfolios to their families:

**Preparation (1-2 sessions):**
- Students review their portfolios with teacher guidance
- Practice presenting: "This is my favorite journal entry because..."
- Prepare one garden demonstration (plant a seed, measure a plant, identify parts of a plant)

**Conference Structure (20-30 minutes per family):**
1. Student greets family and gives a garden tour
2. Student presents 2-3 portfolio pieces, explaining their learning
3. Student demonstrates a garden skill
4. Family asks questions and celebrates
5. Teacher shares brief academic and SEL update
6. Family and student set one goal together for next trimester

## Family Engagement

### Philosophy

Family engagement in the K-2 RootWork curriculum recognizes that:

- Families are children's first and most important teachers
- Trauma-informed practice extends to families, who may have their own trauma histories and complicated relationships with schools
- The garden provides a non-threatening, welcoming entry point for families who may be wary of school settings
- All families have assets, knowledge, and cultural traditions related to growing food
- Engagement should be invitational, flexible, and strengths-based

### Communication Strategies

**Weekly "Garden Growth Report":**
A simple, visual take-home note (half-page) that includes:
- What we learned this week (with a picture)
- A conversation starter: "Ask your child about ___"
- A home activity suggestion (optional, low-barrier)
- A positive note about the child: "This week, [name] showed [specific strength or effort]"

Template:
```
+-------------------------------------+
|  Garden Growth Report               |
|  Week of: ___________               |
|                                     |
|  This Week We Learned:              |
|  [topic with simple illustration]   |
|                                     |
|  Ask Me About:                      |
|  "[conversation starter]"           |
|                                     |
|  Try This at Home:                  |
|  [simple, optional activity]        |
|                                     |
|  [Child's Name] Showed:            |
|  [specific positive observation]    |
+-------------------------------------+
```

**Monthly Family Garden Newsletter:**
- Garden photos with student quotes
- Upcoming events and volunteer opportunities
- Seasonal gardening tips families can use at home
- Recipe using a vegetable from the school garden
- Vocabulary words we're learning (in English and home languages)
- Reminders about portfolio conferences and celebrations

**Digital Communication:**
- Photo/video updates through a secure class platform (with appropriate consent)
- Option for families to communicate in their home language through translation tools
- QR codes on the Garden Growth Report linking to short videos of garden activities

### Family Participation Opportunities

**Low-Barrier (Anytime):**
- Send in seeds, soil, or containers from home
- Share a family recipe using garden vegetables
- Complete the "Ask Me About" conversation and send a response (written, drawn, or recorded)
- Read a garden-themed book together at home

**Moderate Involvement:**
- Attend a Saturday Family Garden Day (quarterly)
- Volunteer for garden maintenance (weeding, watering during breaks)
- Share cultural gardening knowledge or food traditions with the class
- Attend student-led portfolio conferences (twice yearly)

**High Involvement:**
- Join the Garden Advisory Committee (meets quarterly)
- Lead a cooking demonstration using garden produce
- Mentor a student during garden time
- Help coordinate the end-of-year Garden Celebration

### Family Garden Days

Quarterly events designed to welcome families into the garden:

**Format (2 hours, Saturday morning):**

| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 9:00-9:15 | Welcome, coffee, and garden snacks |
| 9:15-9:45 | Guided garden tour led by students |
| 9:45-10:15 | Family planting activity (each family plants something to take home and something for the school garden) |
| 10:15-10:45 | Garden cooking demonstration and tasting |
| 10:45-11:00 | Closing circle: sharing, questions, and next steps |

**Trauma-Informed Family Engagement Principles:**
- Never require family participation for student success or grades
- Provide multiple ways to participate (in-person, at home, digitally)
- Acknowledge that some families may have had negative school experiences
- Start with strengths: "What does your family know about growing food?"
- Create welcoming, non-judgmental environments with food, childcare, and comfortable spaces
- Translate materials into home languages
- Respect cultural differences in communication styles and family structures
- Follow up personally with families who don't attend -- not to pressure, but to offer alternative connections

## Detailed Garden Activity Guide

### Seasonal Garden Calendar for K-2

#### Fall (August-November)

| Week | Garden Activity | Academic Connection | SEL Connection |
|------|----------------|--------------------|--------------|
| 1-2 | Garden orientation and safety; explore beds; plant cool-weather lettuce and spinach seeds | Observation skills; counting seeds; following multi-step directions | Establishing routines; learning garden agreements; building trust |
| 3-4 | Soil exploration: texture, color, organisms; begin compost bin | Sorting and classifying; science vocabulary; recording observations | Caring for living things; teamwork during soil investigation |
| 5-6 | Plant fall bulbs (tulips, daffodils) for spring; measure and record | Measurement; prediction (What will these look like in spring?); counting | Patience and delayed gratification; planning for the future |
| 7-8 | Harvest first lettuce and spinach; weigh and count harvest | Addition; measurement; data collection | Celebration of effort; sharing harvest with others |
| 9-10 | Prepare garden for winter: mulch beds, bring in container plants | Sequencing; discussing seasonal changes; comparing fall to summer | Taking responsibility; caring for things even when it's hard |
| 11-12 | Indoor gardening begins: start herb windowsill garden | Observation of growth in different conditions; comparing indoor vs outdoor | Adaptability; finding new ways to grow |

#### Winter (December-February)

| Week | Garden Activity | Academic Connection | SEL Connection |
|------|----------------|--------------------|--------------|
| 13-14 | Indoor seed starting: plan spring garden; choose varieties from catalogs | Reading informational text; making decisions; budgeting seeds | Decision-making; democratic process (voting on what to plant) |
| 15-16 | Herb harvest and cooking: make herb butter or tea from windowsill herbs | Measurement (cups, teaspoons); following recipes; science of taste | Sharing; gratitude; connecting garden to daily life |
| 17-18 | Build bird feeders; begin winter bird count | Counting; data collection; animal needs in winter | Empathy for animals; community responsibility |
| 19-20 | Vermiculture: build a classroom worm bin | Decomposition science; life cycle of worms; measuring food scraps | Overcoming "yuck" responses; appreciating all organisms |
| 21-22 | Seed science: germination experiments (light vs dark, warm vs cold) | Scientific investigation; prediction; recording data | Hypothesis testing; accepting unexpected results |
| 23-24 | Plan spring garden layout on grid paper; order seeds | Geometry; spatial reasoning; area (2nd grade); budgeting | Collaborative planning; compromise; anticipation |

#### Spring (March-May)

| Week | Garden Activity | Academic Connection | SEL Connection |
|------|----------------|--------------------|--------------|
| 25-26 | Transplant indoor seedlings; direct sow warm-weather seeds | Life cycle stages; measurement of seedling growth; counting | Nurturing; responsibility; excitement about growth |
| 27-28 | Pollinator garden: plant wildflowers to attract bees and butterflies | Insect life cycles; plant-animal relationships; classification | Interdependence; appreciating diversity |
| 29-30 | Growth measurement sprint: measure all plants; update data charts | Measurement; graphing; comparing; analyzing data | Persistence in data collection; pride in scientific work |
| 31-32 | Discover spring bulbs emerging; compare to fall predictions | Connecting past learning to present; time concepts | Delayed gratification payoff; memory and reflection |
| 33-34 | Garden biodiversity survey: count and classify organisms | Classification; counting; data tables; informational writing | Respect for all living things; careful observation |
| 35-36 | End-of-year harvest celebration and Garden Gallery portfolio presentations | All academic domains; presentation skills; data summary | Celebration; sharing with community; pride in growth |

### Garden Safety Guidelines for K-2

**Before Every Garden Session:**
- Check garden for hazards: broken glass, sharp tools left out, standing water, wasp nests
- Verify all children have appropriate footwear (closed-toe shoes required)
- Apply sunscreen and provide hats during warm months
- Review garden safety rules with class (keep consistent):
  1. Walk in the garden (no running)
  2. Only touch plants your teacher says are safe
  3. Wash hands after touching soil, before eating
  4. Carry tools pointing down
  5. Stay where your teacher can see you
  6. If you see something you're not sure about, ask first

**Allergy and Medical Considerations:**
- Maintain an allergy list for all students (bee stings, plant allergies, skin sensitivities)
- Keep first aid kit in the garden at all times
- Ensure EpiPens are accessible during outdoor time for students with known anaphylaxis risk
- Avoid planting known allergens (confirm with families)
- Provide garden gloves for students with skin sensitivities
- Have sunscreen and shade structures for students with sun sensitivity

**Tool Safety:**
- Use only child-sized, age-appropriate tools
- Demonstrate proper use before each new tool is introduced
- One-to-one adult supervision for any sharp tools (pruners used only by adults at K-2 level)
- Tools have designated storage locations; count tools at the start and end of each session
- Establish a "tools down" signal (bell, chime, or hand signal) for immediate stops

### Seasonal Garden Recipes for K-2

Simple, safe recipes that use garden produce and reinforce academic skills:

**Fall: Rainbow Salad**
- Ingredients: Lettuce, cherry tomatoes, shredded carrots, purple cabbage
- Math: Count ingredients; measure dressing (1 tablespoon oil, 1 teaspoon vinegar); fractions (half a tomato)
- Science: Discuss plant parts on the plate (leaves, fruit, root)
- SEL: Sharing a meal; trying new foods; expressing preferences respectfully

**Winter: Herbed Butter**
- Ingredients: Softened butter, fresh herbs from windowsill garden (chives, parsley, dill)
- Math: Measure 1 cup butter, 2 tablespoons herbs; divide into portions
- Science: Observe how herbs change the butter (smell, color, taste); states of matter (solid butter vs melted)
- SEL: Patience (letting butter soften); pride in growing the herbs themselves

**Spring: Garden Smoothie**
- Ingredients: Strawberries (from garden if available), banana, spinach from garden, yogurt
- Math: Measure ingredients in cups; count strawberries; follow sequential steps
- Science: Discuss nutrients; observe how blending changes texture; predict what it will taste like
- SEL: Courage to try something new (spinach in a smoothie!); celebrating the harvest

## RootGuide AI Tutor: K-2 Adaptation

### Interaction Design for Young Learners

The RootGuide AI tutor adapts its behavior for K-2 students in the following ways:

**Language Level:**
- Short sentences (5-10 words average)
- High-frequency vocabulary with garden context
- One question at a time (never compound questions)
- Repetition of key terms with visual supports
- Avoidance of idioms and abstract language

**Pacing:**
- Longer wait times (10-15 seconds for responses)
- Frequent check-ins: "Are you ready for the next question?"
- Breaks offered proactively: "Would you like to take a garden break?"
- Sessions limited to 10-15 minutes for K, 15-20 minutes for 1st-2nd

**Scaffolding:**
- Every problem begins with a concrete context (garden-based)
- Manipulative prompts: "Can you use your seeds to show me?"
- Drawing encouraged as a problem-solving strategy: "Can you draw what you're thinking?"
- Success-oriented: Start with tasks the child can do, then gradually increase difficulty
- Error response is always warm: "Hmm, let's think about that together. Your brain is doing hard work!"

**Regulation Monitoring:**
- Detects long pauses (may indicate frustration, confusion, or dysregulation)
- Detects rapid incorrect responses (may indicate rushing or anxiety)
- Detects session abandonment (may indicate overwhelm)
- Responds with regulation check-ins: "How are you feeling right now? Blooming, growing, or wilting?"
- Offers transition to calm corner activities when dysregulation is detected

### Sample RootGuide Interaction (1st Grade Math)

```
RootGuide: Hi there, little gardener! How are you feeling today?
           Pick one: Blooming | Growing | Wilting

Student:   Growing

RootGuide: A growing day -- that's a great place to be!
           Let's think about our garden today.

           We planted 6 bean seeds on Monday.
           On Tuesday, we planted 4 more.
           How many bean seeds did we plant in all?

           Can you figure it out?

Student:   9

RootGuide: You're close! Let's check together.
           Can you hold up 6 fingers for Monday's seeds?
           Now hold up 4 more for Tuesday's seeds.
           How many fingers are up now?

Student:   10

RootGuide: That's it! 6 and 4 makes 10.
           You checked your work -- that's what great thinkers do!

           Now here's a garden challenge:
           If 3 of those seeds didn't sprout,
           how many plants would we have?

Student:   7

RootGuide: Yes! 10 seeds minus 3 that didn't sprout = 7 plants.
           Your brain is growing like our bean plants!

           Ready for another one, or would you like a garden break?
```

## Year-End Celebrations

### The Garden Gallery

An end-of-year event that celebrates student growth, shares the garden with the community, and provides families with a full picture of their child's learning:

**Event Structure:**

| Station | Description | Student Role |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **Portfolio Station** | Student portfolios displayed on tables with family seating | Student presents their portfolio to their family |
| **Garden Tour** | Guided walk through the school garden | Student guides their family, explaining what they planted and learned |
| **Math Station** | Interactive garden math activities (counting, measuring, graphing) | Student demonstrates a math skill for their family |
| **Science Station** | Seed dissection, soil testing, and plant observation activities | Student leads a mini-investigation with their family |
| **Cooking Station** | Simple garden recipe preparation | Student and family make a garden snack together |
| **Growth Timeline** | Year-long photo display showing garden and student growth | Students find themselves in photos and narrate the story |
| **Seed Library** | Seeds saved from this year's garden, packaged by students | Students give families seeds to grow at home over the summer |

**Inclusion Practices:**
- Invite all family configurations without assumption (parents, grandparents, foster families, group home staff)
- Provide food that accommodates common dietary restrictions
- Offer multiple time options or a virtual tour for families who cannot attend
- Translate signage and key documents into the most common home languages
- Ensure physical accessibility for all garden stations
- Have quiet spaces available for families and children who become overwhelmed
