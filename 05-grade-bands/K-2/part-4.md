# K-2 Grade Band: Social-Emotional Learning & Regulation

## Overview

Social-emotional learning (SEL) in the K-2 RootWork curriculum is not a separate subject or a weekly lesson -- it is the foundation upon which all learning is built. The 5Rs Framework ensures that every academic session begins with emotional safety (Root), includes regulation support (Regulate), builds metacognitive awareness (Reflect), reframes setbacks (Restore), and connects learning to identity and community (Reconnect). For young children, particularly those carrying the weight of adverse childhood experiences, this integrated approach is essential: a child who does not feel safe cannot learn, and a child who cannot regulate cannot think.

The garden provides a uniquely powerful context for SEL development. It is a living metaphor for growth, patience, resilience, and interdependence. Children learn that plants need specific conditions to thrive -- sunlight, water, nutrients, and care -- and through guided reflection, they begin to understand that they too need specific conditions to grow. This metaphorical thinking, introduced gently at the K-2 level, becomes a framework for self-understanding that deepens across all grade bands.

## CASEL Framework Alignment

The RootWork K-2 SEL curriculum aligns with the five CASEL (Collaborative for Academic, Social, and Emotional Learning) competency areas, implemented through garden-based activities and 5Rs integration:

### 1. Self-Awareness

**Definition:** Recognizing one's own emotions, thoughts, and values and their influence on behavior.

**K-2 Garden Integration:**

| Skill | Activity | 5Rs Connection |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| Identifying emotions | Daily emotion check-in using "garden weather" cards (sunny = happy, rainy = sad, cloudy = unsure, stormy = angry, snowy = tired) | Root Phase |
| Recognizing personal strengths | "Garden Strengths" board: each student is represented by a flower with petals showing their strengths ("I am kind," "I am a good listener," "I try hard") | Reconnect Phase |
| Self-confidence | Watching a plant they personally cared for grow and thrive; "I grew that!" | Reconnect Phase |
| Accurate self-perception | Growth journals document learning over time, making growth visible: "Look how much your writing has changed since September!" | Reconnect Phase |
| Emotional vocabulary | Building a feeling word wall using garden metaphors: wilting (sad), blooming (happy), thorny (frustrated), dormant (tired), growing (learning) | Root/Regulate |

**Detailed Activity: Garden Feelings Check-In**

Materials: Set of illustrated cards showing plants in various states (blooming, wilting, storm-tossed, dormant, sprouting, strong and tall)

Procedure:
1. Each morning during Root phase, students choose a plant card that matches their feeling
2. Share with the class or a partner: "I feel like the blooming flower today because I'm happy to be here" or "I feel like the wilting plant because I'm tired"
3. Teacher responds with validation: "Thank you for sharing. Our garden has room for all kinds of plants, and they all matter"
4. No judgment or problem-solving in the moment -- simply acknowledging
5. Teacher notes any students consistently choosing "storm" or "wilting" cards for individual follow-up

**Developmental Progression:**
- K: Choose from 4 simple cards; teacher helps label the feeling
- 1st: Choose from 8 cards; name the feeling independently; use "I feel ___ because ___" structure
- 2nd: Choose or draw their own; journal about why; suggest what might help them feel better

### 2. Self-Management

**Definition:** Managing one's own emotions, thoughts, and behaviors effectively in different situations.

**K-2 Garden Integration:**

| Skill | Activity | 5Rs Connection |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| Impulse control | Waiting for seeds to germinate teaches patience; "We can't make it grow faster by digging it up!" | Reflect/Regulate |
| Stress management | Garden-based calm-down strategies (digging, watering, sensory exploration) | Regulate Phase |
| Self-discipline | Daily garden care routines (watering on schedule, not picking unripe fruit) | Root Phase |
| Goal setting | "My plant goal" and "My learning goal" for the week | Root/Reconnect |
| Organizational skills | Keeping growth journals organized; putting tools away properly | Reflect Phase |

**Detailed Activity: The Patience Garden**

This ongoing project teaches delayed gratification through gardening:

*Week 1:* Plant three types of seeds with different germination times:
- Radishes (fast: 3-5 days to sprout)
- Beans (medium: 7-10 days to sprout)
- Carrots (slow: 14-21 days to sprout)

*Daily:* Students check all three pots. Record observations. Practice patience language: "The carrots aren't ready yet, and that's okay. Some things take longer. We just keep caring for them."

*Guided Reflection:* "Are you sometimes like the radish -- quick to do things? Sometimes like the carrot -- taking more time? Both are good! Different plants grow at different speeds, and so do people."

*Extension:* "When is patience hard for you at school? At home? What helps you wait?"

**Detailed Activity: Calm Corner Garden Tools**

The classroom calm corner includes garden-based regulation tools:

1. **Sensory Soil Box:** A bin of clean, dry potting soil with small garden tools. Students can dig, rake, and smooth the soil as a calming activity.
2. **Herb Smelling Station:** Small pots of lavender, mint, rosemary, and chamomile. Students can smell herbs as a grounding technique. ("Take three slow breaths with the lavender.")
3. **Seed Sorting Tray:** A divided tray with different types of seeds. Sorting by type, size, or color is a focusing, calming activity.
4. **Smooth Stone Collection:** River stones from the garden labeled with calming strategies: "breathe," "count to 10," "squeeze and release," "name 5 things you see."
5. **Growth Mindset Plants:** Two small plants -- one thriving, one that was damaged and is regrowing. "Even when things break, they can grow back. It takes time and care."

### 3. Social Awareness

**Definition:** Understanding the perspectives of and empathizing with others, including those from diverse backgrounds and cultures.

**K-2 Garden Integration:**

| Skill | Activity | 5Rs Connection |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| Perspective-taking | "How do you think the plant feels when it gets water after a hot day?" (building toward human empathy through plant anthropomorphism) | Reflect Phase |
| Empathy | Caring for living things develops empathy: "This plant depends on us!" | Reconnect Phase |
| Appreciating diversity | Growing diverse plants from different cultures; "Every garden is better with many different kinds of plants" | Reconnect Phase |
| Respect for others | Shared garden responsibilities; respecting others' plants and work | Root Phase |
| Understanding social norms | Garden rules co-created by the class: "In our garden, we..." | Root Phase |

**Detailed Activity: The Community Garden**

Each student has a personal plant AND a section of the community garden:

*Personal Plant:* "This is yours to care for. You decide when to water it, what to name it, and where to place it."

*Community Garden:* "This belongs to all of us. We all have to agree on what to plant and how to care for it."

*Guided Discussions:*
- "Jayden wants to plant sunflowers, but Amara wants to plant pumpkins. How can we solve this?"
- "Someone forgot to water the community garden yesterday and some plants are drooping. How can we help without blaming?"
- "We grew so many tomatoes! There are more than we can eat. What should we do with the extras?" (Introduction to sharing with the community)

### 4. Relationship Skills

**Definition:** Establishing and maintaining healthy and supportive relationships.

**K-2 Garden Integration:**

| Skill | Activity | 5Rs Connection |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| Communication | Partner sharing routines; garden buddy conversations | Reflect Phase |
| Cooperation | Shared garden tasks requiring teamwork (carrying heavy watering cans, building a trellis) | Reflect/Reconnect |
| Conflict resolution | Using "garden agreements" to resolve disputes about shared resources | Regulate/Restore |
| Seeking help | "It's okay to ask for help. Even plants need support -- that's why we use stakes and trellises!" | Restore Phase |
| Teamwork | Group planting projects; partner measurement activities | Reflect Phase |

**Detailed Activity: Garden Buddies**

Students are paired as "garden buddies" for 4-6 week rotations:

*Responsibilities:*
- Check in with each other during Root phase: "How are you feeling today?"
- Work together during garden time: one holds, one digs; one measures, one records
- Share observations during Reflect phase: "Turn to your garden buddy and share"
- Celebrate each other during Reconnect phase: "Tell your garden buddy one thing they did well today"

*Teaching Points:*
- "A good garden buddy listens without interrupting"
- "A good garden buddy helps without taking over"
- "A good garden buddy says kind things about your ideas, even if they have a different idea"
- "If you and your garden buddy disagree, use your words: 'I see it differently. Can I share my idea?'"

*Conflict Resolution Protocol (Garden Agreements):*
1. **Stop** -- "Take a deep breath, like smelling a flower"
2. **Say** -- "I feel ___ when ___ because ___"
3. **Listen** -- "Now it's your buddy's turn to share"
4. **Solve** -- "What can we both agree on?" or "Let's ask our teacher gardener for help"

### 5. Responsible Decision-Making

**Definition:** Making caring and constructive choices about personal behavior and social interactions.

**K-2 Garden Integration:**

| Skill | Activity | 5Rs Connection |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| Identifying problems | "Our tomato plants are drooping. What could be wrong?" | Reflect Phase |
| Analyzing situations | "We only have 1 watering can and 3 groups. What should we do?" | Reflect Phase |
| Solving problems | Brainstorming solutions as a class; trying different approaches | Reflect/Restore |
| Evaluating consequences | "If we pick all the flowers now, will there be any for next week?" | Reconnect Phase |
| Ethical responsibility | "Should we use chemicals to kill the bugs, or is there a natural way?" | Reconnect Phase |

**Detailed Activity: Garden Dilemmas**

Present age-appropriate ethical dilemmas connected to the garden:

*Dilemma 1:* "A bird is eating our strawberries. Should we put up a net to keep the birds out? But the birds also eat the bugs that hurt our plants. What should we do?"

*Dilemma 2:* "Your neighbor's garden doesn't have any vegetables growing. Your garden has more tomatoes than you can eat. What could you do?"

*Dilemma 3:* "It's your turn to water the garden, but you really want to go play on the swings. What do you do? Why?"

*Process:* Students discuss in pairs, then share with the class. Teacher facilitates without providing "the right answer," helping students consider multiple perspectives and consequences.

## Regulation Strategies: Detailed Toolkit

### Breathing Exercises for K-2

| Exercise | Instructions | Visual/Prop | When to Use |
|----------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| **Flower Breathing** | Breathe in through nose (smell the flower), out through mouth (blow the petals) | Real or paper flower | Transition times, after recess |
| **Balloon Breathing** | Hands on belly. Breathe in -- belly expands like a balloon. Breathe out -- balloon deflates. | Colored balloons for visual | Beginning of sessions, when anxious |
| **Snake Breathing** | Breathe in for 4 counts, breathe out with a long "sssssss" sound | Snake puppet | When silly/hyperactive energy |
| **Bunny Breathing** | Three quick sniffs in through the nose, one long exhale out | Bunny ear headband | Quick calming between activities |
| **Tree Breathing** | Stand tall like a tree. Breathe in, arms rise like branches. Breathe out, arms lower. | Stand near a tree in the garden | Grounding after distress |
| **Garden Hose** | Breathe in to fill up the hose. Breathe out slowly like water flowing from the hose to water the plants. | A coiled garden hose | Slowing down rushed energy |

### Movement-Based Regulation

**Garden Yoga Sequence (5 minutes):**
1. **Seed Pose:** Curl up small on the ground (child's pose). "You are a tiny seed in the soil."
2. **Sprout:** Slowly begin to rise. "You feel the warmth of the sun. Your stem is pushing through the soil."
3. **Seedling:** Kneel with arms beginning to reach up. "Your leaves are starting to open."
4. **Growing Plant:** Stand with arms at sides. "You're growing taller and stronger each day."
5. **Full Bloom:** Stand tall, arms wide open. "You've bloomed! Your petals are open to the sun."
6. **Tree:** Stand on one foot, arms up like branches (tree pose). "You are a strong, tall tree with deep roots."
7. **Butterfly:** Sit with feet together, knees out, flapping gently. "A butterfly has come to visit your garden."
8. **Rest:** Lie down flat (savasana). "The garden is quiet. Everything is peaceful."

**Heavy Work Activities (for hyperarousal/high energy):**
- Digging in the garden with shovels
- Carrying bags of soil or watering cans
- Pushing a wheelbarrow
- Pulling weeds (with gloves)
- Kneading soil or clay
- Stacking pots or bricks

**Slow, Rhythmic Activities (for hypoarousal/low energy):**
- Gentle watering with a spray bottle
- Raking smooth patterns in soil
- Sorting seeds by type
- Slow garden walk with observation pauses
- Drawing in the growth journal
- Tracing leaf veins with a finger

### Sensory Regulation Tools

**Sensory Profile Matching:**

Different students have different sensory needs. The garden provides diverse sensory inputs:

| Sensory Need | Garden Strategy |
|-------------|----------------|
| **Tactile seeking** | Hands in soil, texture exploration (smooth stones, rough bark, soft petals), water play |
| **Tactile avoiding** | Garden gloves available, tools with smooth handles, option to observe rather than touch |
| **Visual seeking** | Colorful flowers, butterfly watching, kaleidoscope of garden colors |
| **Visual overwhelm** | Shaded garden areas, focus tools (cardboard tube "binoculars" to narrow visual field) |
| **Auditory seeking** | Wind chimes in the garden, listening walks, birdsong identification |
| **Auditory overwhelm** | Noise-canceling headphones during group activities, quiet garden zone |
| **Proprioceptive seeking** | Digging, carrying, pushing, pulling -- heavy garden work |
| **Vestibular seeking** | Bending, reaching, walking uneven garden paths |
| **Olfactory** | Herb smelling station (lavender for calm, mint for alertness, rosemary for focus) |

## Trauma-Responsive Classroom Practices

### Daily Rhythms and Rituals

Predictability is the cornerstone of trauma-informed practice for young children. The K-2 RootWork classroom follows consistent daily rhythms:

**Morning Arrival Ritual:**
1. Greet each child by name at the door
2. Child checks the visual schedule for the day
3. Child waters their personal plant (a calming, purposeful transition activity)
4. Child selects a feeling card and places it on the class check-in board
5. Free exploration of the calm corner, garden station, or journal table until the session begins

**Transition Rituals:**
- Transitions are signaled with the same cue each time (a specific chime, song, or hand signal)
- Two-minute warnings before transitions: "In two minutes, we'll be cleaning up our garden tools"
- Transition activities that bridge spaces: "Carry your seed cup carefully to the garden table" (purposeful movement)
- Acknowledgment that transitions are hard: "I know it's hard to stop when you're having fun. We'll come back to this tomorrow."

**Closing Ritual:**
1. Return all tools to their labeled places
2. Water the community garden
3. Quick partner share: "One thing I'm taking home in my brain today"
4. Closing song or chant
5. Individual farewell: "See you tomorrow, [name]. Your plant will be waiting for you."

### Responding to Dysregulation

**Hyperarousal Signs in K-2:**
- Running, climbing at inappropriate times
- Hitting, pushing, throwing objects
- Loud, rapid speech; difficulty with volume control
- Inability to sit still; constant movement
- Crying that escalates quickly

**Response Protocol:**
1. **Co-regulate first:** Move close (not looming over). Lower your voice. Slow your movements. "I'm right here. You're safe."
2. **Offer choices:** "Would you like to go to the calm corner or take a garden walk with me?"
3. **Reduce demands:** Remove academic expectations temporarily. "We can do math later. Right now, let's just breathe together."
4. **Physical regulation:** Offer heavy work if appropriate: "Want to help me carry the watering can?" Or offer sensory input: "Would you like to hold the smooth stone?"
5. **Name and validate:** When the child is calmer: "Your body was feeling really big feelings. That happens to everyone sometimes. You did a good job finding your calm."

**Hypoarousal Signs in K-2:**
- Staring blankly; "zoning out"
- Head on desk; extreme fatigue
- Minimal response to questions
- Withdrawing from peers
- Appearing "frozen" or very compliant

**Response Protocol:**
1. **Gentle alerting:** Offer a choice that introduces gentle stimulation: "Would you like a drink of water?" "Want to help me water the plants?"
2. **Sensory activation:** Offer a crunchy snack, a cool washcloth, a mint leaf to smell, or a brief walk outside
3. **Proximity without pressure:** Sit near the child without requiring engagement. "I'll be right here when you're ready."
4. **Small, achievable task:** "Can you hand me the blue cup?" -- Success on a small task can help reactivate engagement
5. **Document:** Note the episode for pattern tracking. Persistent hypoarousal may indicate a need for additional support.

### Language Practices

**Use:**
- "I notice that..." instead of "Why are you...?"
- "Your body is telling me..." instead of "You're being..."
- "Let's try..." instead of "You need to stop..."
- "In our garden, we..." instead of "Don't..."
- "That was hard. What can we try differently?" instead of "That was wrong"
- "Your brain is growing right now!" instead of "That's incorrect"
- "Everyone needs help sometimes, even the strongest trees" instead of shaming a child for asking for help

**Avoid:**
- Public correction or call-outs
- Removing privileges related to the garden (the garden should always be a safe space)
- Comparison between students: "Why can't you be like..."
- Labeling children: "troublemaker," "the difficult one," "the quiet one"
- Requiring verbal explanation for behavior during dysregulation (they cannot access language when dysregulated)
- Forced eye contact or physical proximity (both can be triggering for trauma-affected children)

**Regulation Tracking:**
- Daily regulation level at start and end of session (visual analog scale: drooping plant → standing plant → blooming plant)
- Number of calm corner visits per week (tracking trend, not as a behavior metric)
- Duration and intensity of dysregulation episodes (for IEP documentation if applicable)
- Strategies used and their effectiveness (inform future support planning)
