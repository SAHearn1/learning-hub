# 3-5 Reflect Phase: Developing Metacognition

## Overview

The Reflect phase is where core academic learning takes place. For grades 3-5, this phase introduces the TRACE protocol with developmental scaffolding and begins building students' capacity to name and use reasoning moves explicitly. Academic content is delivered through garden-based contexts whenever possible, creating natural connections between abstract concepts and lived experience. Students at this level are transitioning from concrete operational to early formal operational thinking, making this an ideal window for introducing metacognitive language -- the ability to think about their own thinking.

**Core Question**: "What am I thinking, and why?"
**Duration**: 20-35 minutes (the longest phase in each session)
**Garden Metaphor**: Photosynthesis -- actively converting inputs (sunlight, water, nutrients) into growth.

## The TRACE Protocol: 3-5 Scaffolded Implementation

At the 3-5 level, the TRACE protocol is introduced gradually over the first quarter and then used consistently throughout the year. Each step is explicitly taught, modeled, and practiced before students are expected to use it independently.

### Introduction Sequence (First 6 Weeks)

| Week | TRACE Step Introduced | Educator Action | Student Expectation |
|------|----------------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| 1-2 | **Think** | Model "noticing" aloud; pause before every problem | Observe before answering; share one observation |
| 3 | **Reason** | Model strategy selection; think aloud about approach | Name a strategy before solving |
| 4 | **Articulate** | Ask "talk me through it" after every response | Explain reasoning in own words |
| 5 | **Check** | Model verification; show how to check work differently | Attempt one check strategy per problem |
| 6 | **Extend** | Ask "what if" questions; connect to garden | Respond to one extension prompt per session |

### TRACE Prompt Cards

Students receive a laminated TRACE card for their desk or journal. The card uses student-friendly language:

| Step | Adult Language | 3-5 Student Language | Visual Cue |
|------|---------------|---------------------|------------|
| **Think** | "What do you notice first?" | "What do I see?" | Eye icon |
| **Reason** | "What's your plan of attack?" | "What's my plan?" | Lightbulb icon |
| **Articulate** | "Talk me through your thinking." | "How can I explain it?" | Speech bubble icon |
| **Check** | "How do you know you're right?" | "Am I sure?" | Magnifying glass icon |
| **Extend** | "What else could you do with this?" | "Where else does this go?" | Arrow icon |

### Educator Language Models for TRACE

- **Think:** "Before we solve anything, let's just notice. What do you see in this problem? What information is there?"
- **Reason:** "Now that you've noticed the important parts, what's your plan? How are you going to tackle this?"
- **Articulate:** "You got an answer -- now help me understand. Pretend I'm a 2nd grader. Explain it so I could follow along."
- **Check:** "I'm not going to tell you if you're right yet. How can you check for yourself?"
- **Extend:** "Nice work. Now what if I changed this number? What if this happened in our garden?"

## Beginning Reasoning Moves

The seven reasoning moves are introduced at the 3-5 level using garden-based anchor activities. Students learn to name the moves, recognize them in practice, and begin deploying them with prompting.

### Reasoning Move Anchor Lessons

| Reasoning Move | Anchor Activity | Garden Connection | Student-Friendly Definition |
|---------------|-----------------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| **DECOMPOSE** | Break a recipe into individual ingredients and steps | Separating a seed mix into individual seed types | "Break it into smaller pieces" |
| **COMPARE** | Venn diagram of two garden plants | Comparing growth of plants in sun vs. shade | "What's the same? What's different?" |
| **QUESTION** | "I wonder..." wall in the garden | "Why do some seeds sprout faster?" | "Ask why and what if" |
| **VERIFY** | Measure the same plant two different ways | Count seeds by 2s, then by 5s -- same total? | "Check it another way" |
| **GENERALIZE** | Track plant growth data over weeks; find the pattern | "All our tomato plants grew faster after we added compost" | "Find the pattern or rule" |
| **TRANSFORM** | Show the same data as a table, then a bar graph | Draw a garden plot layout, then build a 3D model | "Show it a different way" |
| **CONNECT** | Link garden measurements to classroom math | "The fractions we learned in math help us divide garden beds" | "Link it to something else" |

### Celebrating Reasoning Move Use

When students use reasoning moves, the educator names them explicitly:

- "You just used DECOMPOSE -- you broke that big problem into smaller steps. That's what mathematicians do."
- "Great QUESTIONING! Scientists ask exactly those kinds of 'what if' questions."
- "You VERIFIED your answer by checking it a different way. That takes real discipline."

## Garden Math: Standards-Aligned Activities

### Fractions in the Garden (Grade 3-4)

**Activity: "Fair Shares in the Garden Bed"**
Students divide garden beds into equal sections for different crops. Each section represents a fraction of the whole bed.

1. **Think:** "Our raised bed is 8 feet long. We want to plant 4 different vegetables in equal sections. What do you notice?" (DECOMPOSE the bed into parts.)
2. **Reason:** "How long will each section be? What fraction of the bed is each section?"
3. **Articulate:** "Explain to your partner how you figured out the size of each section."
4. **Check:** "Can you VERIFY by adding all the sections back together? Do they equal the whole bed?"
5. **Extend:** "What if we wanted to give tomatoes twice as much space as the other plants? What fractions would we use then?"

**Standards Alignment:**
- CCSS.Math.Content.3.NF.A.1: Understand a fraction 1/b as the quantity formed by 1 part when a whole is partitioned into b equal parts
- GSE MGSE3.NF.1: Understand a fraction 1/b as the quantity formed by 1 part when a whole is partitioned into b equal parts
- CCSS.Math.Content.4.NF.B.3: Understand a fraction a/b with a > 1 as a sum of fractions 1/b

### Measurement and Data (Grade 3-5)

**Activity: "Garden Growth Tracker"**
Students measure their plants weekly and record data in tables and graphs.

1. Students measure plant height in centimeters using rulers. (VERIFY: Measure twice to confirm.)
2. Record measurements in a data table in their growth journal.
3. Create a line plot or bar graph showing growth over time. (TRANSFORM: Convert table data to visual representation.)
4. Analyze: "Which plant grew the most this week? The least?" (COMPARE across data points.)
5. Predict: "Based on the pattern, how tall will your plant be next week?" (GENERALIZE from data to prediction.)

**Standards Alignment:**
- CCSS.Math.Content.3.MD.B.4: Generate measurement data by measuring lengths using rulers marked with halves and fourths of an inch
- CCSS.Math.Content.4.MD.A.1: Know relative sizes of measurement units within one system of units
- CCSS.Math.Content.5.MD.B.2: Make a line plot to display a data set of measurements in fractions of a unit
- GSE MGSE3.MD.4, MGSE4.MD.1, MGSE5.MD.2

### Multiplication and Area (Grade 3-4)

**Activity: "Designing Garden Beds"**
Students calculate the area of rectangular garden beds and design optimal layouts.

1. **Think:** "Our new garden space is 12 feet by 8 feet. What do you notice about this problem?"
2. **Reason:** "How could we figure out the total area? What operation might help?" (CONNECT to array models used in classroom math.)
3. **Articulate:** "Draw the garden bed on grid paper and explain how you found the area."
4. **Check:** "Can you find the area a different way -- maybe by DECOMPOSING the rectangle into smaller pieces?"
5. **Extend:** "If each plant needs 2 square feet of space, how many plants can we fit?" (TRANSFORM the area problem into a division problem.)

**Standards Alignment:**
- CCSS.Math.Content.3.MD.C.7: Relate area to the operations of multiplication and addition
- GSE MGSE3.MD.7: Relate area to the operations of multiplication and addition

## Garden Science: Standards-Aligned Activities

### Plant Life Cycles (Grade 3)

**Activity: "Seed to Table: The Full Journey"**
Students observe and document the complete life cycle of a fast-growing plant (radishes, lettuce, or beans) over 6-8 weeks.

1. **Week 1-2:** Plant seeds. Observe and sketch daily. Record germination date. (QUESTION: "What conditions does this seed need to sprout?")
2. **Week 3-4:** Measure and document seedling growth. Compare plants in different conditions. (COMPARE: sun vs. shade, watered vs. dry.)
3. **Week 5-6:** Observe flowering and fruiting. Dissect a flower to identify parts. (DECOMPOSE: Identify sepals, petals, stamens, pistil.)
4. **Week 7-8:** Harvest. Collect seeds for next planting. Discuss the cycle. (GENERALIZE: "What pattern do all plant life cycles follow?")

**Standards Alignment:**
- NGSS 3-LS1-1: Develop models to describe that organisms have unique and diverse life cycles
- GSE S3L1.a: Classify organisms into groups based on similarities and differences

### Ecosystems and Interdependence (Grade 5)

**Activity: "Our Garden Ecosystem Web"**
Students map the relationships between organisms in the school garden.

1. Observe and list all living things found in the garden (plants, insects, worms, birds, fungi).
2. Research what each organism eats and what eats it. (QUESTION: "What would happen if we removed the earthworms?")
3. Create a food web diagram. (TRANSFORM: Convert a list into a web diagram.)
4. Discuss: "How does each organism help the garden grow?" (CONNECT: Link to nutrient cycling, pollination, decomposition.)
5. Predict: "If we added a new type of plant, how might the ecosystem change?" (GENERALIZE from observed patterns to predictions.)

**Standards Alignment:**
- NGSS 5-LS2-1: Develop a model to describe the movement of matter among plants, animals, decomposers, and the environment
- GSE S5L4.a: Identify the roles of organisms and the flow of energy within an ecosystem

## Writing Through Garden Journals

### Observation Writing (All Grades 3-5)

Students write detailed observations in their growth journals, building scientific and descriptive writing skills simultaneously.

**Sentence Starters for Developing Writers:**
- "Today I observed that..."
- "I noticed a change in... The change was..."
- "I predict that... because I observed..."
- "This reminds me of... because..."

**Standards Alignment:**
- CCSS.ELA-Literacy.W.3.2: Write informative/explanatory texts to examine a topic and convey ideas
- CCSS.ELA-Literacy.W.4.2: Write informative/explanatory texts to examine a topic and convey ideas and information clearly
- GSE ELAGSE3W2, ELAGSE4W2, ELAGSE5W2

### Procedural Writing (Grade 4-5)

Students write step-by-step instructions for garden tasks (planting seeds, transplanting seedlings, preparing soil).

**TRACE Application:**
- **Think:** "What does someone need to know to plant a seed?"
- **Reason:** "What order should the steps go in? Why?"
- **Articulate:** "Write the instructions so clearly that someone who has never gardened could follow them."
- **Check:** "Have a partner try to follow your instructions exactly. Did they work?"
- **Extend:** "What would change if you were planting a different type of seed?"

## Monitoring During Reflect

Even during active learning, the educator continuously monitors for dysregulation:

- Watch for error cascades (sudden increase in mistakes after a period of success)
- Notice body language shifts (slouching, fidgeting, avoidance of eye contact)
- Listen for negative self-talk ("This is stupid," "I can't do this," "I'm dumb")
- Track engagement patterns (decreasing response length, off-topic deflection)

If dysregulation is detected, transition to the Regulate phase without shame: "I can see your brain is working really hard. Want to take a quick break in the garden?"

## Assessment Indicators

| Indicator | Developing (1) | Approaching (2) | Proficient (3) |
|-----------|----------------|------------------|-----------------|
| **TRACE engagement** | Needs prompting at each step | Follows TRACE with occasional reminders | Uses TRACE card independently |
| **Reasoning move use** | Does not recognize or name moves | Uses moves when prompted and named | Identifies and uses moves spontaneously |
| **Garden math application** | Completes tasks with direct instruction | Connects garden context to math concepts | Independently applies math to garden problems |
| **Scientific observation** | Records minimal observations | Records detailed observations with prompting | Records detailed, accurate observations independently |
| **Journal writing** | Minimal writing; relies on drawings | Writes with sentence starters and scaffolds | Writes detailed, reflective entries independently |

## Transition to Next Phase

- **To Restore:** When a student makes an error that requires analysis and reframing. "Something interesting happened in your work -- let's explore it."
- **To Regulate:** When dysregulation signals are detected. "Let's take a garden break and come back to this when your brain is ready."
- **To Reconnect:** When the learning objective has been met. "You've done strong thinking today. Let's connect it to the bigger picture."
