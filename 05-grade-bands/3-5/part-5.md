# 3-5 Reconnect Phase: Community Connections

## Overview

The Reconnect phase is the culmination of the 5Rs learning cycle, where students apply what they have learned to real-world contexts, connect new knowledge across subject areas, and share their growth with their community. For grades 3-5, this phase emphasizes cross-curricular projects, community garden sharing, beginning portfolio development, and the extension of learning beyond the classroom into family and neighborhood contexts. Students at this developmental level are ready to see their learning as meaningful and impactful -- the Reconnect phase makes that visible.

**Core Question**: "How does this connect to my world?"
**Duration**: 10-15 minutes per session; extended projects span multiple sessions
**Garden Metaphor**: Harvesting and sharing -- bringing the fruits of growth into the wider community.

## Cross-Curricular Project Design

The Reconnect phase deliberately bridges subject boundaries, helping students see that mathematics, science, language arts, and social-emotional learning are not isolated domains but interconnected ways of understanding the world. At the 3-5 level, cross-curricular projects are educator-designed with increasing student input as students mature.

### Project Framework

Each cross-curricular project in the Reconnect phase follows this structure:

| Component | Description | Reasoning Move |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| **Academic anchor** | The core standard or concept from the Reflect phase | CONNECT: Link to the lesson's learning objective |
| **Garden application** | A concrete task in the garden that uses the concept | TRANSFORM: Apply abstract knowledge to a physical context |
| **Second subject connection** | An explicit link to another content area | CONNECT: Bridge disciplines |
| **Community output** | Something that extends beyond the student to others | GENERALIZE: Apply learning to broader contexts |

### Sample Cross-Curricular Projects

**Project: "The Garden Harvest Report" (Grades 3-4, 2-3 sessions)**

| Subject | Activity | Standard |
|---------|----------|----------|
| **Math** | Weigh and measure harvested produce; calculate total yield in pounds and ounces | CCSS.Math.Content.4.MD.A.1; GSE MGSE4.MD.1 |
| **Science** | Discuss which growing conditions produced the best yield; form hypotheses | NGSS 3-5-ETS1-3; GSE S4L1 |
| **ELA** | Write a harvest summary report with data tables and conclusions | CCSS.ELA-Literacy.W.4.2; GSE ELAGSE4W2 |
| **Community** | Present findings to another class; donate produce to school cafeteria or food bank | SEL: Social awareness and community responsibility |

**Project: "Design a Butterfly Garden" (Grade 5, 4-5 sessions)**

| Subject | Activity | Standard |
|---------|----------|----------|
| **Math** | Calculate area and perimeter of the proposed garden; create a scaled drawing | CCSS.Math.Content.5.MD.A.1; GSE MGSE5.MD.1 |
| **Science** | Research which native plants attract pollinators; study pollination and ecosystems | NGSS 5-LS2-1; GSE S5L4.a |
| **ELA** | Write a persuasive proposal to the school administration for the butterfly garden | CCSS.ELA-Literacy.W.5.1; GSE ELAGSE5W1 |
| **Community** | Present the proposal at a school board meeting or family night | SEL: Civic engagement and public speaking |

**Project: "Seed Library" (Grades 3-5, ongoing)**

| Subject | Activity | Standard |
|---------|----------|----------|
| **Math** | Organize seeds by type; create a cataloging system with quantities and fractions | CCSS.Math.Content.3.NF.A.1; GSE MGSE3.NF.1 |
| **Science** | Research germination rates and optimal planting conditions for each seed variety | NGSS 3-LS1-1; GSE S3L1 |
| **ELA** | Write seed packet descriptions with planting instructions (procedural writing) | CCSS.ELA-Literacy.W.4.2; GSE ELAGSE4W2 |
| **Community** | Open the seed library to families and community members; teach others to save seeds | SEL: Generosity and knowledge-sharing |

## Community Garden Sharing

The garden produces tangible results that can be shared with others, creating authentic opportunities for students to see their learning as valuable and their contributions as meaningful. For students who have experienced trauma, the experience of giving -- of having something of value to offer -- can be deeply restorative.

### Sharing Structures

| Activity | Frequency | Community Reach | Student Role |
|----------|-----------|-----------------|-------------|
| **Harvest sharing** | Weekly during growing season | Classmates, school staff | Students harvest, weigh, and distribute produce |
| **Garden tours** | Monthly | Other classes, visiting families | Students serve as tour guides, explaining garden science |
| **Recipe cards** | With each harvest sharing | Families receiving produce | Students write recipe cards using procedural writing skills |
| **Seasonal celebrations** | Quarterly | Families, community partners | Students plan and host garden celebrations |
| **Food bank donations** | As harvest allows | Local community | Students weigh, package, and record donations |

### Educator Language for Community Sharing

- "You grew this with your own hands and your own thinking. Now it's going to feed someone's family. How does that feel?"
- "When you explain how the garden works to a visitor, you're teaching. That means you really understand it."
- "Our garden is part of the community. What we grow here doesn't just help us -- it helps everyone."

## Portfolio Development

Beginning in grade 3, students start building a growth portfolio that documents their learning journey across the 5Rs phases. The portfolio is a student-owned collection of work that demonstrates growth over time, not just achievement at a single point.

### Portfolio Components for 3-5

| Component | Description | Frequency | Reasoning Move |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|----------------|
| **Root phase check-ins** | Selected journal entries showing self-assessment growth | Monthly selection | COMPARE: How has my self-awareness changed? |
| **Reflect phase work samples** | Best work and most improved work (student-selected) | After each unit | CONNECT: What does this work show about my learning? |
| **Brain Growth Moments** | Selected error analysis entries from the growth journal | Quarterly | GENERALIZE: What patterns do I see in my growth? |
| **Garden documentation** | Photos, measurements, observations from garden work | Ongoing | TRANSFORM: Turn experiences into recorded evidence |
| **Reconnect projects** | Completed cross-curricular projects | After each project | CONNECT: How does my learning reach beyond me? |
| **Self-reflection** | Written or drawn reflections on growth | Quarterly | QUESTION: What am I most proud of? What do I want to improve? |

### Portfolio Conferences

Twice per year (mid-year and end-of-year), students present their portfolios in a student-led conference. The audience may include the educator, a family member, and/or an administrator.

**Conference Structure for 3-5:**
1. **Welcome (1 min):** Student greets the audience and introduces their portfolio.
2. **Growth story (3-5 min):** Student walks through 3-4 selected pieces, explaining what each one shows about their learning.
3. **Brain Growth Moment (2 min):** Student shares their favorite error analysis entry and what they learned from it.
4. **Garden highlight (2 min):** Student shares a garden achievement -- a measurement record, a harvest total, or a scientific observation.
5. **Goals (1 min):** Student shares one goal for the next learning period.

**Educator Language for Portfolio Preparation:**
- "Choose the work you are most proud of and the work that shows the most growth. Those might be different pieces."
- "When you present, use your reasoning moves. Tell your audience how you DECOMPOSED a problem or VERIFIED your answer."
- "This portfolio is the story of your brain growing. You get to tell that story."

## Real-World Math Applications

The Reconnect phase ensures that mathematical learning transfers to authentic contexts. At the 3-5 level, real-world applications center on the garden and the community.

### Application Activities

**"Garden Budget" (Grade 4-5):**
Students receive a budget for garden supplies and must plan purchases using addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division with multi-digit numbers.
- DECOMPOSE: Break the total budget into categories (soil, seeds, tools, fertilizer).
- COMPARE: Compare prices from two different suppliers.
- VERIFY: Check calculations before submitting the purchase plan.

**Standards:** CCSS.Math.Content.4.NBT.B.4, CCSS.Math.Content.5.NBT.B.5; GSE MGSE4.NBT.4, MGSE5.NBT.5

**"Cooking with Fractions" (Grade 3-4):**
Students use harvested garden produce to follow recipes, doubling or halving ingredients to practice fraction operations.
- TRANSFORM: Convert a recipe from 4 servings to 8 servings.
- VERIFY: Measure ingredients to check calculations.
- CONNECT: Link fraction operations to a real outcome (food they prepared themselves).

**Standards:** CCSS.Math.Content.4.NF.B.4; GSE MGSE4.NF.4

**"Garden Data Scientist" (Grade 5):**
Students analyze a full season of garden growth data, calculate averages, create graphs, and write a data-driven report.
- GENERALIZE: Identify patterns across the growing season.
- TRANSFORM: Represent data in multiple formats (table, bar graph, line graph).
- QUESTION: "What would we do differently next season based on this data?"

**Standards:** CCSS.Math.Content.5.MD.B.2, CCSS.Math.Content.5.NF.B.4; GSE MGSE5.MD.2, MGSE5.NF.4

## Environmental Stewardship

The Reconnect phase cultivates students' sense of responsibility toward the natural world. Garden-based learning provides a concrete foundation for understanding environmental interdependence.

### Stewardship Activities

| Activity | Learning Focus | Reasoning Move | Standard |
|----------|---------------|----------------|----------|
| **Composting program** | Decomposition, nutrient cycling, waste reduction | CONNECT: Link lunch waste to garden health | NGSS 5-ESS3-1; GSE S5E1 |
| **Water conservation audit** | Measurement, water cycle, resource management | QUESTION: "How much water does our garden use? How can we use less?" | NGSS 5-ESS2-2; GSE S4E3 |
| **Pollinator habitat** | Ecosystems, biodiversity, interdependence | GENERALIZE: "If pollinators disappear, what happens to our food?" | NGSS 5-LS2-1; GSE S5L4 |
| **Native plant identification** | Classification, adaptation, local ecology | COMPARE: Native vs. non-native plants in our garden | NGSS 3-LS4-3; GSE S3L1 |
| **Seasonal garden journal** | Weather patterns, climate, plant adaptation | TRANSFORM: Record seasonal changes over a full year | NGSS 3-ESS2-1; GSE S3E1 |

### Educator Language for Stewardship

- "When we take care of this garden, we're taking care of our community and our planet."
- "Every seed you plant and every worm you protect is part of a bigger system. You are part of that system too."
- "Scientists and environmentalists do exactly what you are doing -- observing, measuring, asking questions, and taking action."

## Family Garden Activities

The Reconnect phase extends learning into the home through family engagement activities. These activities are designed to be low-barrier (requiring minimal materials and no prior gardening knowledge) and high-connection (creating meaningful interactions between students and family members).

### Take-Home Garden Kits

Each quarter, students assemble a take-home kit that includes:
- Seeds or a seedling from the school garden
- A student-written planting guide (procedural writing practice)
- A math connection card ("Measure your plant each week and create a growth chart")
- A conversation prompt ("Ask your family: What did you grow when you were a kid?")

### Family Activities by Quarter

| Quarter | Activity | Academic Connection | Family Prompt |
|---------|----------|--------------------|--------------|
| **Fall** | Plant a windowsill herb garden | Measurement, observation writing | "Cook a meal together using your herbs and record the recipe." |
| **Winter** | Indoor seed starting with recycled containers | Fractions (soil mixtures), life cycles | "Predict which seeds will sprout first. Track the results together." |
| **Spring** | Transplant seedlings to a container or yard | Area, perimeter, garden design | "Design a garden space together. How big should it be?" |
| **End of year** | Harvest celebration recipe book | Procedural writing, measurement | "Share a family recipe that uses something you can grow." |

### Educator Language for Family Engagement

- "Tonight, you get to be the teacher. Show your family what you learned about fractions using the garden."
- "This seed came from our school garden. You grew it here, and now it gets to grow at your home too."
- "Ask someone in your family about their garden memories. Everyone has a connection to growing things, even if they don't have a garden."

## Celebration of Growth

The Reconnect phase celebrates effort, reasoning, and persistence rather than achievement alone. Celebration is specific, process-oriented, and connected to the reasoning moves and regulation skills students have developed.

### Celebration Language Models

| Instead of This | Use This |
|----------------|----------|
| "Good job!" | "You used DECOMPOSE and VERIFY today -- those are powerful reasoning moves." |
| "You're so smart!" | "You tried three different approaches before finding one that worked. That persistence is what scientists do." |
| "100%!" | "You caught your own error before I said anything. That self-checking skill is going to help you for the rest of your life." |
| "Perfect score!" | "You explained your thinking so clearly that anyone could follow your reasoning. That's real mathematical communication." |

### End-of-Session Reflection

Each session ends with a brief reflection that sets up the next Root phase:

- "What reasoning move was most helpful to you today?" (COMPARE across moves used.)
- "What was the hardest part, and how did you get through it?" (CONNECT effort to outcome.)
- "What is one question you still have?" (QUESTION: Keep curiosity alive.)
- "What goal do you want to set for next time?" (Student ownership of learning trajectory.)

## Assessment Indicators

| Indicator | Developing (1) | Approaching (2) | Proficient (3) |
|-----------|----------------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Cross-curricular connections** | Does not see links between subjects | Identifies connections with prompting | Independently connects learning across subjects |
| **Community engagement** | Reluctant to share or present | Participates in sharing with support | Enthusiastically shares knowledge with others |
| **Portfolio development** | Minimal portfolio entries; needs educator selection | Selects work with guidance; basic reflections | Curates portfolio thoughtfully; writes substantive reflections |
| **Real-world application** | Completes application tasks with direct instruction | Applies concepts to garden contexts with prompting | Independently identifies real-world applications |
| **Environmental stewardship** | Participates in garden tasks when directed | Shows care for garden; follows stewardship routines | Initiates stewardship actions; advocates for environmental care |
| **Family engagement** | Does not complete take-home activities | Completes activities; reports minimal family involvement | Completes activities and shares family responses |

## Transition to Next Cycle

The Reconnect phase completes the current 5Rs cycle and sets up the next session's Root phase. When the session ends, the student carries forward:

- A documented learning experience in their growth journal
- A specific intention or question for the next session
- A sense of accomplishment grounded in process, not just product
- A strengthened connection between academic learning and their lived world
- A growing portfolio that tells the story of their cognitive and emotional growth

**Educator Language for Closing:**
- "You did real work today -- thinking work, garden work, and growth work. I'm looking forward to where we go next."
- "Write your goal for next time in your journal. When we come back, we'll start right there."
- "Remember, everything you learned today is like a seed. It's going to keep growing, even when we're not in the garden."

The next session begins again with Root -- grounding and checking in -- continuing the predictable, safe rhythm that trauma-informed practice requires and that healing demands.
