# 3-5 Root Phase: Student-Selected Grounding

## Overview

The Root phase for grades 3-5 marks a developmental shift from adult-directed grounding (K-2) toward student-selected check-in formats. Students at this level (ages 8-11) are developing metacognitive awareness and can begin to identify their own emotional and physiological states with increasing accuracy. The Root phase honors this growth by offering choice in how students communicate their readiness to learn, while maintaining the predictable structure that trauma-informed practice requires.

**Core Question**: "Am I safe and ready to learn?"
**Duration**: 5-7 minutes
**Garden Metaphor**: Preparing the soil -- testing conditions, clearing debris, creating space for new growth.

## Developmental Considerations for Ages 8-11

Students in the 3-5 band are navigating significant cognitive and social transitions:

- Growing capacity for abstract thought alongside continued need for concrete supports
- Increased peer awareness and potential self-consciousness about emotional expression
- Developing internal language for feelings and body sensations
- Expanding executive function skills, including planning and self-monitoring
- Heightened sensitivity to perceived fairness and autonomy

These developmental realities shape every aspect of the Root phase at this level. Students need enough structure to feel safe and enough choice to feel respected.

## Student-Selected Check-In Formats

Rather than a single adult-led check-in, 3-5 students choose from three modalities each session. The educator presents all three options and students select the format that feels right for them that day. Over time, patterns in format selection become valuable assessment data.

| Format | Description | Materials | Best For |
|--------|-------------|-----------|----------|
| **Verbal Check-In** | Student shares aloud using a prompt or regulation scale | Regulation scale poster, prompt cards | Students comfortable with verbal expression |
| **Written Check-In** | Student writes 1-3 sentences in their growth journal | Growth journal, pencils | Students who process through writing |
| **Drawing Check-In** | Student draws their current state using color, weather, or garden imagery | Growth journal, colored pencils, drawing prompts | Students who need nonverbal expression |

### Educator Language Models for Introducing Choice

- "Welcome to our garden time. Before we begin, let's check in with ourselves. You can tell me, write it, or draw it -- your choice today."
- "Take a moment to notice how your body and mind are feeling right now. When you're ready, pick the way you want to share that with me."
- "Our check-in board has three paths today. Which one feels right for you?"

### Sample Check-In Prompts by Format

**Verbal Prompts:**
- "If your energy right now were a type of weather, what would it be?"
- "On our regulation scale, where would you place yourself -- and what does that feel like?"
- "What is one thing you noticed on your way here today?"

**Written Prompts:**
- "Right now I feel __________ because __________."
- "My body is telling me __________ today."
- "One thing I need before I can focus is __________."

**Drawing Prompts:**
- "Draw a plant that shows how you feel right now -- is it wilting, growing, blooming, or something else?"
- "Use colors to show what your energy looks like today."
- "Draw the weather inside your mind right now."

## Regulation Scale with Visual Supports

The 3-5 regulation scale bridges the picture-based system of K-2 and the numerical self-assessment of 6-8. Students use a 1-5 scale anchored by both numbers and garden imagery.

| Level | Garden Image | Feeling State | Body Signals | Educator Response |
|-------|-------------|---------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **5 -- Full Sun** | Bright, blooming flower | Energized, focused, ready | Relaxed body, steady breathing | Proceed to Reflect phase |
| **4 -- Partly Sunny** | Growing plant with some clouds | Mostly ready, slightly distracted | Minor restlessness, generally calm | Brief grounding, then Reflect |
| **3 -- Overcast** | Plant with closed bud | Uncertain, flat, unfocused | Tense shoulders, shallow breathing | Extended grounding activity |
| **2 -- Rainy** | Drooping plant in rain | Upset, worried, frustrated | Clenched fists, rapid heartbeat | Transition to Regulate phase |
| **1 -- Stormy** | Bent plant in wind | Overwhelmed, shut down, unsafe | Tears, withdrawal, agitation | Escalation protocol; safety first |

### Teaching the Regulation Scale

Introduce the scale during the first week through explicit instruction. Use the CONNECT reasoning move to link the scale to students' existing knowledge of weather and plant health.

**Lesson Sequence: "Our Garden Weather Report"**
1. Display the regulation scale poster with garden imagery.
2. Read aloud each level, asking students to act out the body signals. (DECOMPOSE: Break the scale into individual levels.)
3. Share an example: "This morning I woke up feeling like a 4 -- partly sunny. I was mostly ready but a little tired." (Model vulnerability without oversharing.)
4. Students practice placing themselves on the scale using a clothespin or magnet on a class chart.
5. Discuss: "Why might someone be at different levels on different days?" (QUESTION: Challenge the assumption that everyone should always be at a 5.)

## Garden-Based Grounding Activities

Grounding activities for 3-5 students use the garden environment to engage the senses and anchor attention to the present moment. These activities serve dual purposes: emotional grounding and academic content introduction.

### Soil Check-In (5 minutes)
Students visit their garden plot or a shared planting area. Each student touches the soil and completes one observation using their senses.

- "What does the soil feel like today -- dry, damp, cool, warm?" (COMPARE to yesterday's observation)
- "What do you see happening in the garden that was not there last time?" (QUESTION what caused the change)
- "Pick one herb and smell it. Does it make you feel more alert or more calm?" (CONNECT sensory experience to regulation)

**Standards Alignment:**
- NGSS 3-LS1-1: Develop models to describe that organisms have unique and diverse life cycles
- GSE S3L1: Obtain, evaluate, and communicate information about the similarities and differences between plants, animals, and habitats

### Seed Sorting (3-5 minutes)
Students sort a small collection of seeds by observable characteristics (size, shape, color, texture). This low-stakes, hands-on task settles the nervous system through focused attention and fine motor engagement.

**Standards Alignment:**
- CCSS.Math.Content.3.MD.B.3: Draw a scaled picture graph and a scaled bar graph (data from seed sorting)
- GSE MGSE3.MD.3: Draw a scaled picture graph and bar graph to represent data

### Watering Ritual (2-3 minutes)
Each student waters their personal plant or assigned section. The rhythmic, purposeful action of watering provides a predictable transition ritual.

**Educator Language:** "As you water your plant, notice the water soaking in. Just like your plant is taking in what it needs to grow, we are going to take in what we need to learn today."

## Beginning Journaling

The 3-5 growth journal is a student-owned document that travels across all five phases of the 5Rs cycle. During the Root phase, students write or draw a brief entry that serves as both a regulation check and a record of their learning journey.

### Journal Structure for Root Phase

Each Root phase journal entry includes:
1. **Date and regulation level** (number and/or drawing)
2. **Check-in response** (written or drawn, based on student's format choice)
3. **Intention** (one sentence or phrase about what they hope to learn or accomplish)

### Sample Journal Entry (Grade 4)
> **Date:** October 15 | **My Level:** 4 -- Partly Sunny
> **Check-In:** I feel a little nervous because we have a test later, but I like being in the garden.
> **My Intention:** I want to learn more about how seeds know which way to grow.

### Scaffolding Journaling for Reluctant Writers
- Provide sentence starters on a reference card attached to the journal
- Allow drawing with one-word labels as an alternative
- Offer the option to dictate to a peer or educator
- Use the TRANSFORM reasoning move: "Can you show the same feeling a different way?"

## Self-Assessment Introduction

By grades 3-5, students begin to assess their own readiness with increasing accuracy. The educator's role shifts from telling students their regulation level to asking students to identify it themselves, then gently calibrating when needed.

### Calibration Dialogue Examples

**When a student overestimates readiness:**
- "You said you're at a 5 today. I notice your hands are clenching a bit -- sometimes our bodies tell us things our minds haven't caught up to yet. Want to take another look?"
- "I appreciate you wanting to jump in. Let's do one quick grounding activity and then check again."

**When a student underestimates readiness:**
- "You marked yourself at a 2, but I see you making eye contact and your voice sounds steady. Sometimes we feel worse than we actually are. What do you think?"
- "It's okay to start at a 2. Let's see if our garden walk moves that number at all."

**When a student is accurate:**
- "You said you're at a 3 and I see that in your body too. Let's do our soil check-in and see if we can move toward a 4."

## Assessment Indicators

Educators observe and document the following during the Root phase:

| Indicator | Developing (1) | Approaching (2) | Proficient (3) |
|-----------|----------------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Format selection** | Needs prompting to choose a format | Chooses with some hesitation | Independently selects format |
| **Self-assessment accuracy** | Significant gap between stated and observed state | Occasional mismatch | Generally accurate self-assessment |
| **Grounding engagement** | Resists or rushes through activities | Participates with reminders | Engages purposefully |
| **Journal completion** | Leaves entry blank or minimal | Completes with scaffolding | Writes independently with detail |
| **Transition readiness** | Requires extended grounding | Needs brief grounding | Ready after standard check-in |

## Transition to Next Phase

Based on the Root phase check-in, students transition as follows:

- **To Reflect (Level 4-5):** "You seem grounded and ready. Let's grow some thinking today."
- **To extended grounding (Level 3):** "Let's spend a couple more minutes in the garden before we start. Your plant could use some attention too."
- **To Regulate (Level 1-2):** "It sounds like today is a tough one. Let's take care of you first -- the learning will wait."

The transition is always communicated with warmth and without judgment. Students at every level are affirmed: being at a 1 or 2 is not a failure -- it is important information that helps us take care of ourselves.

## Reasoning Moves in the Root Phase

Although the Root phase is primarily focused on emotional grounding, reasoning moves are introduced naturally:

- **CONNECT:** "How does the garden look different from last week? What might have caused that?"
- **COMPARE:** "Is your regulation level the same or different from yesterday? What changed?"
- **QUESTION:** "What do you think your plant needs today? How could you find out?"
- **DECOMPOSE:** "Let's break our check-in into parts -- body, mind, feelings. What does each one tell you?"

These low-stakes applications build familiarity with reasoning vocabulary before students encounter it in the more demanding Reflect phase.
