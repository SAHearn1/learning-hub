# 3-5 Regulate Phase: Building Self-Regulation

## Overview

The Regulate phase for grades 3-5 represents the critical developmental bridge between adult-led co-regulation and student-initiated self-regulation. Students at this level are beginning to develop the internal awareness necessary to recognize their own dysregulation signals, but they still require scaffolding, prompting, and relational support to select and deploy effective regulation strategies. The goal is not independence from adult support but rather a growing partnership in which the student takes increasing ownership of the regulation process.

**Core Question**: "Can I return to my window of tolerance?"
**Duration**: Variable (2-15 minutes depending on tier of intervention)
**Garden Metaphor**: Watering and tending -- providing the right resources at the right time to support healthy growth.

## The Self-Regulation Development Continuum for 3-5

Students in this grade band are typically moving through Stages 2 and 3 of the regulation continuum:

| Stage | Description | 3-5 Indicators | Educator Role |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Stage 1: Co-Regulation** | Educator identifies dysregulation and initiates strategy | Student cannot yet name what is happening | Educator leads; student follows |
| **Stage 2: Prompted Self-Regulation** | Educator helps student notice; student begins choosing | Student responds to "What do you notice in your body?" | Educator prompts; student selects |
| **Stage 3: Independent Self-Regulation** | Student recognizes signals and initiates strategies | Student says "I need a break" or "Can I go to the garden?" | Student leads; educator supports |
| **Stage 4: Proactive Regulation** | Student anticipates and pre-regulates | Rare at this level; emerging in some grade 5 students | Educator celebrates and reinforces |

Most 3rd graders enter at Stage 1-2. By 5th grade, many students can operate at Stage 3 with consistency. The educator's task is to scaffold this progression intentionally.

## Student Identification of Dysregulation Signals

A central goal of the 3-5 Regulate phase is teaching students to recognize their own body-based signals of dysregulation. This is explicitly taught, practiced, and reinforced across the school year.

### Body Signal Mapping Activity

**Lesson Sequence: "My Body's Warning System" (30 minutes, taught early in the year)**

1. **Introduction (5 min):** "Just like a plant shows us it needs water by drooping its leaves, our bodies send us signals when we need help regulating. Today we are going to learn to read our own signals."
2. **Body outline activity (10 min):** Students receive a printed body outline. Using colored pencils, they mark where they feel different emotions. Red for anger/frustration, blue for sadness/shutdown, yellow for anxiety/worry, green for calm/ready. (DECOMPOSE: Break the body into regions and analyze each one.)
3. **Partner sharing (5 min):** Students share their maps with a partner, noting similarities and differences. (COMPARE: "Where did you both put the same color? Where was it different?")
4. **Class discussion (5 min):** Generate a class list of "body signals" for hyperarousal and hypoarousal. Post these in the classroom.
5. **Personal signal card (5 min):** Each student creates a pocket-sized card listing their top 3 personal warning signals. This card stays in their growth journal.

**Standards Alignment:**
- GSE S3L1.b: Describe the function of specific body structures (connecting to body awareness)
- CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.3.1: Engage effectively in collaborative discussions

### Hyperarousal vs. Hypoarousal Recognition

Teach students to distinguish between the two directions of dysregulation using garden language:

| | **Hyperarousal ("Overgrown")** | **Hypoarousal ("Wilting")** |
|---|---|---|
| **Garden image** | A vine growing wildly in all directions | A plant drooping from lack of water |
| **What it feels like** | Racing thoughts, hot face, tight muscles, can't sit still | Foggy brain, heavy body, don't care, want to disappear |
| **What it looks like** | Talking fast, arguing, fidgeting, slamming things | Head down, one-word answers, staring, "I don't know" to everything |
| **What helps** | Slow down, cool down, release energy | Wake up, warm up, gently activate |

**Educator Language Models:**
- "It seems like your garden is overgrown right now -- lots of energy going in all directions. Let's prune it back a little."
- "Your plant looks like it could use some water. Let's do something to gently wake your body up."
- "I notice some signals that you might be outside your window. Want to check in with your body signal card?"

## The Regulation Toolbox

Each student in grades 3-5 develops a personal "Regulation Toolbox" -- a curated collection of strategies that they have tried and found effective. The toolbox is stored in the student's growth journal and updated throughout the year.

### Building the Toolbox

Strategies are introduced one at a time, practiced as a class, and then students decide whether to add them to their personal toolbox. This ensures student ownership and avoids one-size-fits-all approaches.

| Strategy | Type | Best For | How It Works |
|----------|------|----------|-------------|
| **Breathing Roots** | Breathing | Both hyper and hypo | Inhale while imagining roots growing down; exhale while imagining roots spreading wide. 4 counts in, 4 counts out. |
| **Garden Walk** | Movement | Hyperarousal | Walk slowly through the garden, touching 5 different plants and naming each one aloud. |
| **Soil Squeeze** | Sensory | Hyperarousal | Squeeze a ball of garden soil (or clay) tightly for 5 seconds, then release. Repeat 3 times. |
| **Herb Wake-Up** | Sensory | Hypoarousal | Crush a sprig of rosemary or mint between fingers and inhale deeply. The strong scent activates alertness. |
| **Seed Counting** | Focusing | Both | Count seeds into groups of 10. The repetitive, precise task anchors attention. |
| **Partner Check** | Relational | Both | Ask a regulation partner, "What do you notice about me right now?" |
| **Journal Release** | Expressive | Hyperarousal | Write or draw freely in the back pages of the growth journal for 2 minutes. No rules, no grades. |
| **Stretch and Grow** | Movement | Hypoarousal | Stand up and slowly stretch like a plant growing toward the sun, reaching higher with each breath. |

### Educator Language for Toolbox Use

- "It looks like this might be a good time to use something from your toolbox. What strategy do you think would help right now?"
- "Remember last week when the Breathing Roots really helped you settle? Would you like to try that again, or something different?"
- "You chose the Garden Walk yesterday and it seemed to work well. I'm noticing you're adding that to your toolbox -- smart move."

## Peer Regulation Partnerships

Beginning in grade 4, students are introduced to regulation partnerships -- pairs of students who support each other's regulation with educator guidance. This builds community, normalizes regulation needs, and begins developing the peer co-regulation skills that become central in grades 6-8.

### Setting Up Partnerships

1. Educators pair students intentionally (not by student choice), considering temperament, regulation patterns, and relational safety.
2. Partners learn three specific skills:
   - **Notice:** "I see you tapping your pencil a lot. Are you feeling overgrown?"
   - **Offer:** "Do you want to do Breathing Roots together?"
   - **Respect:** "Okay, no problem" (if the partner declines)
3. Partnerships rotate every 4-6 weeks to build a wider network of support.

### Ground Rules for Peer Partnerships

| Do | Do Not |
|----|--------|
| Use kind, quiet voices | Announce a partner's dysregulation to the class |
| Offer strategies from the toolbox | Tell a partner what to do |
| Accept "no thank you" gracefully | Take it personally if a partner declines |
| Tell an adult if you are worried about your partner | Try to handle a serious situation alone |

**Standards Alignment:**
- CCSS.ELA-Literacy.SL.4.1: Engage effectively in collaborative discussions, building on others' ideas
- GSE ELAGSE4SL1: Engage effectively in a range of collaborative discussions

## Garden Calming Strategies

The garden environment offers regulation opportunities unavailable in traditional classroom settings. These strategies are used when in-classroom interventions are insufficient (Tier 2) or when a student needs extended regulation time (Tier 3).

### Tiered Garden Interventions

**Tier 1 -- In-Context (No garden transition needed):**
- Holding a smooth garden stone at their desk
- Smelling a dried herb sachet kept in their supply box
- Looking at a photo of their garden plot posted inside their journal

**Tier 2 -- Brief Garden Visit (5-7 minutes):**
- Walking to the garden to water their personal plant
- Pulling 10 weeds slowly and deliberately
- Sitting on the garden bench for quiet observation with a timer

**Tier 3 -- Extended Garden Regulation (10-15 minutes):**
- Sustained rhythmic activity: raking, digging, turning compost
- Quiet sitting in the garden's regulation space with a journal
- Sensory exploration: sorting seeds, arranging stones, smelling herbs

### Educator Language for Garden Transitions

- "Would the garden help right now? You can water your plant and take a few minutes."
- "I think some fresh air and soil time would do your body good. Want to head out for a bit?"
- "The garden is always there when you need it. You can come back when you feel ready."

## Documentation and Progress Monitoring

Educators track regulation data to identify patterns and measure growth:

### Weekly Regulation Log

| Student | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Strategies Used | Notes |
|---------|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----------------|-------|
| Example | 2-H | 4 | 3 | 2-H | 4 | Garden Walk, Breathing Roots | Pattern: Mon/Thu after transitions from specials |

Key: Number = regulation level at Root check-in. H = hyperarousal. W = hypoarousal (wilting). No letter = regulated.

### Growth Indicators Over Time

- Decrease in frequency and duration of Regulate phase episodes
- Movement along the self-regulation continuum (Stage 1 toward Stage 3)
- Expansion of personal toolbox with varied strategies
- Increased accuracy of self-identification of dysregulation signals
- Growing use of regulation language ("I need a break," "My body is telling me...")

## Assessment Indicators

| Indicator | Developing (1) | Approaching (2) | Proficient (3) |
|-----------|----------------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Signal identification** | Cannot name body signals | Identifies signals with prompting | Independently names personal signals |
| **Strategy selection** | Requires educator to choose strategy | Selects from offered options | Independently chooses from toolbox |
| **Peer partnership** | Disengaged or overstepping boundaries | Participates with reminders | Notices, offers, and respects boundaries |
| **Return to window** | Requires extended support (15+ min) | Returns with moderate support (5-10 min) | Returns with brief support (2-5 min) |
| **Regulation language** | Does not use regulation vocabulary | Uses vocabulary with prompting | Uses vocabulary spontaneously |

## Transition Out of Regulate

The student transitions back to Root for a fresh grounding check when:

- Regulation level has returned to 3 or above on the garden scale
- Student verbally or physically indicates readiness ("I think I'm okay now")
- Breathing has slowed and body language has softened
- Student can engage in brief conversation without distress signals

**Educator Language:**
- "You did a great job taking care of yourself. When you're ready, let's check in again and see where you are."
- "Your body looks so much calmer now. Want to try our check-in again?"
- "There's no rush. Whenever you feel ready, we'll pick up where we left off."

The transition is always student-paced. Forcing a premature return to academic engagement risks re-triggering dysregulation and undermining the trust built during the Regulate phase.
