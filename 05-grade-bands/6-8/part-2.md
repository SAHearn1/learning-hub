# 6-8 Regulate Phase: Independent Regulation Skills

## Overview

The Regulate phase for grades 6-8 addresses the essential question: **"Can I return to my window of tolerance?"** At this developmental stage, students move from adult-directed co-regulation toward independent regulation strategy selection. The adolescent brain is undergoing significant prefrontal cortex development, making this the ideal window to build lifelong self-regulation habits. Garden-based activities like propagation, pruning, and soil preparation provide powerful somatic regulation opportunities that engage the body while calming the nervous system.

**Duration:** 5-10 minutes (flexible based on class regulation state)
**Core Principle:** Students learn to identify their dysregulation pattern, select an appropriate strategy, and return to their window of tolerance with decreasing adult support.

## Dysregulation Detection: Body Mapping

### Understanding the Nervous System

Middle school students are developmentally ready to understand the basic neuroscience behind their regulation states. This knowledge demystifies their experience and reduces shame.

**Key Concepts to Teach:**

| Concept | Student-Friendly Explanation | Garden Analogy |
|---------|------------------------------|----------------|
| Window of Tolerance | The zone where your brain can learn and connect (roughly 30-70 on the scale) | The optimal growing conditions for a plant -- right temperature, right moisture |
| Sympathetic activation (fight/flight) | Your body's gas pedal -- speeds everything up to protect you | A plant bolting in heat -- it is trying to survive, not thrive |
| Parasympathetic activation (rest/digest) | Your body's brake pedal -- slows everything down to recover | A plant going dormant in winter -- conserving energy |
| Dorsal vagal shutdown (freeze/collapse) | Your body's emergency brake -- shuts down when overwhelmed | A wilted plant -- still alive but not able to function right now |

### Body Mapping Activity

Students create personalized body maps showing where they experience dysregulation. This is completed once and updated periodically.

**Protocol:**
1. Provide students with a blank body outline (front and back).
2. Students use colored pencils to mark where they feel stress responses:
   - **Red** = heat, tension, tightness, pain
   - **Blue** = cold, numbness, emptiness, heaviness
   - **Yellow** = tingling, buzzing, vibrating, restlessness
3. Students annotate each area with the specific sensation and the emotion they associate with it.
4. Students identify their top 3 "early warning signals" that tell them they are leaving their window of tolerance.

### Educator Language Models for Body Mapping

- "Your body sends you signals before your brain catches up. Learning those signals is like learning to read the weather before a storm hits."
- "There is no right or wrong body map. Some people feel anxiety in their chest, others in their stomach, others in their hands. Your body is your body."
- "Think about how you check a plant for stress. Yellow leaves, drooping stems, dry soil. You are learning to check yourself the same way."

## Physiological Awareness: The Regulation Dashboard

Students track their physiological state using a simple "dashboard" metaphor. This builds interoception (awareness of internal body signals).

| Dashboard Gauge | What to Notice | Hypo (Too Low) | Window (Regulated) | Hyper (Too High) |
|----------------|---------------|-----------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Heart rate | Speed of heartbeat | Can barely feel it | Steady, rhythmic | Pounding, racing |
| Breathing | Depth and pace | Shallow, barely there | Deep, even, slow | Rapid, chest-only, gasping |
| Muscle tension | Tightness in body | Limp, collapsed, heavy | Relaxed but engaged | Rigid, clenched, braced |
| Temperature | Skin warmth | Cold hands, chills | Neutral, comfortable | Flushed, sweating, hot |
| Stomach | Gut sensation | Empty, hollow, numb | Settled, neutral | Churning, nauseous, tight |

**Quick Dashboard Check (30 seconds):**
Students mentally scan each gauge and rate it as low, window, or high. This can be done silently at any point during the class period.

## Peer Regulation Support

### Moving Beyond Peer Grounding to Peer Regulation

While the Root phase uses peer partnerships for grounding, the Regulate phase teaches students to support each other during active dysregulation.

### Peer Support Protocol: "The Garden Buddy Check"

When a student notices their partner is dysregulated:

| Step | Action | Language Model |
|------|--------|---------------|
| 1. Notice | Observe without assuming | (Internal: "I notice my partner seems different today.") |
| 2. Name | Quietly ask | "Hey, I am noticing you seem [tense/quiet/restless]. Am I reading that right?" |
| 3. Normalize | Validate without fixing | "That makes sense. It is okay to not be okay right now." |
| 4. Navigate | Offer strategy options | "Do you want to do a breathing exercise, take a movement break, or just have quiet space?" |
| 5. Nearby | Stay present | "I am right here. You do not have to talk. I am just going to sit with you." |

### Boundaries for Peer Support

**Educator Language Models for Teaching Boundaries:**

- "You are a buddy, not a counselor. If your partner shares something that worries you about their safety, tell me. That is not snitching. That is caring."
- "You cannot regulate someone else. You can only offer your calm presence and let them borrow your steadiness."
- "If supporting your partner is dysregulating you, it is okay to say, 'I want to help, but I need to take care of myself first.' That is not selfish. That is smart."

## Independent Regulation Strategy Selection

### The Strategy Selection Matrix

Students learn to match their dysregulation type with the most effective strategy category.

| If You Are... | Your Nervous System Is... | Try Strategies That... | Examples |
|---------------|--------------------------|----------------------|----------|
| Shut down, flat, numb | In dorsal vagal (freeze) | Gently activate and energize | Gentle movement, cold water on face, rhythmic bilateral stimulation |
| Anxious, racing, jittery | In sympathetic (fight/flight) | Calm and slow down | Deep breathing, progressive relaxation, grounding through senses |
| Angry, explosive, reactive | In sympathetic with high arousal | Discharge energy safely | Vigorous movement, pushing against a wall, digging in garden soil |
| Scattered, unfocused, foggy | Between states | Orient and organize | 5-4-3-2-1 senses, task list, anchor statement |

### Tiered Intervention Model

| Tier | Who Leads | When | Duration | Strategies |
|------|-----------|------|----------|------------|
| Tier 1: Universal | Student self-directs | Regulation number 60-75 or 25-40 | 1-2 min | Breathing, sensory grounding, quick movement |
| Tier 2: Peer-supported | Student + partner | Regulation number 75-85 or 15-25 | 2-5 min | Partner breathing, buddy walk, garden buddy check |
| Tier 3: Adult co-regulation | Student + educator | Regulation number 85+ or below 15 | 5-10 min | Quiet space, one-on-one co-regulation, garden walk with adult |
| Tier 4: Crisis support | Student + support team | Sustained crisis | As needed | Counselor, safe space, family contact |

## Garden-Based Regulation Activities

### Propagation as Regulation

Plant propagation is a powerful regulation activity because it requires gentle focus, fine motor control, and patience -- all of which activate the parasympathetic nervous system.

**Propagation Station Protocol:**

1. **Setup:** Prepare a propagation station with stem cuttings, rooting hormone, small pots, moist soil, and misting bottles.
2. **Regulation Connection:** "We are going to take a part of a healthy plant and give it the conditions it needs to grow new roots. Sometimes when we are dysregulated, we need to return to our roots too."
3. **Activity Sequence:**
   - Select a healthy stem with 3-4 nodes (DECOMPOSE: identify the parts of the stem)
   - Make a clean cut at 45 degrees below a node (focus, precision, steady hands)
   - Remove lower leaves gently (fine motor, patience)
   - Dip in rooting hormone and place in moist soil (care, tenderness)
   - Mist lightly and label with date (completion, record-keeping)
4. **Debrief:** "Notice how your body feels after that careful, focused work. Check your dashboard."

### Pruning as Regulation

Pruning teaches that intentional removal supports healthier growth -- a powerful metaphor for letting go of what no longer serves us.

**Pruning Protocol:**
- Students identify dead, damaged, or crossing branches on garden plants.
- Make clean cuts with pruning shears (supervised) at the correct angle.
- Discuss: "What do we need to prune from our day to help ourselves grow? Negative self-talk? Comparison to others? Grudges?"

### Soil Preparation as Regulation

Digging, turning, and amending soil provides heavy work input that is deeply regulating for the proprioceptive system.

**Activities:**
- Turning compost with a pitchfork (bilateral, rhythmic, heavy work)
- Mixing soil amendments by hand (sensory, grounding, tactile)
- Building raised bed soil layers (organizing, structuring, purposeful movement)

## Regulation Skill-Building Sequence

### Semester-Long Development Plan

| Weeks | Focus | Student Goal | Garden Integration |
|-------|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| 1-3 | Awareness | Accurately identify dysregulation type using body map | Observe plants for stress signals; compare to own signals |
| 4-6 | Knowledge | Match dysregulation type to strategy category using the matrix | Learn which garden interventions help which plant problems |
| 7-9 | Practice | Use strategies independently at Tier 1 with 80% success | Practice propagation, pruning, and soil prep as regulation tools |
| 10-12 | Peer support | Effectively use buddy check protocol with partner | Co-regulate with garden partner during garden work sessions |
| 13-15 | Fluency | Self-select and deploy strategies before adult prompting | Independently choose garden regulation activity when needed |
| 16-18 | Transfer | Apply regulation skills outside the classroom context | Teach regulation strategies to younger garden buddies |

## Assessment Indicators

| Indicator | Developing | Proficient | Advanced |
|-----------|-----------|------------|----------|
| Body awareness | Cannot identify physical stress signals | Identifies 2-3 early warning signals accurately | Detects subtle shifts and responds before escalation |
| Strategy selection | Uses one strategy regardless of dysregulation type | Matches strategy to dysregulation type most of the time | Flexibly adapts strategy mid-use based on effectiveness |
| Independence | Requires adult prompting to begin regulation | Self-initiates at Tier 1; seeks help at Tier 2-3 | Moves through tiers autonomously; seeks help only when needed |
| Peer support | Avoids or is uncomfortable with buddy check | Follows protocol; offers support when prompted | Naturally notices peer dysregulation and offers appropriate support |
| Garden regulation | Participates passively in garden activities | Engages in garden activities and reports regulation benefit | Independently selects garden activities for specific regulation needs |

## Transition to Reflect Phase

The Regulate phase concludes when students have returned to or near their window of tolerance.

**Transition Check:**
1. Students re-check their regulation number (silent, private).
2. Educator scans the room for readiness signals.
3. Educator uses transition language: "If you are in a place where your brain can engage with new ideas, let us move into our thinking work. If you need another moment, take it. The learning will be here when you are ready."

### For Students Still Dysregulated

- Provide a modified Reflect phase activity that requires less cognitive demand.
- Allow continued garden-based regulation during the Reflect phase if needed.
- Check in privately and adjust expectations: "Today, your job is just to listen and capture one idea in your journal. That is enough."

## Educator Implementation Notes

- **Regulation is not compliance.** A regulated student is one whose nervous system can engage in learning -- not one who is sitting still and being quiet. A student who needs to stand, fidget, or move may be perfectly regulated.
- **Do not rush this phase.** If the class is significantly dysregulated (e.g., after a lockdown drill, a fight at lunch, a community crisis), extend the Regulate phase and shorten Reflect. Content coverage matters less than nervous system readiness.
- **Garden regulation is not a reward.** It is an intervention. Do not frame garden time as something students earn through good behavior. Frame it as a tool everyone can access.
- **Build your own regulation toolkit.** Educator dysregulation is contagious. Your calm nervous system is the most powerful co-regulation tool in the room. Know your own body map and use your own strategies visibly.
- **Document patterns for support teams.** If a student is consistently at Tier 3 or 4, share this data (with student knowledge) with counselors and support staff. This is not a behavior report -- it is a wellness assessment.
