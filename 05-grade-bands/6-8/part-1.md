# 6-8 Root Phase: Self-Assessment and Peer Grounding

## Overview

The Root phase for grades 6-8 asks the essential question: **"Am I safe and ready to learn?"** At this developmental stage, students are capable of sophisticated self-assessment and benefit from peer grounding partnerships. The Root phase transitions from adult-led check-ins to student-owned regulation awareness, building the metacognitive foundation that middle schoolers need to navigate both academic challenges and the social-emotional turbulence of adolescence.

**Duration:** 3-5 minutes per session
**Core Principle:** Students develop independent capacity to assess their own readiness and use peer support for grounding.

---

## The Regulation Scale (0-100)

Middle school students use a numeric self-assessment scale that builds quantitative literacy while developing emotional vocabulary.

| Range | Zone | Description | Indicators | Garden Metaphor |
|-------|------|-------------|------------|-----------------|
| 0-20 | Shutdown | Withdrawn, disconnected, flat affect | Head down, non-responsive, avoidance | Dormant seed -- alive but not yet activated |
| 21-40 | Low Energy | Sluggish, unfocused, passive | Slow responses, low motivation, yawning | Seedling in shade -- needs light and warmth |
| 41-60 | Regulated | Calm, alert, focused, present | Eye contact, engaged posture, responsive | Plant in full sun with water -- thriving |
| 61-80 | Elevated | Restless, anxious, fidgety, reactive | Tapping, rapid speech, difficulty sitting | Overfertilized plant -- too much energy |
| 81-100 | Overwhelmed | Flooded, fight/flight, crisis | Aggression, tears, fleeing, shutting down | Plant in a storm -- needs shelter now |

### Educator Language Models for Scale Introduction

- "Take a moment to check your internal weather. Where are you on the scale right now -- zero to one hundred?"
- "There is no wrong number. A twenty is just as valid as a fifty. We are gathering data about ourselves."
- "Your number might change from first period to third period. That is completely normal. We are tracking patterns, not judging feelings."
- "Think of this like checking soil moisture before you water. You would not pour water without checking first."

## Peer Grounding Partnerships

### Structure and Purpose

Peer grounding partnerships pair students for mutual support during the Root phase. Partners are assigned (not self-selected) and rotate every 2-3 weeks to build community breadth.

### Partnership Protocol (2 minutes)

| Step | Duration | Action | Partner A | Partner B |
|------|----------|--------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | 15 sec | Settle | Both partners sit facing each other, feet flat on floor | Same |
| 2 | 30 sec | Check-in | Shares regulation number and one word for current state | Listens without responding |
| 3 | 30 sec | Check-in | Listens without responding | Shares regulation number and one word |
| 4 | 30 sec | Grounding | Partner A selects a shared grounding activity | Partner B participates |
| 5 | 15 sec | Transition | Both state: "I am at [number] and I am ready to begin." | Same |

### Educator Language Models for Partnerships

- "Your job as a partner is to witness, not to fix. You are not a therapist. You are a fellow human showing up."
- "If your partner shares a low number, you do not need to make it better. You just need to hear it."
- "Respect the container. What your partner shares stays between you two unless there is a safety concern."

### Grounding Activity Menu (Partner-Selected)

Students choose from a posted menu. Activities are designed to be quiet and non-disruptive.

1. **Synchronized Breathing** -- Both partners breathe in for 4 counts, hold for 4, out for 6. Three cycles.
2. **Sensory Scan** -- Partners each name one thing they can see, hear, and feel in the room. No repeats.
3. **Soil Touch** -- If garden soil samples are available, both partners hold soil and describe its temperature and texture silently.
4. **Bilateral Tapping** -- Partners alternately tap their own knees left-right for 30 seconds at a slow, steady pace.
5. **Anchor Statement** -- Each partner states: "My name is ___. I am in ___ class. Today is ___. I am here."

## Growth Journal Entry: Root Page

Each session begins with a brief journal entry that serves as both a regulation record and a writing warm-up.

### Journal Prompt Structure

Students maintain a dedicated Growth Journal. The Root page is the first entry for each session.

**Template:**

```
Date: _______________  Period: _______________
Regulation Number: _____ / 100
One-Word Weather Report: _______________
What I notice in my body right now: _______________
My grounding strategy today: _______________
I am / am not ready to learn because: _______________
```

### Educator Language Models for Journaling

- "This is your private data. I will never read it aloud or share it. This is between you and your journal."
- "Write honestly. If you are at a fifteen, write fifteen. Pretending you are at a fifty does not help you learn."
- "Scientists record observations before experiments. You are the scientist and your inner state is the data."

## Garden Observation Journals

### Connecting Root Phase to Garden Science

The Root phase integrates naturally with garden observation, linking self-assessment to scientific observation skills.

**Weekly Garden Observation Protocol (when garden access is available):**

| Observation Type | Scientific Skill | Self-Connection | Standards Alignment |
|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Measure plant height and record growth | Quantitative data collection | "Growth is not always visible day to day" | MGSE6.SP.1, MGSE6.SP.5 |
| Sketch leaf structure and note changes | Detailed scientific illustration | "Close observation reveals what rushing misses" | S7L4a (GA Science) |
| Test soil pH and moisture levels | Using measurement instruments | "Conditions affect growth -- for plants and for us" | S8P1a (GA Science) |
| Record weather conditions and correlations | Identifying variables | "External conditions influence internal states" | NGSS MS-ESS2-5 |
| Photograph and compare over time | Longitudinal data analysis | "Change is measurable when you track it" | CCSS.MATH.PRACTICE.MP2 |

### Sample Garden Observation Journal Entry

```
Date: ___________  Weather: ___________  Temp: _____
My regulation number today: _____ / 100

PLANT OBSERVATION:
Plant ID: ___________  Location: ___________
Height (cm): _____  Number of leaves: _____
Soil moisture (1-10): _____  Soil pH: _____
Changes since last observation: ___________
Sketch:

CONNECTION:
One way my plant's growth connects to my own: ___________
```

## Independent Grounding Strategies

By grades 6-8, students should develop a personal toolkit of grounding strategies they can deploy without adult prompting.

### Strategy Development Sequence

**Week 1-2:** Students try all strategies from the class menu with guidance.
**Week 3-4:** Students identify their top 3 preferred strategies.
**Week 5-6:** Students practice deploying strategies independently when they notice dysregulation.
**Week 7+:** Students can self-select and initiate grounding without prompting.

### Independent Strategy Menu

| Strategy | Type | Best For | Garden Connection |
|----------|------|----------|-------------------|
| 5-4-3-2-1 Senses | Sensory | Dissociation, spacing out | Name 5 plants, 4 garden textures, 3 sounds, 2 scents, 1 taste |
| Progressive muscle tension-release | Somatic | Physical tension, restlessness | Squeeze soil, release; grip trowel, release |
| Square breathing (4-4-4-4) | Respiratory | Anxiety, racing thoughts | Breathe in rhythm with wind through garden |
| Cold water on wrists | Thermal | Emotional flooding | Hold cool watering can; touch morning dew on leaves |
| Mental inventory | Cognitive | Overwhelm, shutdown | Mentally list every plant species in the garden |
| Journaling sprint | Expressive | Anger, frustration | Write a letter to your plant about your day |

## Assessment Indicators

### Student Self-Assessment Accuracy

Educators observe whether students can accurately and honestly assess their regulation state over time.

| Indicator | Developing | Proficient | Advanced |
|-----------|-----------|------------|----------|
| Uses regulation scale | Needs prompting; numbers do not match observed behavior | Independently rates; numbers generally align with behavior | Accurately rates; can articulate why and predict shifts |
| Peer partnership engagement | Passive; one-word responses; avoids eye contact | Engages reciprocally; listens and shares | Adapts approach to partner's needs; models grounding |
| Growth journal completion | Incomplete entries; surface-level responses | Consistent entries; genuine self-reflection | Entries show pattern recognition and self-insight |
| Independent grounding | Requires adult direction to initiate | Uses toolkit when prompted by scale number | Self-initiates before dysregulation escalates |
| Garden observation quality | Minimal data; no self-connection | Accurate data; attempts connection | Rich data; insightful parallels between plant and self |

## Transition to Regulate Phase

The Root phase concludes with a readiness signal. For grades 6-8, this is a private, autonomous action.

**Transition Protocol:**
1. Student reviews their regulation number.
2. If 30-70: Student gives a subtle thumbs-up on their desk (visible only to educator).
3. If below 30 or above 70: Student places a colored card on desk corner (yellow = "I need a moment," red = "I need support").
4. Educator acknowledges all signals with a nod -- no verbal commentary, no public attention.

### Educator Language Model for Transition

- "If you are in the thirties-to-seventies zone, give me a quiet thumbs-up and we will move forward together."
- "If you are outside that zone, that is okay. Use your card. I will check in with you. There is no penalty for not being ready."
- "Readiness is not performance. It is honesty. I would rather you tell me you are at twenty than pretend you are at fifty."

## Educator Implementation Notes

- **Consistency is non-negotiable.** The Root phase happens every session, even when time is short. A 90-second version (number + one breath) is better than skipping.
- **Never publicly discuss a student's number.** The scale is private data. If you need to check in, do so quietly and individually.
- **Rotate partnerships thoughtfully.** Avoid pairing students in active conflict. Consider regulation patterns -- do not always pair high-regulation students with low-regulation students.
- **Model vulnerability.** Share your own regulation number occasionally. "I am at about a sixty-two today. I had a tough commute. I am using my breathing to bring it down."
- **Track patterns, not incidents.** If a student is consistently below 30, that is information for a support conversation -- not a discipline referral.
- **Garden time amplifies Root.** When possible, conduct the Root phase in or near the garden. The sensory environment naturally supports grounding.
