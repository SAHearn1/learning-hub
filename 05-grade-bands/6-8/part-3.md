# 6-8 Reflect Phase: Full TRACE Implementation

## Overview

The Reflect phase for grades 6-8 addresses the essential question: **"What am I thinking, and why?"** This is where the full TRACE protocol is implemented for the first time in the curriculum sequence. Middle school students engage all five TRACE steps -- Think, Reason, Articulate, Check, Extend -- along with all seven reasoning moves. Garden-based content integrates math (ratios, proportions, statistics, geometry), science (soil chemistry, ecology, photosynthesis), and ELA (argumentative and informational writing).

**Duration:** 20-35 minutes (core instructional time)
**Core Principle:** Students develop metacognitive awareness -- they do not just learn content; they learn how they learn.

---

## The TRACE Protocol: Full Implementation

### Protocol Overview

| Step | Question | Student Action | Duration | Reasoning Moves Used |
|------|----------|---------------|----------|---------------------|
| **T - Think** | What do I already know? | Activate prior knowledge; identify what is familiar and what is new | 2-3 min | CONNECT, QUESTION |
| **R - Reason** | How can I work through this? | Apply reasoning moves to engage with new content; solve, investigate, analyze | 10-15 min | DECOMPOSE, COMPARE, VERIFY, TRANSFORM |
| **A - Articulate** | How do I explain my thinking? | Write or speak their reasoning process; make invisible thinking visible | 3-5 min | GENERALIZE, CONNECT |
| **C - Check** | Does my thinking hold up? | Verify work, check for errors, test reasoning against evidence | 3-5 min | VERIFY, QUESTION, COMPARE |
| **E - Extend** | Where else does this apply? | Transfer learning to new contexts; generate new questions | 2-3 min | GENERALIZE, TRANSFORM, CONNECT |

### Educator Language Models for TRACE

- **Think:** "Before we dig into this, what do you already know? What seeds of understanding are already planted?"
- **Reason:** "Now work through it. Use your reasoning tools. Show me the path your brain takes, not just the destination."
- **Articulate:** "Explain your thinking as if you are teaching someone who was absent today. Be specific."
- **Check:** "Go back through your work like a gardener inspecting a row after planting. Did you miss any spots? Are the seeds at the right depth?"
- **Extend:** "Where else in your life, your community, or other subjects does this idea show up? Plant this knowledge in new soil."

## The Seven Reasoning Moves

### Complete Reference for Grades 6-8

| Reasoning Move | Definition | Signal Phrases for Students | Garden Example |
|---------------|-----------|---------------------------|----------------|
| **DECOMPOSE** | Break a complex problem into smaller, manageable parts | "Let me break this down..." / "The parts of this are..." | Breaking down the factors affecting plant growth: soil pH, water, sunlight, nutrients, temperature |
| **COMPARE** | Identify similarities and differences between two or more things | "This is similar to... because..." / "The difference is..." | Comparing growth rates of plants in sun vs. shade; comparing soil types |
| **QUESTION** | Generate productive questions that deepen understanding | "I wonder why..." / "What would happen if..." | "What would happen if we changed the soil pH from 6.5 to 4.0?" |
| **VERIFY** | Test a claim or solution against evidence or logic | "I can check this by..." / "The evidence shows..." | Measuring actual plant growth against predicted growth; retesting soil pH |
| **GENERALIZE** | Identify patterns and form rules or principles from specific cases | "I notice a pattern..." / "This means that in general..." | "Plants in our garden with more nitrogen grew taller, so nitrogen supports vertical growth" |
| **TRANSFORM** | Change the representation or form of information | "Another way to show this is..." / "If I convert this..." | Converting garden measurement data into graphs, ratios, or scale drawings |
| **CONNECT** | Link new learning to prior knowledge or other domains | "This reminds me of..." / "This connects to..." | "The way plants compete for sunlight connects to what we learned about competition in ecosystems" |

### Teaching the Reasoning Moves

**Introduction Sequence (first 3 weeks):**
- Week 1: Introduce DECOMPOSE and CONNECT using garden sorting and observation activities
- Week 2: Introduce COMPARE and QUESTION using paired plant investigations
- Week 3: Introduce VERIFY, GENERALIZE, and TRANSFORM using data analysis from garden measurements

**Sustained Practice:**
- Post reasoning moves on the wall with garden-themed icons.
- Students identify which reasoning moves they used after each TRACE cycle.
- Growth journals include a "Reasoning Moves I Used Today" section.

## Garden-Based Math: TRACE in Action

### Sample Lesson: Ratios and Proportions in the Garden

**Standards:** MGSE6.RP.1, MGSE6.RP.2, MGSE6.RP.3, MGSE7.RP.1, MGSE7.RP.2

**TRACE Walkthrough:**

**T - Think (2 min):**
Educator: "We are going to explore ratios in our garden. What do you already know about ratios? Where have you seen them?"
Students activate prior knowledge: recipes, sports statistics, scales on maps.

**R - Reason (12 min):**
Students receive garden data and work through ratio problems:

| Garden Scenario | Math Task | Reasoning Move |
|----------------|-----------|---------------|
| Our garden bed is 4ft x 8ft. We want to build one that is proportionally 1.5 times larger. | Calculate new dimensions using scale factor | TRANSFORM |
| We planted 24 tomato plants and 18 pepper plants. | Express the ratio in simplest form; find unit rate | DECOMPOSE |
| 15 out of 20 seeds germinated in Bed A; 18 out of 30 germinated in Bed B. | Compare germination rates using equivalent fractions | COMPARE, VERIFY |
| Our compost recipe calls for 3 parts brown material to 1 part green material. We have 12 buckets of brown material. | Find the amount of green material needed; scale the recipe | GENERALIZE, TRANSFORM |
| Tomato plants grew an average of 2.5 inches per week for 8 weeks. Pepper plants grew 1.8 inches per week. | Calculate total growth; compare rates; predict future growth | COMPARE, GENERALIZE |

**A - Articulate (4 min):**
Students write in their Growth Journal: "Explain how you set up and solved one ratio problem. Include which reasoning moves you used and why."

**C - Check (3 min):**
Students swap journals with a partner. Partner checks:
- Is the ratio set up correctly (same units, correct order)?
- Does the math check out?
- Does the answer make sense in context (reasonableness)?

**E - Extend (2 min):**
"Where else do ratios and proportions show up in gardening? In cooking? In your life?"
Students generate 2-3 new ratio questions they could investigate.

| Math Topic | Grade | Garden Application | Standards |
|-----------|-------|-------------------|-----------|
| Statistical variability | 6 | Measure and analyze variation in plant heights across beds | MGSE6.SP.1-5 |
| Proportional relationships | 7 | Scale garden designs; fertilizer dilution ratios | MGSE7.RP.1-3 |

## Garden-Based Science: TRACE in Action

### Sample Lesson: Soil Chemistry and Plant Growth

**Standards:** NGSS MS-LS1-5, MS-LS2-1, MS-ESS3-3, GA S8P1

**TRACE Walkthrough:**

**T - Think (3 min):**
Educator: "We know plants need soil to grow. But what is actually in soil that matters? What do you already know about what makes soil good or bad for plants?"
Students brainstorm in pairs, using CONNECT to link to prior garden observations.

**R - Reason (15 min):**
Students conduct a soil investigation:

1. **DECOMPOSE:** Separate soil components -- sand, silt, clay, organic matter -- using jar sedimentation test.
2. **COMPARE:** Test pH of three different soil samples (garden bed, compost, clay from schoolyard).
3. **QUESTION:** "What would happen to our tomato plants if we grew them in the high-pH clay soil instead of our amended garden bed soil?"
4. **VERIFY:** Design a controlled experiment -- same seeds, different soils, measure germination and growth over 3 weeks.
5. **TRANSFORM:** Record data in tables; create graphs comparing growth across soil types.

**A - Articulate (4 min):**
Students write a hypothesis and explain their experimental design: "I predict ___ because ___. I will control for ___ by ___."

**C - Check (3 min):**
Peer review of experimental designs:
- Is there a clear independent variable?
- Are controls identified?
- Is the dependent variable measurable?
- Is the prediction based on evidence or reasoning?

**E - Extend (3 min):**
"How does understanding soil chemistry connect to food justice? Why might some communities have healthier soil than others? What does this mean for food access?"

### Additional Science Topics with Garden Integration

| Science Topic | Focus | Garden Application | Standards |
|--------------|-------|-------------------|-----------|
| Photosynthesis | Energy conversion | Measure light exposure and correlate with plant growth; leaf chromatography | NGSS MS-LS1-6 |
| Ecosystems and interdependence | Ecological relationships | Map the food web in the school garden; identify pollinators, decomposers, predators | NGSS MS-LS2-1, MS-LS2-2 |
| Water cycle | Earth systems | Track irrigation water, rainfall, transpiration, and evaporation in garden | NGSS MS-ESS2-4 |

## ELA Integration: Argumentative and Informational Writing

### Argumentative Writing with Garden Context

**Standards:** CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.6-8.1, CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.6-8.1a-e

**TRACE Application for Argumentative Writing:**

| TRACE Step | Writing Application | Sample Prompt |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| Think | Brainstorm position and opposing views | "Should our school replace part of the athletic field with a food garden?" |
| Reason | Gather evidence from research and garden data | Students use DECOMPOSE to identify claim, reasons, and evidence |
| Articulate | Draft argument with clear claim, reasons, evidence, and counterclaim | Students use CONNECT to link evidence to claim; COMPARE to address counterclaim |
| Check | Peer review using argumentative writing checklist | Students use VERIFY to check logical consistency; QUESTION to probe weak points |
| Extend | Revise and consider real-world impact | "Who would you send this argument to? A school board member? A community leader?" |

### Informational Writing with Garden Context

**Standards:** CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.6-8.2, CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.6-8.2a-f

**Sample Prompts:**
- "Write an informational guide for a new student joining our garden program. Explain the science behind composting."
- "Create a technical report analyzing our garden's yield data over the past semester. Include data displays and explanations."
- "Write a procedural guide for propagating three different plant species, explaining the science behind each method."

## Assessment Indicators

| Indicator | Developing | Proficient | Advanced |
|-----------|-----------|------------|----------|
| TRACE protocol use | Completes steps with prompting; steps are surface-level | Independently moves through all 5 steps with substance | Fluidly integrates steps; adapts sequence to task; teaches others |
| Reasoning move application | Uses 1-2 moves; cannot name them | Uses 3-4 moves appropriately; can name and explain choices | Strategically selects from all 7 moves; combines moves; reflects on effectiveness |
| Math problem solving | Arrives at answer but cannot explain process | Shows work and explains reasoning; catches some errors | Multiple solution paths; evaluates efficiency; creates new problems |
| Scientific investigation | Follows procedures but cannot explain why | Designs investigations with controlled variables; uses evidence | Generates hypotheses from patterns; critiques experimental design |
| Written articulation | Brief, vague explanations | Clear, specific explanations with evidence and reasoning | Nuanced explanations that acknowledge complexity and limitations |
| Garden content knowledge | Basic plant identification and care | Understands scientific principles behind garden practices | Applies scientific understanding to novel garden challenges |

## Differentiation Within the Reflect Phase

| Student Need | Modification | TRACE Adaptation |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Below grade level in math | Provide graphic organizers; reduce number of problems; use manipulatives | Extend Think step with more prior knowledge activation; provide reasoning move sentence starters |
| Above grade level in math | Offer extension problems; ask for multiple solution strategies | Add complexity to Extend step; ask student to create problems for peers |
| English learner | Provide bilingual vocabulary; use visual supports; allow oral articulation | Pair with language buddy during Articulate step; allow home language for Think step |
| Student with IEP | Follow IEP accommodations; chunk Reason step into smaller segments | Provide TRACE checklist; allow extra time; offer choice in Articulate mode (write, speak, draw) |
| Dysregulated student | Offer modified task with lower cognitive demand; allow garden regulation break | Shorten TRACE cycle; focus on Think and one Reason problem; skip Extend if needed |

## Educator Implementation Notes

- **TRACE is a protocol, not a worksheet.** It is a thinking routine students internalize over time. In the first weeks, you guide each step explicitly. By mid-year, students should initiate TRACE on their own.
- **Name the reasoning moves constantly.** Say: "You just used COMPARE. Did you notice that?" Make the invisible visible.
- **Garden content is not an add-on.** The garden is the primary context for standards-aligned learning. Math problems are garden problems. Science investigations happen in the garden. Writing is about the garden.
- **Protect the Articulate step.** It is tempting to skip or shorten this step when time is tight. Do not. Articulation is where learning solidifies. If you must shorten something, reduce the number of Reason problems.
- **Use the Check step to normalize error.** When students find mistakes during Check, celebrate: "You found something. That means the Check step is working. This is exactly what scientists and mathematicians do."
- **Extend builds transfer.** Without the Extend step, knowledge stays siloed in one context. This is the step where garden math becomes life math.
