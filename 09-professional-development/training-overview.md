# Professional Development Training Overview

## Program Philosophy

Effective implementation of the From a Garden Growth (FG2G) curriculum requires educators who embody its principles — not just understand them intellectually but practice them personally. The FG2G Professional Development program is built on the premise that educators cannot facilitate trauma-informed, garden-based learning unless they have experienced the 5Rs framework themselves.

> *"You cannot guide a student through the garden if you have never felt the soil between your own fingers. Professional development in FG2G is not training about a curriculum — it is transformation through a curriculum."*

## Ongoing Support Structures

### Coaching Model

Each new FG2G educator receives dedicated coaching support during their first implementation year:

| Support Type | Frequency | Format |
|---|---|---|
| On-site coaching visits | Monthly (first semester); bimonthly (second semester) | In-person observation and debrief |
| Virtual check-ins | Biweekly | 30-minute video calls |
| Lesson plan review | Weekly (first quarter); biweekly thereafter | Asynchronous written feedback |
| Peer learning circles | Monthly | Small-group virtual meetings |
| Emergency consultation | As needed | Phone/video within 24 hours |

### Professional Learning Community (PLC) Structure

FG2G educators form PLCs organized by grade band:

- **Monthly Meetings:** 90-minute structured sessions using a consistent protocol
- **Focus Areas:** Rotating through lesson study, student data analysis, garden planning, and personal regulation check-ins
- **Shared Resources:** Digital repository of lesson plans, student work samples, and garden documentation
- **Cross-Grade Collaboration:** Quarterly meetings across grade bands to ensure vertical alignment

## Assessment of Educator Development

### Competency Framework

| Domain | Beginning | Developing | Proficient | Distinguished |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trauma-Informed Practice | Understands core concepts | Applies principles with support | Consistently creates trauma-sensitive environments | Models and mentors others in trauma-informed practice |
| 5Rs Facilitation | Follows lesson plans as written | Adapts phases based on student needs | Fluidly moves between phases based on real-time assessment | Innovates within the framework while maintaining fidelity |
| Garden Integration | Maintains basic garden activities | Connects garden experiences to curriculum | Designs original garden-based learning experiences | Develops garden programs that extend beyond the classroom |
| TRACE Protocol | Uses protocol with prompts | Facilitates protocol independently | Differentiates protocol by student need | Trains others in TRACE facilitation |
| Regulation Assessment | Identifies obvious dysregulation | Uses scale with moderate accuracy | Assesses regulation levels with consistency and nuance | Calibrates assessment across team members |
| Co-Regulation | Maintains personal regulation | Co-regulates with individual students | Creates regulated classroom environments | Supports colleague regulation and school-wide culture |

### Evidence Collection

Educators document their growth through:

1. **Teaching Portfolio** — Video recordings, lesson plans, student work samples
2. **Garden Journal** — Personal garden practice documentation
3. **Regulation Log** — Self-monitoring of personal regulation patterns
4. **Student Impact Data** — Pre/post assessments, regulation tracking, academic outcomes
5. **Peer Feedback** — Structured observation reports from PLCs
6. **Reflective Narrative** — Annual growth essay connecting personal and professional development

## Program Outcomes

### Target Outcomes for Year 1 Participants

- 100% complete Foundational Training Intensive
- 85% demonstrate proficiency in 5Rs facilitation by year end
- 80% report increased confidence in trauma-informed practices
- 75% successfully establish functional garden learning spaces
- 90% report personal benefit from garden-based regulation practices
- Student outcomes show measurable improvement in regulation and academic engagement
