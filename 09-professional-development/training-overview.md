# Professional Development Training Overview

## Guiding Philosophy

The RootWork professional development program is built on a foundational conviction: **educators cannot implement trauma-informed practice without first developing their own regulation skills.** Just as we do not ask students to learn in a state of dysregulation, we do not ask educators to facilitate healing-centered instruction from a place of depletion, burnout, or unaddressed stress. The parallel process is intentional -- every strategy we teach educators to use with students, we first practice with educators themselves.

This philosophy rests on three interconnected principles:

1. **Embodied Knowledge Over Theoretical Knowledge.** Educators who have personally experienced grounding exercises, regulation strategies, and reflective protocols are far more effective at facilitating them than educators who have only read about them. Professional development sessions model the 5Rs Framework so that educators learn through doing.

2. **Sustainability Through Self-Care.** Trauma-informed education places significant emotional demands on practitioners. Without intentional attention to educator well-being, compassion fatigue and secondary traumatic stress undermine both teaching quality and retention. Our PD program treats educator wellness not as an add-on but as a prerequisite.

3. **Community of Practice.** Isolated implementation fails. Educators need a professional community that shares language, practices, and accountability. The RootWork PD program builds cohorts that grow together across a multi-year trajectory, fostering the relational trust that mirrors the safety we create for students.

## Training Structure: Three-Year Trajectory

### Year 1: Foundation (40 Hours)

Year 1 establishes the knowledge base and foundational skills required for RootWork implementation. Educators complete 40 hours of professional development distributed across the school year.

| Component | Hours | Format |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| Summer Institute (4 days) | 24 | In-person workshop + garden practicum |
| Fall Coaching Cycle (6 weeks) | 6 | Classroom observation + debrief |
| Mid-Year Retreat (1 day) | 6 | In-person workshop + reflection |
| Spring Peer Observation | 2 | Peer classroom visit + debrief |
| Online Module Completion | 2 | Self-paced digital learning |

**Year 1 Outcomes:**
- Understand the neuroscience of trauma and its impact on learning
- Demonstrate fluency with the 5Rs Framework and TRACE protocol
- Implement at least two garden-based lesson cycles per quarter
- Establish a personal regulation practice
- Complete the RootGuide platform onboarding sequence

### Year 2: Advanced Practice (20 Hours)

Year 2 deepens implementation fidelity and introduces advanced pedagogical strategies. Educators complete 20 hours of continuing professional development.

| Component | Hours | Format |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| Advanced Summer Workshop (2 days) | 12 | In-person workshop + garden practicum |
| Coaching Cycle (4 weeks) | 4 | Focused on differentiation + fidelity |
| Action Research Project | 2 | Site-based inquiry with peer support |
| Online Advanced Modules | 2 | Self-paced digital learning |

**Year 2 Outcomes:**
- Differentiate 5Rs implementation across diverse learner profiles
- Design original garden-based lessons aligned to grade-level standards
- Analyze student data from the RootGuide platform to adjust instruction
- Contribute to a school-based community of practice
- Begin mentoring new Year 1 educators informally

### Year 3: Leadership and Mentorship (20 Hours)

Year 3 prepares educators to serve as site-based leaders, PD facilitators, and mentors for incoming cohorts.

| Component | Hours | Format |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| Leadership Institute (2 days) | 12 | In-person facilitation training |
| Mentorship Practicum | 4 | Coaching a Year 1 educator |
| Conference Presentation or Publication | 2 | Professional contribution |
| Portfolio Defense | 2 | Evidence-based presentation |

**Year 3 Outcomes:**
- Facilitate PD sessions for colleagues using the RootWork model
- Mentor at least one Year 1 educator through a full coaching cycle
- Present implementation evidence at a school, district, or conference event
- Complete a Master Educator portfolio with student outcome data
- Contribute to curriculum refinement through action research findings

## Competency Domains

The RootWork PD program develops proficiency across six interconnected competency domains. Each domain includes specific knowledge indicators and performance benchmarks assessed through observation, portfolio evidence, and self-reflection.

### Domain 1: Trauma Science

- Understand the neurobiology of stress and trauma (ACEs, toxic stress, polyvagal theory)
- Recognize behavioral indicators of dysregulation in students
- Distinguish between trauma-informed response and traditional behavioral management
- Articulate the relationship between felt safety and cognitive availability
- Understand secondary traumatic stress and its impact on educator functioning

### Domain 2: 5Rs Facilitation

- Demonstrate fluency in all five phases (Root, Regulate, Reflect, Restore, Reconnect)
- Facilitate smooth transitions between phases based on student cues
- Adapt the 5Rs sequence to different content areas and grade bands
- Use the framework's language consistently in classroom discourse
- Design lesson plans that embed the 5Rs as a structural organizer

### Domain 3: Garden Pedagogy

- Maintain a classroom or school garden appropriate to grade band
- Integrate garden activities with academic content standards (GSE, CCSS, NGSS)
- Use the garden as a living laboratory for scientific inquiry, mathematical reasoning, and literacy development
- Facilitate garden-based regulation activities (sensory grounding, mindful observation)
- Manage garden logistics including seasonal planning, supply procurement, and safety protocols

### Domain 4: TRACE Protocol

- Facilitate the five TRACE moves (Think, Reason, Articulate, Check, Extend) across content areas
- Use TRACE questioning stems to scaffold student thinking
- Identify and name reasoning moves (Decompose, Compare, Question, Verify, Generalize, Transform, Connect) during student discourse
- Analyze TRACE engagement data from the RootGuide platform
- Design TRACE-aligned assessment tasks

### Domain 5: Regulation Strategies

- Maintain a personal regulation practice (breathing, grounding, mindfulness)
- Implement co-regulation strategies appropriate to student developmental level
- Teach self-regulation skills explicitly within the Regulate phase
- Create a physically and emotionally safe classroom environment
- Use the regulation continuum (co-regulation to self-regulation) to scaffold student independence

### Domain 6: Cultural Responsiveness

- Recognize how culture shapes expressions of distress, engagement, and learning
- Design garden-based lessons that honor students' cultural backgrounds and foodways
- Use culturally sustaining language in all 5Rs facilitation
- Engage families as partners using culturally responsive communication
- Examine personal biases and their impact on trauma-informed practice
- Incorporate diverse agricultural traditions and knowledge systems

## Training Modalities

### In-Person Workshops

Workshops are the primary modality for introducing new concepts, practicing strategies, and building community. All workshops model the 5Rs structure: beginning with a grounding activity (Root), checking in on educator wellness (Regulate), engaging with new content (Reflect), working through implementation challenges (Restore), and connecting to classroom practice (Reconnect).

**Workshop Norms:**
- Confidentiality: "What's shared here stays here; what's learned here leaves here."
- Choice: Educators always have the right to pass on sharing.
- Presence: Devices down during experiential activities.
- Vulnerability: Modeling risk-taking as facilitators and participants.

### Garden Practicum

Every PD cycle includes hands-on time in a demonstration garden. Educators work through the same garden activities they will facilitate with students, experiencing the sensory grounding, scientific inquiry, and collaborative problem-solving firsthand. The garden practicum is not optional -- it is a core component of embodied learning.

### Coaching Cycles

Each coaching cycle follows a three-part structure:

1. **Pre-Observation Conference (30 minutes):** Educator identifies a focus area and the coach reviews the planned lesson for 5Rs alignment.
2. **Classroom Observation (45-60 minutes):** Coach observes using the RootWork Observation Protocol, noting 5Rs facilitation, TRACE implementation, regulation strategies, and student engagement.
3. **Post-Observation Debrief (45 minutes):** Coach and educator review observation data together, celebrate strengths, and collaboratively identify next steps.

Coaching is non-evaluative. Coaches are peers or trained mentors, not administrators. This distinction is essential for creating the psychological safety required for honest reflection and growth.

### Peer Observation

Beginning in Year 1 and continuing throughout, educators observe colleagues implementing the 5Rs and provide structured feedback using the Peer Observation Protocol. This builds collective capacity, normalizes classroom visits, and provides diverse models of implementation.

### Online Modules

Self-paced digital modules supplement in-person learning and are accessed through the RootGuide platform. Modules include video demonstrations, interactive scenarios, knowledge checks, and reflection prompts. Each module is designed for 30-60 minutes of engagement and addresses specific competency indicators.

**Available Online Modules:**
- Trauma and the Developing Brain (Domain 1)
- The 5Rs in Action: Video Case Studies (Domain 2)
- Garden Planning for Your Grade Band (Domain 3)
- TRACE Protocol Deep Dive (Domain 4)
- Regulation Strategies Toolkit (Domain 5)
- Culturally Responsive Garden Pedagogy (Domain 6)
- RootGuide Platform Navigation (Technology)
- Data-Informed Instruction with RootGuide (Assessment)

## Differentiated Support

Not all educators enter the program with the same background or comfort level. The RootWork PD program provides differentiated support pathways:

- **New to Teaching:** Additional coaching hours and a designated mentor in Year 1.
- **Experienced, New to Trauma-Informed Practice:** Focused support on paradigm shift from compliance-based to regulation-based classroom management.
- **Experienced with Trauma-Informed Practice:** Accelerated pathway through foundational content; focus on garden integration and TRACE protocol.
- **Special Education Specialists:** Supplementary module on adapting the 5Rs for IEP-aligned instruction and related service integration.
- **Administrators:** Modified pathway focused on observation literacy, systems support, and creating conditions for implementation fidelity.

## Assessment of Educator Competency

Educator competency is assessed through multiple measures across the three-year trajectory:

| Measure | Frequency | Domains Assessed |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| Self-Assessment Survey | Quarterly | All 6 domains |
| Observation Protocol | 2-4x per year | Domains 2, 3, 4, 5 |
| Portfolio Artifacts | Ongoing | All 6 domains |
| Student Outcome Data | Semester | Domains 2, 3, 4 |
| Coaching Debrief Notes | Per cycle | Focus domain |
| Online Module Completion | As assigned | Assigned domain |

Competency is not scored on a pass/fail basis during Years 1 and 2. Instead, educators receive descriptive feedback and co-develop growth plans with their coach. Year 3 requires demonstration of proficiency across all six domains as part of the Master Educator portfolio defense.

## Connection to School Culture

Professional development does not exist in a vacuum. The RootWork PD program is designed to integrate with broader school culture initiatives:

- **Staff Meetings:** Monthly 15-minute "5Rs Check-In" embedded in existing staff meetings.
- **PLCs:** Professional Learning Communities adopt the 5Rs as a shared framework for instructional planning and student support conversations.
- **MTSS/PBIS Alignment:** The 5Rs align with Multi-Tiered Systems of Support, with Regulate strategies mapping to Tier 1 universal supports and targeted regulation interventions mapping to Tier 2.
- **Administrative Walkthroughs:** Administrators use a modified observation protocol to provide supportive, non-evaluative feedback on 5Rs implementation.

The goal is for the 5Rs to become the shared language of the entire school community -- not a program that operates in one classroom, but a framework that shapes how every adult interacts with every child.
