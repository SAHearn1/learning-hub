# PD Session Plans

## Overview

The RootWork professional development program comprises eight core sessions delivered during Year 1. Each session is three hours in length and follows the 5Rs structure: opening with Root (grounding), moving through Regulate (educator check-in), Reflect (new learning), Restore (working through challenges), and Reconnect (planning for classroom application). This parallel process ensures that educators experience the framework before they facilitate it.

All sessions include facilitator notes, participant materials lists, and reflection prompts. Sessions are designed for cohorts of 15-25 educators and require a trained RootWork facilitator.

---

## Session 1: Understanding Trauma and the Brain

### Objectives

By the end of this session, educators will be able to:
- Define trauma, toxic stress, and adverse childhood experiences (ACEs)
- Explain how trauma impacts brain development, particularly the prefrontal cortex, amygdala, and hippocampus
- Recognize behavioral indicators of dysregulation in students
- Distinguish between trauma-informed responses and punitive behavioral management
- Articulate the concept of "felt safety" and its relationship to learning readiness

### Agenda (3 Hours)

| Time | Phase | Activity |
|------|-------|----------|
| 0:00-0:20 | Root | Welcome and grounding: Guided sensory exercise with garden elements (soil, herbs, seeds). Introductions using "My Garden Name" activity. |
| 0:20-0:35 | Regulate | Personal check-in using the Regulation Thermometer. Facilitator models vulnerability by sharing their own regulation level. |
| 0:35-1:15 | Reflect | Interactive lecture: "The Brain Under Stress." Facilitator uses a brain model to demonstrate the impact of trauma on the amygdala, prefrontal cortex, and hippocampus. Video: "Still Face Experiment" and group discussion. |
| 1:15-1:30 | --- | Break |
| 1:30-2:10 | Reflect (cont.) | Case study activity: Small groups analyze student scenarios and identify behavioral indicators of hyperarousal, hypoarousal, and the window of tolerance. Gallery walk to share findings. |
| 2:10-2:40 | Restore | Paradigm shift exercise: Educators rewrite punitive responses as trauma-informed responses using the "From Compliance to Connection" worksheet. Open discussion about challenges and resistance. |
| 2:40-3:00 | Reconnect | Action planning: Each educator identifies one student and one classroom practice to approach differently this week. Closing circle with a grounding breath. |

### Materials

- Brain model (physical or projected diagram)
- Garden sensory kit (soil samples, fresh herbs, seeds, textured leaves)
- Regulation Thermometer handout
- "Still Face Experiment" video (3 minutes)
- Case study scenario cards (8 scenarios across grade bands)
- "From Compliance to Connection" worksheet
- Action planning template
- Participant journal

### Reflection Prompts

1. What did you learn about the brain today that changes how you understand a specific student's behavior?
2. When in your own life have you experienced being outside your window of tolerance? What helped you return?
3. What is one punitive practice in your classroom that you are now reconsidering?

## Session 2: The 5Rs Framework

### Objectives

By the end of this session, educators will be able to:
- Name and describe all five phases of the 5Rs Framework (Root, Regulate, Reflect, Restore, Reconnect)
- Explain the theoretical foundations underlying each phase
- Identify the garden metaphors associated with each phase
- Sequence a sample lesson using the 5Rs structure
- Articulate the difference between linear and responsive use of the framework

### Agenda (3 Hours)

| Time | Phase | Activity |
|------|-------|----------|
| 0:00-0:20 | Root | Garden walk: Educators spend 10 minutes in the demonstration garden observing plants at different growth stages. Return to share one observation that connects to learning. |
| 0:20-0:35 | Regulate | Breathing exercise: "Roots and Branches" (inhale-ground down, exhale-reach up). Brief partner share about readiness to learn today. |
| 0:35-1:15 | Reflect | Interactive presentation: Each phase introduced with its theoretical foundation, garden metaphor, and classroom example. Educators take notes using the "5Rs Field Guide" graphic organizer. |
| 1:15-1:30 | --- | Break |
| 1:30-2:15 | Reflect (cont.) | Lesson mapping activity: In grade-band teams, educators map an existing lesson onto the 5Rs structure. Teams present their mapped lessons and receive peer feedback. |
| 2:15-2:40 | Restore | Honest conversation: "Where will this be hard?" Educators identify anticipated barriers to implementation and co-develop solutions. Facilitator normalizes struggle as part of the learning process. |
| 2:40-3:00 | Reconnect | Commit to one full 5Rs lesson cycle before Session 3. Closing: Each educator names the phase they are most excited about and the phase that feels most challenging. |

### Materials

- Access to a demonstration garden or garden photos/video
- "5Rs Field Guide" graphic organizer
- 5Rs phase cards (large, laminated) for sorting and sequencing
- Sample lesson plans (one per grade band) for mapping activity
- Barrier/solution T-chart template
- Participant journal

### Reflection Prompts

1. Which phase of the 5Rs feels most natural to your current practice? Which feels most unfamiliar?
2. How does the garden metaphor deepen your understanding of the learning process?
3. What would it look like if your entire school day was structured around the 5Rs?

## Session 3: The TRACE Protocol in Practice

### Objectives

By the end of this session, educators will be able to:
- Facilitate all five TRACE moves (Think, Reason, Articulate, Check, Extend) in a content lesson
- Use TRACE questioning stems across content areas
- Identify and name the seven reasoning moves during student discourse
- Analyze a video of TRACE facilitation and provide feedback
- Plan a TRACE-embedded lesson for their own classroom

### Agenda (3 Hours)

| Time | Phase | Activity |
|------|-------|----------|
| 0:00-0:15 | Root | Mindful observation: Educators examine a garden specimen (leaf, flower, seed pod) and practice the "Think" move -- "What do you notice first?" Silent observation for 2 minutes, then share. |
| 0:15-0:30 | Regulate | Regulation check-in. Brief discussion: "How did slowing down to observe affect your internal state?" |
| 0:30-1:15 | Reflect | TRACE deep dive: Facilitator models a full TRACE sequence using a garden-based math problem. Educators experience the protocol as learners first. Debrief the experience. Then, explicit instruction on each move with questioning stem cards. |
| 1:15-1:30 | --- | Break |
| 1:30-2:10 | Reflect (cont.) | Video analysis: Watch two classroom clips of TRACE facilitation (one strong, one developing). Small groups use the TRACE Observation Rubric to analyze and discuss. Whole-group debrief. |
| 2:10-2:40 | Restore | Practice fishbowl: Volunteer educators facilitate a mini-TRACE sequence while colleagues observe and provide warm/cool feedback. Normalize imperfection as part of learning the protocol. |
| 2:40-3:00 | Reconnect | Lesson planning: Educators draft TRACE questioning stems for an upcoming lesson. Partner share and refine. Closing commitment to try TRACE with students this week. |

### Materials

- Garden specimens (leaves, flowers, seed pods, or high-quality photographs)
- TRACE questioning stem cards (one set per participant)
- Garden-based math problem for modeling
- Video clips of TRACE facilitation (2 clips, 5-7 minutes each)
- TRACE Observation Rubric
- Lesson planning template with TRACE stems
- Reasoning moves reference chart
- Participant journal

### Reflection Prompts

1. What was it like to experience the TRACE protocol as a learner? What did you notice about your own thinking?
2. Which reasoning moves do you already encourage in your classroom? Which are new?
3. How might TRACE change the nature of student discourse in your content area?

## Session 4: Garden as Classroom

### Objectives

By the end of this session, educators will be able to:
- Articulate the research base for garden-based learning
- Align garden activities with grade-level academic standards (GSE, CCSS, NGSS)
- Plan a seasonal garden calendar appropriate to their grade band
- Identify safety protocols and logistics for garden-based instruction
- Design a garden-based lesson that integrates at least two content areas

### Agenda (3 Hours)

| Time | Phase | Activity |
|------|-------|----------|
| 0:00-0:30 | Root | Extended garden immersion: Educators spend 20 minutes working in the demonstration garden (planting, weeding, watering, or harvesting depending on season). Grounding through direct contact with soil and plants. Return to share the experience. |
| 0:30-0:45 | Regulate | Body scan: After physical garden work, facilitator guides a brief body scan to notice how the body feels after outdoor engagement. Discussion of the regulation benefits of garden activity. |
| 0:45-1:15 | Reflect | Research review: Facilitator presents the evidence base for garden-based learning (academic achievement, SEL outcomes, health and nutrition, environmental stewardship). Educators annotate key findings in their journals. |
| 1:15-1:30 | --- | Break |
| 1:30-2:15 | Reflect (cont.) | Standards alignment workshop: In grade-band teams, educators use the RootWork Standards Alignment Matrix to identify where garden activities naturally connect to their content standards. Teams create a seasonal garden calendar with standards-aligned activities for each quarter. |
| 2:15-2:40 | Restore | Problem-solving stations: Rotating stations address common garden logistics challenges -- limited space (container gardens, vertical gardens), budget constraints (seed saving, community donations), seasonal limitations (indoor growing, winter garden activities), accessibility (raised beds, adaptive tools). |
| 2:40-3:00 | Reconnect | Lesson design: Each educator outlines a cross-curricular garden-based lesson for their grade band. Gallery walk to view designs. Closing: "One seed I'm planting this week." |

### Materials

- Demonstration garden access (or garden simulation materials for indoor settings)
- Garden tools, gloves, watering cans
- Research summary handout (key studies on garden-based learning outcomes)
- RootWork Standards Alignment Matrix (by grade band)
- Seasonal garden calendar template
- Problem-solving station materials (container garden samples, budget worksheets, seed catalogs, adaptive tool examples)
- Cross-curricular lesson design template
- Participant journal

### Reflection Prompts

1. How did working in the garden affect your energy, mood, and focus? What might this mean for your students?
2. Which academic standards in your content area are most naturally served by garden-based learning?
3. What is your biggest logistical barrier to garden-based instruction, and what is one step you can take to address it?

## Session 5: Regulation Strategies for Educators and Students

### Objectives

By the end of this session, educators will be able to:
- Describe the regulation continuum from co-regulation to self-regulation
- Demonstrate at least five regulation strategies appropriate for their grade band
- Identify their own stress responses and develop a personal regulation plan
- Explain the difference between regulation and compliance
- Create a classroom regulation toolkit with at least three sensory-based and three cognitive-based strategies

### Agenda (3 Hours)

| Time | Phase | Activity |
|------|-------|----------|
| 0:00-0:20 | Root | Sensory grounding circuit: Educators rotate through five stations (scented herbs, textured seeds, sound of water, visual focus on a plant, taste of a garden herb). Discuss how sensory input supports regulation. |
| 0:20-0:40 | Regulate | "My Stress Signature" activity: Educators complete a self-assessment identifying their physical, emotional, and behavioral stress indicators. Partner share. Facilitator discusses secondary traumatic stress and compassion fatigue as occupational realities. |
| 0:40-1:15 | Reflect | Strategy stations: Educators rotate through six regulation strategy stations, experiencing each strategy firsthand: (1) Deep breathing techniques, (2) Progressive muscle relaxation, (3) Grounding exercises with garden elements, (4) Movement-based regulation, (5) Journaling and expressive writing, (6) Guided imagery with nature themes. |
| 1:15-1:30 | --- | Break |
| 1:30-2:10 | Reflect (cont.) | Developmental lens: Facilitator presents the regulation continuum across grade bands. Small group work: Educators adapt each strategy for their specific grade band, considering developmental appropriateness, cultural responsiveness, and individual needs. |
| 2:10-2:40 | Restore | Personal regulation plan: Educators develop their own daily regulation practice (morning grounding, transition rituals, end-of-day decompression). Honest conversation about barriers to self-care in the teaching profession. Facilitator normalizes the struggle and co-develops solutions. |
| 2:40-3:00 | Reconnect | Classroom regulation toolkit design: Educators select strategies for their classroom toolkit and plan how to introduce them to students. Closing commitment: one personal regulation practice and one classroom strategy to implement this week. |

### Materials

- Sensory grounding circuit materials (herbs, seeds, water feature or recording, plants, edible herbs)
- "My Stress Signature" self-assessment handout
- Regulation strategy station materials (breathing guides, relaxation scripts, garden elements, movement cards, journals, imagery scripts)
- Regulation continuum developmental chart
- Personal regulation plan template
- Classroom regulation toolkit planning sheet
- Participant journal

### Reflection Prompts

1. Which regulation strategy resonated most with your body? Which felt most challenging?
2. What is your honest assessment of your current self-care practices? What is one realistic change you can make?
3. How do you currently respond when a student is dysregulated? How might that response change after today?

## Session 6: Culturally Responsive Garden Pedagogy

### Objectives

By the end of this session, educators will be able to:
- Define culturally responsive and culturally sustaining pedagogy in the context of garden-based learning
- Identify how culture shapes expressions of distress, engagement, and learning styles
- Incorporate diverse agricultural traditions and foodways into garden curriculum
- Design garden-based lessons that honor students' cultural backgrounds
- Examine personal biases and their impact on trauma-informed, culturally responsive practice
- Engage families as cultural knowledge holders in the garden program

### Agenda (3 Hours)

| Time | Phase | Activity |
|------|-------|----------|
| 0:00-0:20 | Root | "Seeds from Home" activity: Educators bring or choose from a collection of seeds/plants that hold cultural significance. Each person shares a brief story about a plant connected to their heritage, family, or community. |
| 0:20-0:35 | Regulate | Reflective breathing with intention: "Breathe in what you bring. Breathe out what you want to release." Brief journaling on feelings that arise when discussing culture and identity in professional settings. |
| 0:35-1:15 | Reflect | Interactive presentation: Culturally responsive pedagogy frameworks (Ladson-Billings, Paris & Alim) applied to garden-based learning. Case studies of garden programs that successfully integrated African, Indigenous, Latinx, and Asian agricultural traditions. Discussion of the danger of a "single story" garden curriculum. |
| 1:15-1:30 | --- | Break |
| 1:30-2:15 | Reflect (cont.) | Cultural audit activity: In grade-band teams, educators examine their current curriculum materials and garden plans for cultural representation, bias, and omission. Using the Cultural Responsiveness Audit Tool, teams identify gaps and generate specific strategies for greater inclusion. |
| 2:15-2:40 | Restore | Courageous conversation: Facilitated dialogue about the intersection of trauma, race, and culture. Using "Courageous Conversation" protocols (Singleton), educators explore how cultural identity impacts the trauma experience and the healing process. Facilitator holds space for discomfort and models repair. |
| 2:40-3:00 | Reconnect | Family engagement planning: Educators design one strategy for inviting families to share cultural knowledge in the garden program (family garden day, recipe sharing, seed exchange, oral history project). Closing: "One commitment I make to my students' cultural identities." |

### Materials

- Diverse seed and plant collection representing multiple cultural traditions
- Cultural Responsiveness Audit Tool
- Case study handouts (culturally integrated garden programs)
- Research summary on culturally responsive pedagogy frameworks
- "Courageous Conversation" protocol cards
- Family engagement planning template
- Participant journal

### Reflection Prompts

1. What cultural traditions around food, gardening, or nature shaped your own upbringing? How might those traditions inform or limit your practice?
2. Whose agricultural knowledge and traditions are currently missing from your garden curriculum?
3. What is one uncomfortable truth you confronted today about culture and bias in your practice?

## Session 7: Assessment and Portfolio Systems

### Objectives

By the end of this session, educators will be able to:
- Describe the RootWork multi-modal assessment philosophy (process over product, growth over proficiency)
- Use the RootWork Observation Protocol to assess 5Rs implementation
- Guide students in creating growth portfolios that document learning across domains
- Analyze data from the RootGuide platform to inform instruction
- Design performance-based assessments aligned to the 5Rs and garden-based learning

### Agenda (3 Hours)

| Time | Phase | Activity |
|------|-------|----------|
| 0:00-0:15 | Root | "Growth Evidence" grounding: Educators bring one artifact from their own implementation journey (photo, student work sample, journal entry). Brief sharing of what the artifact represents. |
| 0:15-0:30 | Regulate | Regulation check-in. Brief discussion: "What does the word 'assessment' bring up for you emotionally?" Acknowledging the stress and pressure often associated with assessment in education. |
| 0:30-1:15 | Reflect | Assessment philosophy presentation: The RootWork approach to assessment -- trauma-informed, culturally responsive, multi-modal. Introduction to four assessment tools: (1) Growth Portfolios, (2) TRACE Engagement Rubric, (3) SEL Progress Monitoring, (4) Garden Learning Documentation. Facilitator models each tool with examples. |
| 1:15-1:30 | --- | Break |
| 1:30-2:10 | Reflect (cont.) | Hands-on practice: Educators use the TRACE Engagement Rubric to score a video sample of student work. Calibration exercise to ensure inter-rater reliability. Then, educators explore the RootGuide platform's data dashboard and practice generating reports. |
| 2:10-2:40 | Restore | Assessment challenge workshop: Small groups tackle common assessment challenges -- grading garden work, documenting SEL growth, communicating progress to families, aligning with district grading policies. Groups share solutions. |
| 2:40-3:00 | Reconnect | Portfolio launch plan: Educators develop a plan for introducing growth portfolios to their students. Timeline, artifact selection criteria, reflection prompts, and celebration strategies. Closing: "One way I will honor student growth this week." |

### Materials

- Educator artifacts (self-brought)
- Assessment philosophy presentation slides
- Growth Portfolio sample (complete student portfolio from a pilot classroom)
- TRACE Engagement Rubric (print and digital)
- Video sample of student work for calibration exercise
- RootGuide platform access (laptops or tablets)
- Assessment challenge scenario cards
- Portfolio launch planning template
- Participant journal

### Reflection Prompts

1. How does a growth-oriented assessment philosophy change what you value and notice in student work?
2. What is one assessment practice you currently use that may not be trauma-informed? How might you modify it?
3. How will you help students see their portfolios as celebrations of growth rather than collections of grades?

## Session 8: RootGuide Technology Integration

### Objectives

By the end of this session, educators will be able to:
- Navigate all educator-facing features of the RootGuide platform
- Set up a class roster, assign lessons, and monitor student progress
- Use the AI lesson plan generator to create 5Rs-aligned lessons
- Interpret data visualizations and analytics dashboards
- Troubleshoot common technical issues and support students with platform navigation
- Understand data privacy protocols and FERPA/COPPA compliance

### Agenda (3 Hours)

| Time | Phase | Activity |
|------|-------|----------|
| 0:00-0:15 | Root | "Tech feelings" check-in: Educators place themselves on a spectrum from "excited" to "anxious" about technology integration. Grounding exercise to acknowledge that technology can be both a powerful tool and a source of stress. |
| 0:15-0:30 | Regulate | Brief breathing exercise. Facilitator establishes a "no judgment" norm for the session -- all levels of tech comfort are welcome and honored. |
| 0:30-1:15 | Reflect | Guided platform tour: Facilitator walks educators through the RootGuide platform in real time. Educators follow along on their own devices. Key features covered: class setup, curriculum library, lesson assignment, student progress monitoring, AI lesson plan generator. Pause points for questions. |
| 1:15-1:30 | --- | Break |
| 1:30-2:15 | Reflect (cont.) | Hands-on practice: Educators complete a structured set of platform tasks: (1) Create or verify class roster, (2) Browse the curriculum library and assign a lesson, (3) Generate a lesson plan using the AI tool, (4) Review a simulated student dashboard, (5) Generate a parent-facing progress report. Facilitator and tech support circulate to assist. |
| 2:15-2:40 | Restore | Troubleshooting workshop: Common issues and solutions. Educators practice the "tech triage" protocol: (1) Check the Help Center, (2) Ask a colleague, (3) Submit a support ticket. Discussion of digital equity and ensuring all students have access. Data privacy review: FERPA, COPPA, and RootWork's data handling policies. |
| 2:40-3:00 | Reconnect | Integration planning: Educators plan their first week of RootGuide-supported instruction. When will students access the platform? What lessons will they complete? How will the educator monitor progress? Closing: "One way technology will support my students' growth." |

### Materials

- Laptops or tablets for each educator (with RootGuide platform access)
- RootGuide Quick Start Guide (printed)
- Platform task checklist
- Simulated student data for dashboard exploration
- Troubleshooting guide and FAQ
- Data privacy policy summary
- Integration planning template
- Tech support contact information
- Participant journal

### Reflection Prompts

1. How does the RootGuide platform support the 5Rs Framework? Where do you see alignment?
2. What is your biggest concern about technology integration, and what is one step you can take to address it?
3. How will you ensure that the technology serves the human connection at the heart of this curriculum, rather than replacing it?

## Session Sequencing and Scheduling Guidance

### Recommended Schedule

| Session | Recommended Timing | Prerequisite |
|---------|-------------------|--------------|
| Session 1: Understanding Trauma and the Brain | Summer Institute Day 1 | None |
| Session 2: The 5Rs Framework | Summer Institute Day 2 | Session 1 |
| Session 3: The TRACE Protocol in Practice | Summer Institute Day 3 | Session 2 |
| Session 4: Garden as Classroom | Summer Institute Day 4 | Session 2 |
| Session 5: Regulation Strategies | Fall, Week 3-4 | Sessions 1-2 |
| Session 6: Culturally Responsive Garden Pedagogy | Fall, Week 6-8 | Sessions 1-4 |
| Session 7: Assessment and Portfolio Systems | Mid-Year Retreat | Sessions 1-6 |
| Session 8: RootGuide Technology Integration | Mid-Year Retreat | Sessions 1-3 |

### Adaptation Notes

- Sessions 3 and 4 can be delivered in either order.
- Sessions 5 and 6 may be scheduled during the Summer Institute if a 5-day format is available.
- Session 8 may be moved earlier if the school is launching the RootGuide platform at the start of the year.
- All sessions can be extended to 4 hours for deeper engagement if scheduling permits.
- Virtual adaptations are available for Sessions 1, 2, 5, and 8; Sessions 3, 4, 6, and 7 are strongly recommended for in-person delivery due to their experiential components.
