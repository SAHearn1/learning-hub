# Rootwork Regulation Pods

## Overview

Rootwork Regulation Pods are semi-enclosed spaces within the Living Learning Lab designed specifically to support emotional regulation. When a student needs to move from academic engagement into the Regulate phase of the 5Rs framework, the regulation pod provides a safe, sensory-rich environment for co-regulation with an adult or independent self-regulation practice.

Regulation pods are not "time-out" spaces. They are intentionally designed therapeutic environments rooted in polyvagal theory and sensory integration research. A student who uses a regulation pod is engaging in active learning -- learning about their own nervous system and developing the self-regulation skills that are prerequisite to academic success.

## Design Principles

### Safety and Containment
- The pod provides a sense of enclosure without full closure -- students should feel held, not trapped
- An adult can see into the pod at all times (no fully enclosed or locked spaces)
- Materials are soft, non-breakable, and non-threatening
- Exit is always available; students are never required to stay

### Sensory Regulation
- The pod environment is designed to bring the nervous system back to baseline
- Sensory inputs are intentional: calming textures, scents, sounds, and visual elements
- Students can modulate sensory input based on their needs (add or remove stimuli)

### Predictability
- The pod is always available, always in the same location, always stocked with the same materials
- Consistent environment reduces anxiety about what to expect
- Clear, simple procedures for entering and leaving the pod

### Choice and Agency
- Students choose whether to use the pod (it is offered, never mandated)
- Within the pod, students select from available regulation tools
- Students determine when they are ready to leave

## Physical Specifications

### Structure Options

**Option A: Garden Gazebo Pod**
- 6' x 6' or 8' x 8' open-sided gazebo with partial walls or curtain panels
- Natural wood or metal frame
- Weather-resistant fabric panels that can be drawn for privacy or opened for ventilation
- Placement under existing tree canopy or with dedicated shade structure

**Option B: Living Wall Pod**
- Semi-circular arrangement of tall planters (ornamental grasses, bamboo, or evergreen hedging)
- Creates a natural enclosure 6-8' in diameter
- Pea gravel or mulch floor
- Optional overhead shade from a pergola or sail

**Option C: Indoor-Adjacent Pod**
- Located just inside a doorway adjacent to the garden, in a hallway alcove, or classroom corner
- Fabric canopy or tent structure creating a defined space
- 4' x 6' minimum footprint
- Acoustic dampening panels to reduce ambient noise

### Interior Elements

**Seating:**
- Bean bag chair or floor cushions (washable covers)
- Rocking chair or glider (rhythmic motion supports regulation)
- Hammock chair (proprioceptive input through gentle swinging)

**Sensory Tools (Regulation Toolkit):**
| Tool | Sensory Channel | Regulation Purpose |
|------|----------------|-------------------|
| Weighted lap pad (3-5 lbs) | Proprioceptive | Grounding, calming |
| Smooth stones from the garden | Tactile | Grounding, focus |
| Fidget tools (spinners, cubes) | Tactile/motor | Discharge nervous energy |
| Noise-canceling headphones | Auditory | Reduce overstimulation |
| Guided audio meditations | Auditory | Structured calming |
| Lavender sachets | Olfactory | Calming, sensory grounding |
| Visual timer (sand timer) | Visual | Predictability, self-pacing |
| Drawing supplies | Motor/visual | Expressive regulation |
| Growth journal | Motor/cognitive | Reflection, processing |

**Visual Supports:**
- Regulation scale poster (1-10 or color-coded)
- Breathing technique cards with visual instructions
- Menu of available regulation strategies
- "I feel... I need..." sentence starters

## Regulation Pod Procedures

### Entry Protocol
1. Student or educator identifies need for regulation support.
2. Educator offers the pod: "Would you like some time in the regulation pod?"
3. Student transitions to the pod at their own pace.
4. Student selects tools from the regulation toolkit.
5. Educator remains nearby and available but does not hover.

### During Pod Use
- No academic demands are placed on the student.
- Educator checks in at intervals (every 3-5 minutes) with brief, non-intrusive language: "Just checking in. Take the time you need."
- If the student requests adult presence, the educator sits with them and models calm breathing or engages in quiet co-regulation.
- Maximum pod use in a single episode: 15-20 minutes. If longer regulation is needed, consider escalation protocol.

### Exit Protocol
1. Student signals readiness (verbal, thumbs-up, or returning materials to their places).
2. Educator conducts a brief re-grounding check: "How are you feeling now? On a scale of 1-10?"
3. Student returns to the Root phase of the learning session for a fresh start.
4. Educator documents the regulation event in the student's record.

### Emergency Considerations
- If a student shows signs of crisis (self-harm risk, extreme distress, dissociation lasting more than 10 minutes), follow the school's crisis response protocol.
- The regulation pod is not a substitute for professional mental health support.
- Persistent regulation pod use (3+ times per week for the same student) should trigger a team review and possible referral.

## Grade Band Adaptations

### K-2: "The Cozy Corner"
- Emphasis on soft textures, stuffed animals, picture books
- Adult co-regulation is the primary strategy
- Simple visual regulation scale (sunshine/cloud/rain)
- Transition songs to mark entering and leaving the space
- Duration: 3-10 minutes typical

### 3-5: "The Quiet Garden"
- Expanded toolkit including journaling and drawing
- Introduction of self-selected regulation strategies
- Numbered regulation scale (1-5)
- Student begins to practice independent regulation with adult nearby
- Duration: 5-15 minutes typical

### 6-8: "The Regulation Station"
- Full toolkit including guided audio, breathing exercises, and journaling prompts
- Students develop personal regulation plans
- Self-assessment using regulation scale (1-10)
- Peer regulation partnerships (trained students can support each other)
- Duration: 5-15 minutes typical, self-paced

### 9-12: "The Reset Space"
- Student-directed use with minimal adult intervention
- Mindfulness and meditation resources
- Reflection journaling connected to metacognitive development
- Students may mentor younger students in regulation strategies
- Duration: student-paced with check-in at 15 minutes

## Connection to the 5Rs Framework

The regulation pod sits at the intersection of the Root and Regulate phases:

- **Root**: The pod provides the safety and grounding that the Root phase establishes at the start of each session. A student leaving the pod re-enters the Root phase.
- **Regulate**: The pod is the primary physical space for Tier 2 and Tier 3 regulation interventions.
- **Reflect**: What happens in the pod is itself a form of learning -- students are developing self-awareness, emotional vocabulary, and self-regulation skills.
- **Restore**: The pod can be used after an error-related emotional reaction, giving the student space to process before re-engaging with Restore phase error analysis.
- **Reconnect**: Over time, students connect their regulation experiences to broader life skills: "I used my breathing technique before my basketball game this weekend."

## Data Collection and Continuous Improvement

### Track per student:
- Frequency of pod use (daily, weekly, monthly trends)
- Duration of each pod visit
- Regulation level at entry and exit
- Strategies used and their effectiveness
- Triggers identified (academic, social, environmental)
- Progress toward self-regulation milestones

### Track per lab:
- Overall pod utilization rates
- Peak usage times (time of day, day of week)
- Most frequently used regulation tools
- Average regulation recovery time
- Correlation between pod use and academic engagement

This data informs individual support plans, IEP goals, program evaluation, and resource allocation decisions.
