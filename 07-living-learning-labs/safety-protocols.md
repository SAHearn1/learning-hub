# Safety Protocols

## Overview

Safety in the Living Learning Lab encompasses physical safety, environmental health, emotional safety, and emergency preparedness. These protocols ensure that the garden environment remains a space of healing and growth, not a source of additional stress or harm. All safety protocols are designed with trauma-informed principles: clear expectations communicated in advance, predictable routines, and student agency wherever possible.

## Physical Safety

### General Rules
1. Walk, don't run, in the garden at all times.
2. Carry tools at your side, pointed down, never overhead.
3. Wash hands before eating anything from the garden and after handling soil.
4. Wear closed-toe shoes in the garden (no sandals or bare feet).
5. Use sunscreen and wear hats during warm-weather sessions.
6. Drink water regularly; hydration station available at all times.

### Tool Safety

#### Student-Appropriate Tools
Students may use the following tools with standard supervision:
- Hand trowels
- Hand cultivators and weeders
- Watering cans and hose (with nozzle)
- Rakes (age-appropriate size)
- Harvest baskets and buckets
- Measuring tools (tape measures, rulers, scales)
- Magnifying glasses and observation tools

#### Restricted Tools (Direct Adult Supervision Required)
The following tools require an adult present and directly supervising:
- Pruning shears and loppers
- Full-size shovels and spading forks
- Wheelbarrows (younger students)
- Any cutting tools for harvest preparation

#### Prohibited for Student Use
- Power tools of any kind
- Chainsaws, hedge trimmers
- Chemical sprayers or applicators
- Heavy machinery

### Tool Safety Orientation
Before their first garden session, all students receive a tool safety orientation:

1. **Proper carrying**: Demonstrate how to carry each tool safely (at side, pointed down)
2. **Proper use**: Show correct technique for each tool
3. **Personal space**: Maintain arm's length from others when using tools
4. **Return protocol**: Clean and return tools to designated storage after every use
5. **Damage reporting**: Report any broken or damaged tools immediately to the educator

## Environmental Safety

### Soil Safety
- Conduct baseline soil testing for lead and heavy metals before establishing any garden where students will grow food
- If lead levels exceed 400 ppm, use raised beds with imported soil only
- Do not grow food in soil near buildings painted before 1978 (lead paint risk)
- Use only organic, OMRI-listed amendments and fertilizers
- No synthetic pesticides, herbicides, or fungicides in areas where students work

### Water Safety
- Use potable water sources only for irrigation of edible crops and handwashing
- Test rain barrel water before use on edible crops (appropriate for ornamentals and non-food plants)
- Ensure no standing water persists longer than 48 hours (mosquito breeding prevention)
- Drain and clean water features weekly

### Plant Safety
- Remove or fence off any existing toxic plants in the garden area before student use
- Do not plant known toxic species (oleander, foxglove, nightshade, castor bean, poison hemlock)
- Teach students to never eat any plant or berry unless an adult has confirmed it is safe and it has been properly washed
- Maintain a list of all planted species with toxicity status

### Allergen Awareness
- Review student health records for known allergies before garden activities (bee stings, plant contact allergies, food allergies)
- Have epinephrine auto-injectors (EpiPens) on-site for students with known anaphylaxis risk
- Identify and manage bee/wasp nesting areas (relocate if near student activity zones)
- Inform students about plants that cause contact dermatitis (poison ivy identification, stinging nettle)
- Stock antihistamine cream and provide access to indoor handwashing

### Weather Safety
- **Heat**: Cancel or modify outdoor activities when heat index exceeds 100 degrees F. Ensure shade and water access. Watch for signs of heat exhaustion.
- **Cold**: Provide gloves and appropriate clothing guidance. Limit exposure in temperatures below 40 degrees F.
- **Lightning**: Move all students indoors at the first sign of lightning or thunder. Wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before returning outside.
- **High wind**: Secure loose materials; cancel garden sessions in sustained winds above 25 mph.
- **Rain**: Light rain is acceptable for garden work (students often enjoy it). Move indoors during heavy rain or storms.

## Food Safety

### Harvest Handling
1. Wash all produce thoroughly before consumption using potable water.
2. Use clean cutting boards and utensils for any food preparation.
3. Students wash hands with soap and water before handling harvested food.
4. Consume harvested produce the same day whenever possible.
5. Refrigerate produce that will not be consumed immediately.

### Preparation Areas
- Maintain a clean, food-safe preparation surface in the harvest area.
- Sanitize all preparation surfaces before and after use.
- Separate cutting boards for different food types (if preparing multiple items).
- Child-safe cutting tools only; direct adult supervision for any cutting activities.

### Allergy Protocol for Food Activities
1. Before any tasting or cooking activity, review the allergy list for all participating students.
2. Clearly label all prepared items with ingredients.
3. Never require a student to eat or taste anything.
4. Provide alternatives for students with known food allergies.
5. Keep allergen-free zones for students with airborne allergies (e.g., nut dust).

## Emotional Safety

### Trauma-Informed Safety Practices
The Living Learning Lab must be an emotionally safe space:

- **No shaming language**: Never use garden responsibilities as punishment ("You have to pull weeds because you misbehaved")
- **Predictable structure**: Post and follow a consistent routine for garden sessions
- **Choice and control**: Offer options within activities ("Would you like to water or weed today?")
- **Safe boundaries**: Respect students who do not want to touch certain materials (soil, insects, etc.)
- **Quiet availability**: The regulation pod is always accessible; no student should ever be denied access

### Supervision Ratios
| Grade Band | Minimum Ratio | Maximum Group Size |
|------------|--------------|-------------------|
| K-2 | 1 adult : 6 students | 12 students |
| 3-5 | 1 adult : 8 students | 16 students |
| 6-8 | 1 adult : 10 students | 20 students |
| 9-12 | 1 adult : 12 students | 24 students |

Additional adults required for students with IEP-specified support needs.

## Emergency Procedures

### First Aid
- A stocked first aid kit must be on-site during every garden session
- At least one staff member present must have current first aid/CPR certification
- First aid kit contents: adhesive bandages, gauze, antiseptic wipes, tweezers (for splinters), instant cold packs, antihistamine cream, gloves, emergency contact list

### Injury Protocol
1. Assess the injury -- minor (scrapes, small cuts, insect bites) or serious (deep cuts, allergic reactions, broken bones, heat stroke)
2. **Minor**: Administer first aid, document in incident log, inform parent/guardian at pickup
3. **Serious**: Call 911 if needed, administer first aid, contact school nurse and administration, notify parent/guardian immediately, complete incident report within 24 hours

### Emergency Contacts
Maintain a binder at the garden entrance with:
- Emergency contact numbers for all students
- School nurse contact
- Nearest hospital address and phone number
- Poison control number: 1-800-222-1222
- Local cooperative extension (for plant identification questions)

### Evacuation
- Establish a garden-specific evacuation route to the nearest building
- Practice evacuation once per semester as part of the school's overall drill schedule
- Maintain a headcount at the start and end of every garden session

## Annual Safety Checklist

Complete this checklist at the start of each school year and after any significant changes to the garden:

- [ ] Soil tested for lead and contaminants
- [ ] All toxic plants removed or fenced
- [ ] Irrigation system inspected and functional
- [ ] Tool inventory inspected for damage
- [ ] First aid kit stocked and accessible
- [ ] Student allergy records reviewed
- [ ] Emergency contact binder updated
- [ ] Shade structures secure and in good condition
- [ ] Pathways clear and accessible (ADA compliant)
- [ ] Fencing and gates secure and lockable
- [ ] Compost system inspected and managed
- [ ] Staff first aid/CPR certifications current
- [ ] Student safety orientation materials updated
- [ ] Supervision ratio plan documented
- [ ] Emergency evacuation route posted
