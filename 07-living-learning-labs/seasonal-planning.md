# Seasonal Planning

## Overview

The Living Learning Lab operates on a seasonal rhythm that mirrors both the agricultural calendar and the academic year. Effective seasonal planning ensures that garden activities align with curriculum standards, that crops are planted and harvested during the school calendar, and that the 5Rs framework is consistently integrated throughout the year.

This guide is calibrated for the southeastern United States (USDA Hardiness Zones 7b-8a, typical of Georgia), but includes adaptation notes for other regions. Local cooperative extension offices provide region-specific planting dates and variety recommendations.

## Seasonal Calendar Overview

| Season | Months | Garden Focus | 5Rs Emphasis |
|--------|--------|-------------|--------------|
| Fall | Aug - Nov | Cool-season planting, garden establishment | Root & Reflect |
| Winter | Dec - Feb | Planning, indoor activities, cover crops | Reflect & Restore |
| Spring | Mar - May | Warm-season planting, peak growth | All 5Rs in full cycle |
| Summer | Jun - Jul | Maintenance, harvest, planning ahead | Reconnect |

## Fall Semester (August - November)

### August: Garden Launch

**Garden Activities:**
- Conduct soil testing with students (science: data collection and analysis)
- Prepare beds: clear summer weeds, add compost, amend soil based on test results
- Plant fall crops: lettuce, spinach, kale, broccoli, radishes, carrots, beets, peas
- Establish herb garden: parsley, cilantro, dill (fall herbs)
- Set up student garden journals

**Curriculum Connections:**
- Math: Measuring bed dimensions, calculating area and perimeter, estimating soil volume
- Science: Soil composition, pH testing, plant life cycles, seed germination requirements
- Language Arts: Procedural writing (how to prepare a garden bed), observation journaling

**5Rs Integration:**
- Root: Establish garden rituals and routines; first grounding experiences in the garden
- Reflect: Soil science investigations using the TRACE protocol

### September: Growing Season

**Garden Activities:**
- Monitor fall crop growth; thin seedlings as needed
- Begin data collection: measure plant height, leaf count, and growth rate weekly
- Start composting program; build or activate compost bins
- Plant cover crops in unused beds (crimson clover, winter rye)

**Curriculum Connections:**
- Math: Graphing growth data, calculating growth rates, ratio and proportion in soil mixing
- Science: Photosynthesis, plant anatomy, composting science (decomposition, microorganisms)
- Language Arts: Descriptive writing about garden observations, compare/contrast plant varieties

**5Rs Integration:**
- Reflect: Data collection and analysis using TRACE protocol
- Restore: First experiences with garden setbacks (seeds that don't germinate, pest damage) -- reframe as learning opportunities

### October: Mid-Season and Harvest

**Garden Activities:**
- First harvests of fast-growing crops (radishes, lettuce, spinach)
- Weigh and record harvest yields
- Continue data collection on remaining crops
- Identify and manage fall pests (aphids, cabbage worms)
- Prepare garden for cooler temperatures (row covers, cold frames)

**Curriculum Connections:**
- Math: Harvest weights, yield calculations, cost-benefit analysis (input costs vs. harvest value)
- Science: Pest identification, integrated pest management, seasonal temperature changes
- Language Arts: Persuasive writing (why school gardens matter), recipe writing for harvested produce

**5Rs Integration:**
- Reconnect: Share harvest with community; students present garden data to other classes
- Root: Mid-semester regulation check-in; adjust garden activities based on student needs

### November: Season Transition

**Garden Activities:**
- Final fall harvests
- Plant garlic and overwintering crops (collards, kale can continue through mild winters)
- Mulch beds for winter protection
- Clean and store tools
- End-of-season garden assessment with students

**Curriculum Connections:**
- Math: End-of-season data analysis; compile and interpret full growth datasets
- Science: Seasonal change, dormancy, frost and its effect on plants
- Language Arts: Reflective writing on the growing season, growth journal review

**5Rs Integration:**
- Reflect: Students analyze their full dataset from the semester
- Reconnect: Celebration of growth -- both plants and thinking

## Winter Period (December - February)

### December: Planning and Indoor Activities

**Garden Activities:**
- Seed catalog review and spring garden planning
- Indoor seed starting (if grow lights available): herbs, flowers, early spring crops
- Worm bin maintenance and observation
- Garden design projects (planning spring layouts)

**Curriculum Connections:**
- Math: Budget planning for spring garden, scale drawing of garden layouts, seed quantity calculations
- Science: Germination experiments (comparing seed types, light conditions, temperature)
- Language Arts: Research writing on garden topics, letter writing to seed companies or community partners

### January: Deep Planning

**Garden Activities:**
- Finalize spring planting calendar
- Order seeds and supplies
- Repair and maintain garden infrastructure (beds, irrigation, fencing)
- Indoor activities: container gardening, windowsill herbs, sprout growing

**Curriculum Connections:**
- Math: Calendar math, scheduling, supply ordering with quantity and cost calculations
- Science: Plant taxonomy, seed anatomy, genetics (heirloom vs. hybrid varieties)
- Language Arts: Technical writing (garden plan documents), persuasive writing (grant applications)

### February: Pre-Season Preparation

**Garden Activities:**
- Start warm-season transplants indoors (tomatoes, peppers, herbs) -- 6-8 weeks before last frost
- Conduct spring soil testing
- Turn compost; assess compost readiness
- Prune perennial herbs and plants
- Clean and organize tool storage

**Curriculum Connections:**
- Math: Counting back from frost dates, temperature data analysis, germination rate calculations
- Science: Soil science review, composting cycle completion, seed germination tracking
- Language Arts: Instructional writing (planting guides), spring garden journal entries

## Spring Semester (March - May)

### March: Spring Planting Begins

**Garden Activities:**
- Direct seed cool-season crops for spring: peas, lettuce, spinach, radishes, carrots
- Transplant cool-weather seedlings: broccoli, kale, cabbage
- Install or activate irrigation systems
- Resume weekly data collection

**Curriculum Connections:**
- Math: Spacing calculations, seed depth ratios, irrigation flow rate measurement
- Science: Germination conditions, soil temperature monitoring, weather data collection
- Language Arts: Observation journaling, hypothesis writing for garden experiments

**5Rs Integration:**
- Root: Spring re-grounding; establish new semester garden rituals
- Reflect: New experiments and data collection cycles

### April: Peak Growth

**Garden Activities:**
- Transplant warm-season crops after last frost: tomatoes, peppers, squash, cucumbers, basil
- Succession planting of fast-growing crops
- Pollinator observation and documentation
- Pest monitoring and IPM implementation

**Curriculum Connections:**
- Math: Area and volume calculations for new plantings, data graphing, ratio and proportion
- Science: Pollination, ecosystems, beneficial insects, weather and climate
- Language Arts: Scientific observation reports, poetry inspired by the garden

**5Rs Integration:**
- Full 5Rs cycle in every session: the garden provides natural contexts for all phases
- Restore: Spring pest challenges provide authentic error-analysis opportunities

### May: Late Spring Harvest and Celebration

**Garden Activities:**
- Harvest spring crops (peas, lettuce, radishes, herbs)
- Plant summer succession crops
- Prepare summer maintenance plan
- End-of-year garden celebration and showcase

**Curriculum Connections:**
- Math: Full-season data analysis, yield comparison, growth rate calculations
- Science: Plant reproduction, seed saving, seasonal transitions
- Language Arts: Presentation preparation, reflective essays, portfolio compilation

**5Rs Integration:**
- Reconnect: End-of-year celebration of learning; community harvest events
- Reflect: Portfolio review and metacognitive reflection on the year's growth

## Summer Maintenance (June - July)

### Options for Summer Care
1. **Summer program integration**: If the school runs a summer program, integrate garden care into activities
2. **Volunteer schedule**: Establish a rotation of families, community members, and staff for weekly maintenance
3. **Automated irrigation**: Ensure drip systems are functional and on timers
4. **Cover cropping**: Plant heat-tolerant cover crops (buckwheat, cowpeas) to protect and improve soil
5. **Community partnerships**: Partner with local community gardens, youth programs, or master gardener groups for summer stewardship

### Pre-Summer Preparation
Before the school year ends:
- Set up and test automated irrigation
- Apply heavy mulch to reduce water needs and suppress weeds
- Harvest any remaining spring crops
- Document the current state of the garden with photos and notes
- Distribute summer volunteer sign-up schedule

## Adaptation Notes

### Northern Climates (Zones 3-6)
- Shift fall planting to early September
- Extend indoor growing season (grow lights, cold frames, greenhouses)
- Focus winter months on planning, indoor experiments, and container gardening
- Spring planting may not begin until late April or May

### Southern and Subtropical Climates (Zones 9-10)
- Year-round growing possible; adjust for extreme heat rather than cold
- Summer may be the "fallow" season due to heat stress
- Plant warm-season crops in early spring and late summer
- Cool-season crops thrive October through March

### Arid Climates
- Emphasize water conservation, xeriscaping, and drought-tolerant varieties
- Use shade cloth during peak summer heat
- Focus on water-efficient irrigation (drip systems, ollas)
- Select crops adapted to dry conditions
