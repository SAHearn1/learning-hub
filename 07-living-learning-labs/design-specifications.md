# Living Learning Lab Design Specifications

## Overview

Living Learning Labs are outdoor and semi-outdoor educational spaces where the RootWork curriculum's garden-based pedagogy comes to life. These labs serve as the physical environment for therapeutic horticulture, hands-on academic instruction, regulation support, and community engagement. Every design decision prioritizes trauma-informed principles: safety, predictability, choice, collaboration, and empowerment.

A well-designed Living Learning Lab is not simply a school garden -- it is an intentionally constructed learning environment where every element serves both a horticultural and a pedagogical purpose.

## Site Selection Criteria

### Essential Requirements
- **Minimum area**: 2,000 square feet for a single-class lab; 5,000+ square feet for a multi-class or school-wide installation
- **Sunlight**: Minimum 6 hours of direct sunlight during the growing season
- **Water access**: Within 100 feet of a potable water source with hose bib or irrigation connection
- **Drainage**: Adequate drainage to prevent standing water; avoid low-lying areas prone to flooding
- **Proximity**: Within a 3-minute walk from primary classrooms to facilitate transition without excessive disruption
- **Visibility**: Visible from at least one staff-occupied building for passive supervision

### Preferred Features
- Southern or southeastern exposure for maximum sun
- Windbreak on the north and west sides (fence, building, or existing vegetation)
- Existing shade for regulation pod placement
- Level or gently sloping terrain
- Proximity to a storage area for tools and supplies

### Avoid
- Sites with known soil contamination (test soil before installation)
- Areas beneath power lines or over underground utilities
- Locations with poor security or frequent vandalism
- Sites with excessive shade from buildings or trees
- Flood-prone or poorly drained areas

## Lab Zones

The Living Learning Lab is organized into distinct functional zones, each aligned with the 5Rs framework:

### Zone 1: Transition Gateway (Root Phase)
The entry point to the lab, designed to facilitate the transition from classroom to garden and activate the Root phase grounding process.

- **Entrance arbor or archway**: A physical threshold marking the transition into the learning environment
- **Welcome board**: Displays the day's activities, weather, and a grounding prompt
- **Handwashing station**: Hygiene and sensory grounding (warm water when possible)
- **Supply station**: Aprons, gloves, tools organized in labeled bins for student self-selection
- **Check-in area**: A small seating area with visual regulation check-in tools

### Zone 2: Production Garden (Reflect Phase)
The primary growing area where academic content integrates with horticultural practice.

- **Raised beds**: 4' x 8' standard size, 24-30" height for accessibility, with 36" pathways for wheelchair access
- **In-ground plots**: Individual student plots (4' x 4') for personal responsibility and long-term observation
- **Row crops**: Larger area for seasonal vegetables connecting to nutrition and culinary arts curriculum
- **Companion planting demonstrations**: Living examples of ecological relationships for science instruction
- **Measurement stations**: Permanent markers, rulers, and data collection points embedded in beds
- **Signage**: Bilingual plant identification labels with scientific names and growing data

### Zone 3: Sensory Garden (Regulate Phase)
A dedicated space for sensory engagement and emotional regulation.

- **Aromatic herbs**: Lavender, rosemary, mint, lemon balm -- plants selected for calming properties
- **Texture garden**: Plants with varied tactile qualities (lamb's ear, succulents, ornamental grasses)
- **Sound elements**: Wind chimes, water feature, rustling grasses
- **Color therapy plantings**: Cool blues and purples for calming; warm yellows and oranges for energizing
- **Quiet seating**: Benches or stumps positioned for individual reflection

### Zone 4: Regulation Pods (Regulate Phase)
Semi-enclosed spaces for active co-regulation and self-regulation. See the Rootwork Regulation Pods documentation for detailed specifications.

### Zone 5: Outdoor Classroom (Reflect/Reconnect Phases)
A structured instructional area within the garden environment.

- **Seating**: Log rounds, stump stools, or weather-resistant benches for 15-25 students arranged in a semicircle
- **Instruction surface**: Outdoor whiteboard or chalkboard, weatherproof
- **Shade structure**: Pergola, sail shade, or tree canopy for sun protection
- **Power access**: Outdoor-rated outlet for technology integration (tablets, digital tools)
- **Display area**: Space for student work, data visualizations, and growth tracking charts

### Zone 6: Harvest and Preparation Area (Reconnect Phase)
Where harvested produce is processed and prepared for sharing.

- **Wash station**: Produce washing area with food-safe drainage
- **Sorting and weighing station**: Scale, baskets, and record-keeping materials
- **Basic preparation area**: Cutting boards, child-safe knives, and serving supplies for simple food preparation
- **Distribution point**: For community sharing, family take-home, and food bank donations

### Zone 7: Composting and Tool Storage (Restore Phase)
Where garden "waste" becomes future nourishment -- a physical metaphor for the Restore phase.

- **Compost system**: Three-bin system for active composting instruction
- **Worm bin**: Vermicomposting station for decomposition observation and science instruction
- **Tool storage**: Locked shed or cabinet with labeled, organized tool storage
- **Potting station**: Bench with soil, pots, and propagation supplies

## Accessibility Standards

All Living Learning Lab designs must meet ADA accessibility standards:

- 36" minimum pathway width (48" preferred) for wheelchair access
- Raised beds at wheelchair-accessible height (24-30") with knee clearance
- Firm, stable pathway surfaces (decomposed granite, compacted gravel, or pavers)
- Accessible water sources and handwashing stations
- Shade and seating accessible to all students
- Signage at accessible heights with Braille or tactile elements where feasible

## Infrastructure Requirements

### Water System
- Primary irrigation: Drip system on timer for production beds
- Hose bibs: Minimum 2, placed at opposite ends of the lab
- Rain collection: Rain barrel system for sustainability education (where legally permitted)
- Handwashing: Dedicated station with soap dispenser near entrance and preparation areas

### Soil
- Raised bed fill: 60% topsoil, 30% compost, 10% vermiculite or perlite
- In-ground amendment: Soil test before planting; amend to target pH 6.0-7.0
- Annual soil testing: Conducted each spring with results used for science instruction

### Fencing and Security
- Perimeter fencing: 4-foot minimum height, with lockable gate
- Fencing material: Chain link with privacy slats, or wooden fence panels
- Security: Lockable tool storage and supply cabinets
- Signage: "Living Learning Lab" identification and visitor guidelines

### Electrical
- Outdoor-rated GFCI outlets for technology use
- Solar-powered lighting for early morning or late afternoon sessions
- Weather station connection (optional, for data collection)

- Tool replacement and maintenance: $100-300
