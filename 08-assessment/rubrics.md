# Rubrics

## Overview

Rubrics in the FG2G curriculum serve as transparent, trauma-informed assessment tools that communicate expectations clearly and celebrate growth. Every rubric in this section measures along a four-level progression: Emerging, Developing, Proficient, and Exemplary. These levels are intentionally strength-based -- there is no "failing" level. All students are positioned somewhere on a growth continuum.

Rubrics are shared with students before the assessed task begins, used for self-assessment during the task, and applied by educators after the task. This three-point use cycle ensures rubrics function as learning tools, not just evaluation instruments.

---

## 1. Reasoning Move Rubric

This rubric assesses student proficiency in each of the seven reasoning moves across the FG2G curriculum.

### Observing

| Level | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **Emerging** | Notices one or two obvious features; observations are general ("It's green") |
| **Developing** | Notices multiple features using more than one sense; begins to use descriptive language ("The leaves are dark green with bumpy edges") |
| **Proficient** | Systematically observes using multiple senses; uses precise, scientific vocabulary; records observations with detail and accuracy |
| **Exemplary** | Observations are thorough, organized, and include quantitative data; identifies subtle features others miss; self-directs observation process |

### Comparing

| Level | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **Emerging** | Identifies one similarity or one difference with prompting |
| **Developing** | Identifies multiple similarities and differences; uses comparison language ("both," "unlike") |
| **Proficient** | Organizes comparisons systematically (e.g., Venn diagram, T-chart); identifies meaningful attributes for comparison |
| **Exemplary** | Draws conclusions from comparisons; identifies patterns across multiple items; explains why differences matter |

### Inferring

| Level | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **Emerging** | Makes guesses without reference to evidence |
| **Developing** | Makes inferences with partial evidence; begins to distinguish between observation and inference |
| **Proficient** | Makes logical inferences supported by specific evidence; explains reasoning chain clearly |
| **Exemplary** | Makes multiple plausible inferences from the same evidence; evaluates which inference is strongest; revises inferences when new evidence emerges |

### Predicting

| Level | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **Emerging** | Makes predictions without rationale or based on wishes rather than evidence |
| **Developing** | Makes predictions with some connection to prior knowledge or patterns |
| **Proficient** | Makes predictions grounded in observed patterns and data; explains reasoning; identifies variables that could affect outcome |
| **Exemplary** | Makes quantitative predictions with margins of uncertainty; designs tests to verify predictions; revises prediction models based on results |

### Evaluating

| Level | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **Emerging** | Offers opinions without criteria ("I like it" or "It's good") |
| **Developing** | Uses one or two criteria to evaluate; begins to provide reasons for judgments |
| **Proficient** | Applies multiple relevant criteria systematically; provides evidence-based judgments; considers trade-offs |
| **Exemplary** | Develops original criteria for evaluation; weighs competing considerations; proposes improvements based on evaluation; considers multiple stakeholder perspectives |

## 2. TRACE Protocol Rubric

This rubric assesses student proficiency in applying the full TRACE protocol to a garden-based problem or academic task.

| TRACE Step | Emerging | Developing | Proficient | Exemplary |
|------------|----------|------------|------------|-----------|
| **Think** | Begins responding immediately without pausing to observe | Pauses briefly; notices surface-level features of the problem | Takes deliberate time to observe; identifies key information and unknowns | Systematically surveys the full problem; generates questions before attempting a response; identifies what additional information is needed |
| **Reason** | Offers a response with no visible reasoning process | Applies one piece of evidence or prior knowledge; reasoning has gaps | Constructs a logical reasoning chain with multiple pieces of evidence; connects to prior knowledge | Considers multiple reasoning pathways; evaluates the strength of each; selects the most well-supported approach |
| **Articulate** | Cannot explain thinking or uses only "I don't know" / "Because" | Explains thinking in fragments; ideas are present but disorganized | Communicates reasoning clearly using complete thoughts; uses domain-specific vocabulary; reasoning is followable by peers | Explanation is precise, well-organized, and persuasive; anticipates and addresses counterarguments; uses academic language fluently |
| **Check** | Does not review work; accepts first answer as final | Checks answer superficially (e.g., "It looks right") | Verifies answer through a specific checking strategy (re-reads, re-calculates, compares to evidence); identifies and corrects errors | Uses multiple checking strategies; explains why the answer makes sense in context; identifies limitations of the answer |
| **Extend** | Does not connect learning to other contexts | Makes a vague connection ("This is like something else") | Identifies specific connections to other content areas, real-world situations, or prior learning | Generates new questions from the task; proposes applications to community or personal contexts; identifies how the reasoning applies to novel problems |

### Scoring Guide

- **Emerging (1)**: Student demonstrates initial awareness of the TRACE step but requires significant scaffolding.
- **Developing (2)**: Student demonstrates partial proficiency; can perform the step with prompting.
- **Proficient (3)**: Student demonstrates consistent, independent proficiency in the step.
- **Exemplary (4)**: Student demonstrates deep, flexible mastery that extends beyond the immediate task.

**Overall TRACE Score**: Sum of all five steps (range 5-20). Proficiency threshold: 15 or above.

## 3. Garden Project Rubric

This rubric assesses comprehensive garden projects that integrate academic content with garden-based learning.

| Dimension | Emerging (1) | Developing (2) | Proficient (3) | Exemplary (4) |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Planning** | No written plan; began without preparation | Basic plan with missing elements; did not anticipate challenges | Detailed plan with timeline, materials, and roles; identified potential challenges | Comprehensive plan with contingencies; incorporated research; timeline is realistic and well-sequenced |
| **Scientific Understanding** | Minimal connection to science content; factual errors present | Some accurate science content; connections to garden context are surface-level | Accurate science content integrated throughout; clear connections between garden observations and scientific concepts | Deep scientific understanding demonstrated; original insights about garden phenomena; connects to broader ecological principles |
| **Data Collection** | No data collected or data is incomplete/inaccurate | Some data collected; methods are inconsistent; records are incomplete | Data collected systematically using appropriate tools; records are organized and accurate | Data collection is rigorous; multiple data sources triangulated; data visualization enhances understanding |
| **TRACE Application** | TRACE protocol not evident in project work | One or two TRACE steps visible in project documentation | Full TRACE protocol applied to at least one key project decision; documented in project journal | TRACE protocol applied iteratively throughout the project; multiple cycles of Think-Reason-Articulate-Check-Extend are documented |
| **Collaboration** | Worked in isolation or experienced unresolved conflict | Participated in group work with some difficulty; uneven contribution | Contributed equitably; communicated effectively with team members; resolved minor conflicts | Facilitated group success; supported peers' contributions; modeled constructive conflict resolution |
| **Presentation** | Did not present or presentation lacked coherence | Presented with significant gaps; limited audience engagement | Clear, organized presentation with visual supports; answered audience questions | Compelling presentation that engaged the audience; demonstrated deep knowledge through Q&A; connected to community relevance |

## 4. Growth Mindset Indicators Rubric

This rubric tracks growth mindset development across the school year, assessed through observation and student self-report.

| Indicator | Emerging | Developing | Proficient | Exemplary |
|-----------|----------|------------|------------|-----------|
| **Response to Challenge** | Avoids challenging tasks; gives up quickly; may become dysregulated | Attempts challenging tasks with adult support; expresses frustration but continues with encouragement | Persists through challenging tasks independently; uses strategies when stuck; asks for help appropriately | Seeks out challenging tasks; views difficulty as a sign of learning; supports peers through challenges |
| **Response to Error** | Hides errors; becomes upset or shuts down when errors are identified | Acknowledges errors with adult support; beginning to see errors as information | Views errors as learning opportunities; can analyze what went wrong; applies corrections | Actively seeks feedback; analyzes errors independently using TRACE protocol; shares error analysis with peers to support collective learning |
| **Effort Attribution** | Attributes success/failure to fixed traits ("I'm dumb" / "I'm just good at this") | Beginning to connect effort to outcome with prompting | Consistently connects effort and strategy to outcomes; uses growth language ("I haven't learned this yet") | Articulates specific strategies that led to improvement; mentors others in growth mindset; self-advocates for learning needs |
| **Risk-Taking in Learning** | Only engages in tasks where success is guaranteed | Takes small risks with significant adult encouragement | Volunteers answers, asks questions, and tries new approaches in the garden and classroom | Proposes original ideas, experiments, and approaches; comfortable with uncertainty; models intellectual courage for peers |

## 5. Regulation Self-Assessment Rubric

This rubric is used by students to assess their own regulation capacity. It is introduced during the Regulate phase and practiced regularly throughout the year.

### Student-Facing Version (Grades 3-12)

| My Regulation Level | What This Feels Like | What I Can Do | What I Might Need |
|--------------------|---------------------|---------------|-------------------|
| **1 - Overwhelmed** | My body feels tight, shaky, or frozen; I cannot focus on anything; I might want to leave | Use my calm-down plan; go to the regulation station; ask for help | A break from demands; a safe, quiet space; an adult I trust nearby |
| **2 - Struggling** | I feel distracted or irritated; my thoughts are jumpy; I can focus a little but not for long | Use a breathing strategy; do a garden regulation task (watering, sorting); talk to my regulation buddy | Reduced task demands; a sensory tool; a check-in with my teacher |
| **3 - Managing** | I feel mostly calm; I can focus on my work; I might get distracted sometimes but I can bring myself back | Keep working; use strategies if I start to slip; take a short break if needed | Occasional check-ins; access to strategies if I need them |
| **4 - Regulated** | I feel calm and focused; I can do my work and help others; I can handle small frustrations | Engage fully in learning; support a peer; take on challenges | Continue current conditions; recognition of my regulation success |
| **5 - Thriving** | I feel energized and engaged; I am excited to learn; I can manage my emotions even when things are hard | Lead activities; mentor others; extend my learning; take creative risks | Opportunities for leadership and extension; new challenges |

### Educator Observation Companion

| Regulation Level | Observable Behaviors | Recommended Educator Response |
|-----------------|---------------------|------------------------------|
| **1** | Flight/fight/freeze responses; crying; aggression; complete withdrawal; inability to respond to verbal cues | Prioritize safety; reduce sensory input; offer co-regulation (calm presence, rhythmic movement); do not assess or make academic demands |
| **2** | Fidgeting; irritability; off-task behavior; difficulty following multi-step directions; frequent requests to leave | Offer choice of regulation activity; reduce task complexity; provide one-step directions; check in with warmth |
| **3** | Generally on-task with occasional drift; can respond to redirection; may need repetition of directions | Provide standard instruction with periodic check-ins; offer optional regulation supports |
| **4** | Consistently engaged; follows directions; manages transitions; appropriate social interactions | Standard instruction; offer extension opportunities; invite student to support peers |
| **5** | Highly engaged; self-directed; shows leadership; handles frustration with flexibility | Provide enrichment; invite leadership roles; document as evidence of regulation growth |

## Rubric Implementation Guidance

### Introducing Rubrics to Students

1. **Co-construct rubrics when possible.** In grades 3-12, invite students to help define what "proficient" looks like for a garden task before beginning the work.
2. **Use anchor examples.** Show student work samples (anonymous or from previous years) at each rubric level so students can see the progression.
3. **Practice self-assessment.** Before educator scoring, have students assess their own work using the rubric. Compare student and educator ratings in a portfolio conference.
4. **Celebrate growth across levels.** Explicitly recognize movement from Emerging to Developing as powerfully as movement from Proficient to Exemplary.

### Trauma-Informed Rubric Practices

- Never use rubric scores as public rankings or comparisons between students.
- Frame all feedback in terms of "next steps" rather than deficits.
- Allow reassessment after additional learning and practice.
- If a student's regulation level drops during assessment, pause and prioritize regulation over evaluation.
- Use regulation self-assessment data to contextualize academic rubric scores -- a student who scored "Developing" while at regulation level 2 may actually be performing above their current capacity.
