# Assessment Philosophy Overview

## Guiding Principles

Assessment in the FG2G curriculum serves a fundamentally different purpose than traditional testing. Rooted in trauma-informed practice, our assessment philosophy recognizes that how we assess is just as important as what we assess. Every assessment interaction must maintain the safety and regulation established through the 5Rs Framework while providing meaningful data on both academic progress and social-emotional development.

### Core Beliefs

1. **Assessment is a relationship act.** The way an educator assesses communicates care, expectation, and belief in a student's capacity. Trauma-informed assessment prioritizes the relational context.
2. **Dual-purpose assessment is non-negotiable.** Every assessment in FG2G measures both academic growth and social-emotional learning. A garden observation log captures scientific reasoning and reflective capacity simultaneously.
3. **Assessment should be regulation-aware.** A student in a dysregulated state cannot demonstrate their true knowledge. Educators must assess regulation readiness before administering any evaluative task.
4. **Growth over time matters more than single-point performance.** Portfolios, progress monitoring, and longitudinal data tell a more complete story than any single test.
5. **Student voice belongs in assessment.** Students participate in setting criteria, self-assessing, and selecting portfolio evidence. This builds agency and metacognition.

## Dual-Purpose Assessment Framework

Every FG2G assessment tool measures along two dimensions simultaneously.

| Assessment Tool | Academic Dimension | SEL Dimension |
|----------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Garden observation log | Scientific observation skills, data recording accuracy | Regulation during task, reflective depth |
| TRACE protocol response | Reasoning quality, content accuracy | Metacognitive awareness, willingness to revise |
| Growth portfolio | Cumulative academic achievement across standards | Self-selection rationale, growth mindset evidence |
| Harvest presentation | Communication skills, content mastery | Collaboration, community connection |
| Reasoning move checklist | Frequency and quality of reasoning moves | Persistence through challenge, self-regulation |
| Peer teaching demonstration | Depth of content understanding | Empathy, patience, social awareness |

## Formative vs. Summative Assessment

### Formative Assessment (Assessment FOR Learning)

Formative assessment in FG2G occurs continuously during garden-based instruction. It provides real-time data that educators use to adjust instruction within and across sessions.

| Formative Tool | Frequency | Data Collected | 5Rs Phase |
|---------------|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| Regulation check-in | Every session (minimum 2x) | Self-reported regulation level (1-5 scale) | Regulate |
| Garden observation prompt | Daily during garden time | Quality of observation, reasoning moves used | Reflect |
| TRACE quick-check | 2-3 times per week | Proficiency on each TRACE step | Restore |
| Exit ticket | End of each session | Key concept understanding, remaining questions | Reflect |
| Peer discussion observation | During collaborative work | Communication quality, reasoning articulation | Reconnect |
| Educator anecdotal notes | Ongoing | Behavioral observations, regulation patterns | All phases |

### Summative Assessment (Assessment OF Learning)

Summative assessment in FG2G occurs at natural culmination points aligned with garden cycles (planting, growing, harvest).

| Summative Tool | Timing | What It Measures |
|---------------|--------|-----------------|
| Growth portfolio review | End of each garden cycle (approx. every 8-10 weeks) | Cumulative academic and SEL growth |
| Garden project presentation | End of each unit | Content mastery, communication, community connection |
| Reasoning move proficiency assessment | Quarterly | Mastery level of targeted reasoning moves |
| TRACE protocol performance task | Semester | Full protocol application to a novel garden problem |
| Standards-aligned content assessment | Per district schedule | Academic content knowledge |

### Assessment AS Learning

The most powerful assessment in FG2G is assessment as learning, where the assessment process itself deepens understanding:

- **Self-assessment using rubrics**: Students evaluate their own work against clear criteria before submitting, building metacognitive skill.
- **Portfolio curation**: The act of selecting and justifying portfolio pieces requires students to reflect on their growth trajectory.
- **Peer feedback protocols**: Structured peer review using TRACE-aligned feedback stems develops both the reviewer's and the author's understanding.
- **Error analysis tasks**: Students examine their own previous errors, identify the reasoning breakdown, and explain what they now understand differently.

## Regulation-Aware Assessment Timing

A central tenet of trauma-informed assessment is that regulation must precede evaluation. The following protocol guides assessment timing decisions.

### Pre-Assessment Regulation Check

Before any formal assessment, educators conduct a brief regulation scan:

1. **Observe the room**: Note body language, energy level, and engagement across the group.
2. **Conduct a check-in**: Use the visual regulation scale. If more than 25% of students report a level of 1 or 2 (dysregulated), delay the assessment.
3. **Offer regulation supports**: Provide 5-10 minutes of garden-based regulation activities (watering, soil sifting, herb smelling) before attempting assessment.
4. **Reassess readiness**: After regulation activities, check in again. Proceed only when the majority of students report level 3 or above (regulated).

### Individual Accommodations

| Regulation State | Assessment Accommodation |
|-----------------|-------------------------|
| **Fully regulated (4-5)** | Standard assessment conditions |
| **Mostly regulated (3)** | Offer choice of assessment format; provide sensory tool access |
| **Partially dysregulated (2)** | Reduce assessment length; allow movement breaks; offer oral response option |
| **Significantly dysregulated (1)** | Postpone assessment; prioritize regulation support; reschedule for another day |

### Timing Principles

- Never assess during the first 10 minutes of a session (Root phase is for safety, not evaluation).
- Avoid high-stakes assessment on Mondays or the day after school breaks, when regulation may be lower.
- Schedule performance-based assessments (presentations, demonstrations) during the Reconnect phase when students have had the benefit of the full 5Rs sequence.
- Allow flexible deadlines for portfolio submissions, recognizing that trauma responses may cause inconsistent availability.

## Assessment Data Use

Assessment data in FG2G serves four audiences:

| Audience | Data They Need | Format |
|----------|---------------|--------|
| **Students** | Progress toward personal goals, regulation patterns, growth evidence | Student dashboard, portfolio conferences |
| **Educators** | Instructional adjustment data, individual student needs, group trends | Educator dashboard, data team meetings |
| **Families** | Child's growth story, strengths, areas for development | Portfolio conference narratives, progress reports |
| **Program evaluators** | Aggregate outcomes, implementation fidelity, effectiveness data | Analytics dashboards, annual reports |

### Data Privacy and Trauma Sensitivity

- Regulation data is never shared publicly or used punitively.
- SEL assessment results are communicated with strength-based language.
- Students have the right to exclude portfolio items they find distressing.
- All data is stored in compliance with FERPA and COPPA regulations (see Section 10: Technology Integration).

## Connecting Assessment to the 5Rs

| 5Rs Phase | Assessment Focus | Key Question |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------|
| **Root** | Safety and belonging indicators | "Does this student feel safe enough to take academic risks?" |
| **Regulate** | Regulation capacity and growth | "Is this student developing greater self-regulation over time?" |
| **Reflect** | Metacognitive depth | "Can this student observe and articulate their own thinking?" |
| **Restore** | Academic skill and knowledge | "Is this student demonstrating growth in content understanding and reasoning?" |
| **Reconnect** | Transfer and community application | "Can this student apply learning to new contexts and contribute to community?" |

## Implementation Guidance

### Year 1 Focus

During the first year of FG2G implementation, assessment priorities should be:

1. Establish consistent regulation check-in routines (every session, non-negotiable).
2. Introduce garden observation logs as the primary formative tool.
3. Begin portfolio collection with simple structures (physical folders are acceptable).
4. Practice one reasoning move assessment per quarter.
5. Delay high-complexity summative assessments until the second semester.

### Mature Implementation

In years 2 and beyond, assessment systems expand to include:

- Full digital portfolio platform with student self-curation.
- Automated regulation tracking through the RootGuide platform.
- Quarterly TRACE protocol performance tasks.
- Cross-grade portfolio conferences.
- Data-informed instructional cycles with structured data team protocols.
