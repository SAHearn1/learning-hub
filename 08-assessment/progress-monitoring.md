# Progress Monitoring

## Overview

Progress monitoring in the FG2G curriculum is a continuous, multi-dimensional process that tracks student growth across regulation capacity, reasoning proficiency, TRACE protocol mastery, and academic content knowledge. Unlike traditional progress monitoring that focuses solely on academic benchmarks, FG2G monitoring integrates social-emotional data alongside academic data to provide a holistic picture of each learner's trajectory.

The system is designed to be low-burden for educators through structured routines and, where available, digital tools that automate data collection and visualization.

---

## Monitoring Dimensions

FG2G tracks progress along five interconnected dimensions. Each dimension has specific indicators, collection methods, and reporting cycles.

| Dimension | What Is Measured | Collection Method | Frequency | Data Source |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|-----------|-------------|
| **Regulation Capacity** | Self-regulation level, regulation strategy use, recovery time after dysregulation | Student self-report (regulation scale); educator observation | Every session (self-report); weekly (observation notes) | Regulation check-in tool; educator anecdotal records |
| **Reasoning Move Proficiency** | Frequency and quality of seven reasoning moves | Reasoning move checklist during garden tasks; scored work samples | Bi-weekly (checklist); quarterly (scored samples) | Reasoning move tracker; rubric-scored artifacts |
| **TRACE Protocol Mastery** | Proficiency in each of the five TRACE steps | TRACE protocol performance tasks; educator observation during instruction | Monthly (observation); quarterly (performance task) | TRACE rubric scores; observation notes |
| **Academic Content Growth** | Standards-aligned content knowledge and skills | Formative checks; unit assessments; portfolio evidence | Per unit (formative); quarterly (summative); ongoing (portfolio) | Assessment scores; portfolio artifacts |
| **Community Connection** | Transfer of learning to community contexts; collaborative skills | Project evaluations; service learning documentation; peer feedback | Per project; semester (overall rating) | Project rubrics; peer and community feedback |

## Regulation Level Tracking

### Daily Regulation Data Collection

Every FG2G session includes at minimum two regulation check-ins using the visual regulation scale (levels 1-5).

| Check-In Point | Timing | Purpose |
|---------------|--------|---------|
| **Entry check-in** | During Root phase (first 5 minutes) | Establish baseline regulation for the session; identify students needing immediate support |
| **Mid-session check-in** | During transition from Regulate to Restore phase | Assess regulation readiness for academic demands; adjust instruction if needed |
| **Exit check-in** (optional but recommended) | During Reflect/Reconnect phase | Document regulation trajectory across the session; identify end-of-day patterns |

### Regulation Progress Indicators

Progress in regulation is assessed through the following metrics tracked over time:

| Metric | Definition | Target Trajectory |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Average daily regulation level** | Mean of all check-in scores for a given day | Increase from baseline by 0.5 points per quarter |
| **Regulation range** | Difference between highest and lowest check-in in a day | Decrease over time (greater stability) |
| **Recovery time** | Time from dysregulation event to return to level 3 or above | Decrease over time (faster recovery) |
| **Strategy use frequency** | Number of times student independently uses a regulation strategy | Increase over time (more self-directed regulation) |
| **Sustained regulation duration** | Length of time student maintains level 3+ during academic tasks | Increase over time (longer focus periods) |

### Regulation Data Visualization

The educator dashboard displays regulation data in three views:

1. **Individual student timeline**: A line graph showing regulation check-in scores over the past 30 days, with a trend line indicating overall trajectory.
2. **Class heat map**: A color-coded matrix showing all students' regulation levels at each check-in point, making it easy to identify group patterns (e.g., low regulation on Mondays).
3. **Growth summary**: A quarterly comparison showing each student's average regulation level at the start of the quarter versus the end.

|------------|----------------------------------------|-------------------|

## TRACE Protocol Proficiency Monitoring

### Monthly TRACE Observation

Educators observe students during TRACE protocol application and score each step on the four-level rubric (see Rubrics section).

| TRACE Step | Observation Focus | Recording Method |
|------------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Think** | Does the student pause to observe before responding? How long? What do they notice? | Tally of pause duration; checklist of observation behaviors |
| **Reason** | Does the student reference evidence? Connect to prior knowledge? Use logical structure? | Anecdotal notes on reasoning quality |
| **Articulate** | Can the student explain their thinking clearly? To a peer? To the class? | Rating on articulation rubric |
| **Check** | Does the student verify their answer? What checking strategies do they use? | Checklist of checking strategies observed |
| **Extend** | Does the student make connections beyond the immediate task? | Anecdotal notes on extension quality |

### Quarterly TRACE Performance Task

Each quarter, students complete a formal TRACE performance task using a novel garden problem. These tasks are scored using the full TRACE rubric and results are tracked over time to show growth.

**Sample Performance Task (Grades 3-5, Quarter 2)**:

> Two tomato plants were planted at the same time in the same garden bed. Plant A is 18 inches tall with dark green leaves. Plant B is 10 inches tall with pale yellow leaves. Using the TRACE protocol, investigate what might explain this difference and propose a solution.

Scoring produces a TRACE profile for each student showing relative strength across the five steps. Common patterns include:

| TRACE Profile Pattern | What It Suggests | Instructional Response |
|----------------------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Strong Think and Reason; weak Articulate | Student thinks well but struggles to communicate | Provide sentence frames; increase partner sharing; practice oral explanation |
| Strong Articulate; weak Think and Reason | Student is verbal but may rush to respond without deep thinking | Increase wait time; require written planning before speaking; model deliberate observation |
| Weak Check across all tasks | Student does not verify or revise answers | Explicitly teach checking strategies; make Check step a required written component |
| Strong across all steps | Student has mastered the protocol at current level | Increase task complexity; invite student to mentor peers; introduce advanced extensions |

## Academic Growth Measures

### Formative Academic Checks

Aligned to state and national standards, formative academic checks occur during the Restore phase of instruction.

| Check Type | Format | Frequency | Data Use |
|-----------|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Quick-write** | 3-5 sentence written response to a content question | 2-3 times per week | Identify misconceptions for next-day reteaching |
| **Garden lab check** | Observation of student procedure and data recording during garden tasks | Weekly | Assess science process skills; inform grouping |
| **Math application** | Garden-contextualized math problem (measurement, data analysis, proportional reasoning) | Weekly | Track computational and reasoning growth |
| **Vocabulary check** | Use of domain-specific vocabulary during garden discussions | Ongoing | Assess language development; inform vocabulary instruction |

### Quarterly Academic Benchmarks

Each quarter, students complete a standards-aligned benchmark assessment contextualized within the garden curriculum. Results are compared to beginning-of-year baseline data.

## Data Dashboard

### Educator Dashboard Features

The FG2G data dashboard (accessible through the RootGuide platform or as a standalone spreadsheet template) provides:

| Dashboard View | Data Displayed | Update Frequency | Action Triggered |
|---------------|---------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Student snapshot** | Individual student's regulation, reasoning, TRACE, and academic data in one view | Real-time (digital) or weekly (manual) | Informs individual conferencing and goal-setting |
| **Class overview** | Aggregate class data across all five monitoring dimensions | Weekly | Informs instructional planning and grouping |
| **Growth trends** | Longitudinal graphs showing progress across quarters | Quarterly | Informs report card narratives and portfolio conferences |
| **Alert flags** | Highlights students whose data shows regression or stagnation in any dimension | Weekly | Triggers intervention planning or support team referral |
| **Comparison view** | Compares current period data to previous period for each student | Quarterly | Informs goal revision and celebration of growth |

### Family-Facing Progress Reports

Families receive progress reports at the end of each quarter that include:

1. **Regulation growth summary**: A brief narrative describing the student's regulation trajectory with specific examples.
2. **Reasoning and TRACE highlight**: One specific example of the student's strongest reasoning move or TRACE step.
3. **Academic progress**: Standards-based progress indicators with garden-contextualized descriptions of what the student can do.
4. **Growth celebration**: A specific, concrete example of growth the student has shown.
5. **Next-step goals**: 1-2 collaboratively set goals for the next quarter.

## Reporting Cycles

| Cycle | Timing | Activities | Stakeholders |
|-------|--------|------------|-------------|
| **Weekly** | Every Friday | Educator reviews class data; updates observation notes; identifies students for check-ins | Educator |
| **Monthly** | First week of each month | TRACE observation scoring; regulation trend review; data team meeting | Educator team |
| **Quarterly** | End of each quarter | Benchmark assessment; portfolio review; family progress reports; goal revision | Educator, student, family |
| **Semester** | End of fall and spring | Comprehensive portfolio conference; program-level data review | All stakeholders |
| **Annual** | End of school year | Summative growth report; transition documentation for next-year educator | Educator, student, family, administration |

## Implementation Guidance

### Getting Started (Year 1)

1. Begin with regulation check-ins only. Establish the routine before adding other monitoring dimensions.
2. Use paper-based tracking for the first quarter, even if digital tools are available. This builds understanding of the data before automating.
3. Start reasoning move tracking with just Observing and Comparing (the two foundational moves).
4. Introduce TRACE monitoring in the second quarter after students have learned the protocol.
5. Align academic benchmarks to existing district assessment schedules to avoid over-testing.

### Avoiding Monitoring Fatigue

- Rotate observation focus: do not try to monitor all five dimensions for all students every day.
- Use a "focus student" protocol: deeply observe 3-5 students per session on a rotating schedule.
- Automate what you can: digital regulation check-ins, photo-based documentation, and pre-populated checklists save time.
- Protect planning time: data review and entry should occur during designated planning periods, not during instruction.
