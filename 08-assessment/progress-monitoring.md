# Progress Monitoring

## Introduction

Progress monitoring in the RootWork curriculum operates on a fundamental premise: if we wait until the end of a unit to discover that a student is struggling, we have waited too long. Just as a gardener walks the rows daily -- checking soil moisture, looking for pest damage, noting which seedlings have emerged and which have not -- RootWork educators maintain continuous awareness of each student's academic, social-emotional, and regulatory trajectory. Progress monitoring is the practice of paying close, systematic attention so that instructional adjustments happen in days, not weeks.

This document describes the tools, schedules, and decision rules that make up the RootWork progress monitoring system. It covers academic monitoring, social-emotional monitoring, the RootGuide digital dashboard, family reporting structures, and the integration of progress monitoring data with the Multi-Tiered System of Supports (MTSS) framework.

## Academic Progress Monitoring

### Reasoning Move Tracking

The TRACE protocol provides a natural structure for monitoring academic progress because it makes student thinking visible. Rather than tracking only whether a student gets a correct answer, reasoning move tracking documents the quality and sophistication of a student's cognitive engagement across the five TRACE steps.

**Reasoning Move Tracking Rubric:**

| TRACE Step | Beginning (1) | Approaching (2) | Meeting (3) | Extending (4) |
|------------|--------------|-----------------|-------------|---------------|
| **Think** | Does not offer observations; waits for teacher direction | Offers surface-level observations with prompting ("It's green") | Offers detailed, relevant observations independently ("The leaves on this plant are wider and darker than last week") | Makes observations that generate productive questions ("The leaves changed color after we moved it -- I wonder if light matters") |
| **Reason** | Does not propose a plan or strategy; looks to teacher for direction | Proposes a strategy only when given options ("Should I measure it?") | Proposes a reasonable strategy independently and explains rationale | Proposes multiple strategies, compares their strengths, and selects the most efficient |
| **Articulate** | Cannot describe own thinking; gives only answers | Describes thinking in fragments when prompted ("I added them") | Explains thinking clearly in complete thoughts, using academic vocabulary | Explains thinking using precise academic language, anticipates questions, and connects to prior learning |
| **Check** | Does not verify work; accepts first answer | Checks when reminded by teacher; identifies errors with guidance | Independently verifies using a second strategy; catches and corrects own errors | Develops systematic verification habits; explains why verification is important; checks peers' work constructively |
| **Extend** | Does not connect to other contexts; sees the problem as isolated | Makes connections when teacher suggests them | Independently connects to other problems, real-world contexts, or prior learning | Poses new problems; identifies patterns across contexts; teaches concepts to peers |

**Data Collection Schedule:**

Teachers record reasoning move data during TRACE interactions using a simple coding system:

- Record 4-5 students per session on a rotating basis (each student is observed at least once per week)
- Use the codes T1-T4, R1-R4, A1-A4, C1-C4, E1-E4 to note the level observed for each TRACE step
- Enter codes into the RootGuide platform at the end of each session (takes approximately 2 minutes)
- The platform generates a running line graph for each student showing reasoning move trajectories over time

### TRACE Engagement Monitoring

Beyond the quality of reasoning moves, teachers also monitor the pattern of student engagement with the TRACE protocol:

| Metric | What It Measures | Data Source | Frequency |
|--------|-----------------|-------------|-----------|
| Engagement duration | How long student sustains productive thinking before disengaging | Teacher observation | Each TRACE session |
| Initiation | Whether student enters TRACE independently or requires teacher prompting | Teacher observation | Each TRACE session |
| Engagement mode preference | Which modes (forward solving, reverse engineering, error analysis, multiple pathways, problem posing) the student gravitates toward and avoids | Teacher observation, student choice data | Weekly summary |
| Peer interaction quality | How productively the student engages with partners during Articulate and Check phases | Teacher observation | Each collaborative session |
| Regulation during cognitive demand | Whether student maintains regulation during challenging TRACE tasks or requires co-regulation support | Teacher observation, student self-report | Each TRACE session |

**Engagement Monitoring Decision Rules:**

- If a student's engagement duration declines over two consecutive weeks, investigate for regulation needs, task-match issues, or external stressors
- If a student consistently avoids one engagement mode (e.g., always avoids error analysis), gently scaffold entry into that mode with high-support tasks
- If peer interaction quality drops, assess whether the partnership is still productive or whether regrouping is needed
- If regulation during cognitive demand is consistently low, adjust the difficulty level or increase regulation supports before TRACE interactions

### Curriculum-Based Measurement (CBM)

Curriculum-Based Measurement provides standardized, brief probes that track academic skill development over time. In the RootWork system, CBMs are garden-contextualized wherever possible.

**CBM Schedule and Format by Subject Area:**

| Subject | Probe Format | Duration | Frequency | Garden Context Example |
|---------|-------------|----------|-----------|----------------------|
| Reading Fluency | Oral reading of grade-level garden-themed passage | 1 minute | Biweekly | Passage about seed germination or composting process |
| Math Computation | Grade-level computation problems using garden numbers | 2 minutes | Biweekly | "Each row has 8 plants. There are 6 rows. How many plants?" (adapted by grade) |
| Math Concepts | Word problems set in garden and food contexts | 5 minutes | Monthly | Calculating area of garden beds, unit pricing for farmers market |
| Written Expression | Timed writing responding to a garden observation prompt | 3 minutes | Biweekly | "Describe what you observed in the garden today and what you think it means" |
| Science Vocabulary | Matching or defining key terms from current garden science unit | 3 minutes | Monthly | Terms related to soil science, plant biology, ecology, or nutrition |

**CBM Administration Guidelines for Trauma-Informed Contexts:**

- Always frame CBMs as "a quick check so I can help you better," never as a test
- Administer in a quiet, familiar setting -- never in a public space where peers can observe
- Allow regulation tools during administration
- If a student becomes visibly dysregulated during a probe, stop and return to it another day without penalty
- Never time probes with a visible countdown timer; use a silent timer and give a gentle verbal cue at 30 seconds remaining
- Graph results collaboratively with students so they can see their own growth

## Social-Emotional Progress Monitoring

### Regulation Capacity Tracking

Regulation capacity is monitored daily because it directly affects academic access. The RootWork system tracks three dimensions of regulation: awareness (can the student identify their arousal state?), strategy use (does the student employ regulation strategies?), and autonomy (can the student self-regulate, or do they need co-regulation support?).

**Daily Regulation Data Points:**

| Data Point | Collection Method | Scale | Collected By |
|-----------|-------------------|-------|-------------|
| Morning arousal state | Student check-in (mood meter, body scan, or color zone) | 5-point scale: Very Low / Low / Calm-Alert / High / Very High | Student self-report, verified by teacher observation |
| Regulation strategy use | Teacher observation during Root and Regulate phases | Count: Number of times student independently selects and uses a strategy | Teacher |
| Co-regulation needs | Teacher observation across the full session | Count: Number of times student required adult co-regulation support | Teacher |
| Recovery time | Teacher observation after dysregulation episodes | Minutes: Time from peak dysregulation to return to calm-alert | Teacher |
| End-of-session arousal state | Student check-in at Reconnect phase | Same 5-point scale as morning | Student self-report |

**Regulation Growth Trajectory:**

Over the course of a school year, healthy regulation development typically follows this progression:

1. **Weeks 1-4 (Baseline):** Student relies primarily on adult co-regulation. May not yet identify arousal states accurately. Recovery time after dysregulation may be 15-30+ minutes.
2. **Weeks 5-12 (Emerging Awareness):** Student begins to accurately identify arousal states. Begins to accept regulation strategies offered by adults. Recovery time decreases to 10-20 minutes.
3. **Weeks 13-24 (Strategy Adoption):** Student independently selects regulation strategies from a familiar menu. Co-regulation needs decrease. Recovery time decreases to 5-15 minutes.
4. **Weeks 25-36 (Autonomy):** Student self-regulates in most situations. Seeks co-regulation only during significant stressors. Recovery time is typically under 10 minutes. Student may begin supporting peers.

**Important Note:** This progression is a general trajectory, not a rigid timeline. Students who have experienced significant trauma may move through these phases more slowly, may regress temporarily after triggering events, and may show uneven progress across settings. The purpose of tracking is to notice the trend, not to impose an expected pace.

### SEL Competency Monitoring

Social-emotional competencies are monitored quarterly using the CASEL framework as the organizing structure. Each competency is assessed through teacher observation, student self-assessment, and (at older grade bands) peer feedback.

**Quarterly SEL Competency Assessment Matrix:**

| CASEL Competency | Observable Indicators | Assessment Method | Proficiency Levels |
|-----------------|----------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Self-Awareness** | Accurately identifies own emotions; recognizes personal strengths; demonstrates growth mindset language | Teacher observation checklist; student self-assessment | Beginning / Approaching / Meeting / Extending |
| **Self-Management** | Uses regulation strategies independently; sets and works toward goals; manages impulses in group settings | Daily regulation data aggregation; goal-tracking review | Beginning / Approaching / Meeting / Extending |
| **Social Awareness** | Shows empathy for peers; recognizes diverse perspectives; demonstrates respect for cultural differences in the garden | Teacher observation during collaborative work; peer feedback (grades 6+) | Beginning / Approaching / Meeting / Extending |
| **Relationship Skills** | Communicates constructively; works collaboratively; resolves conflicts peacefully; seeks and offers help appropriately | Teacher observation during group work and garden activities; peer feedback (grades 6+) | Beginning / Approaching / Meeting / Extending |
| **Responsible Decision-Making** | Considers consequences before acting; makes ethical choices in garden stewardship; balances personal needs with group needs | Teacher observation; student reflection journal; garden stewardship rubric | Beginning / Approaching / Meeting / Extending |

## RootGuide Dashboard Metrics

The RootGuide digital platform aggregates all progress monitoring data into a dashboard that supports teacher decision-making, family communication, and MTSS processes.

### Teacher Dashboard View

The teacher dashboard displays the following metrics for each student and for the class as a whole:

**Individual Student Metrics:**

| Metric Category | Specific Metrics | Visualization | Update Frequency |
|----------------|-----------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Academic Trajectory | TRACE reasoning move levels (line graph over time); CBM scores (line graph with aimline); current proficiency levels by standard | Line graphs, proficiency bar chart | Weekly |
| Engagement Pattern | TRACE engagement duration trend; engagement mode distribution; participation frequency | Trend line, pie chart | Weekly |
| Regulation Profile | Daily arousal state pattern; co-regulation need frequency; strategy use count; average recovery time | Heat map (time of day by day of week), trend lines | Daily |
| SEL Summary | Current proficiency level for each CASEL competency; growth since last quarter | Radar chart, growth arrows | Quarterly |
| Portfolio Status | Number of artifacts by domain; last portfolio update date; conference readiness indicator | Progress bar, status flag | Ongoing |

**Class-Level Metrics:**

| Metric | Purpose | Visualization |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| Proficiency distribution by standard | Identify standards that need reteaching for the whole class | Stacked bar chart |
| Regulation climate | Monitor overall class regulation to detect environmental stressors | Daily class average arousal with range |
| TRACE engagement trends | Identify which TRACE steps or engagement modes need more instructional support | Class average trend lines by TRACE step |
| MTSS tier distribution | Monitor the percentage of students at each tier of support | Pie chart with student counts |

### Administrator Dashboard View

Administrators see aggregated data at the grade-band and school level:

- Percentage of students at each proficiency level by standard and grade band
- Regulation climate trends across classrooms
- MTSS tier distribution by classroom and grade band
- SEL competency averages by grade band
- Teacher assessment completion rates (ensuring fidelity to the monitoring schedule)

## Family Reporting

### Communication Schedule and Formats

| Communication | Frequency | Format | Content |
|--------------|-----------|--------|---------|
| Weekly Update | Weekly | Digital message via RootGuide family portal or printed note | Brief narrative highlighting one strength and one focus area; garden activity summary |
| Progress Snapshot | Every 4-5 weeks | Printed or digital one-page summary | Current proficiency levels, regulation growth summary, garden stewardship notes |
| Quarterly Report | End of quarter | Formal report card (printed and digital) | Proficiency levels for all standards, SEL summary, regulation narrative, attendance, garden participation |
| Conference Preparation | Before each conference | Student-selected portfolio work sent home for preview | Portfolio artifacts the student plans to present, conversation starters for families |
| Annual Growth Summary | End of year | Multi-page printed report | Year-long growth story with data visualizations, transition notes, summer recommendations |

### Family-Friendly Language Guidelines

Progress reports use clear, jargon-free language. The following translations ensure accessibility:

| Educator Language | Family-Friendly Language |
|------------------|------------------------|
| "Proficiency level 2: Approaching" | "Your child is making progress toward grade-level expectations and is getting closer every day" |
| "Demonstrates emerging self-regulation" | "Your child is learning to manage big feelings and is starting to use calming strategies on their own" |
| "TRACE engagement is developing" | "Your child is building their ability to explain their thinking during learning activities" |
| "CBM scores indicate below-benchmark performance" | "Your child's reading/math skills are still developing, and we have a plan to provide extra support" |
| "Tier 2 intervention recommended" | "We'd like to give your child some extra practice time in a small group to help build their skills faster" |

Reports are translated into families' home languages and are available in both digital and printed formats. Family conferences include interpreter support when needed.

## MTSS Integration

The RootWork progress monitoring system directly feeds the Multi-Tiered System of Supports framework. Assessment data determines which tier of support each student receives and triggers movement between tiers based on clear decision rules.

### Tier 1: Universal Supports (All Students)

**What Students Receive:**
- Full RootWork curriculum with 5Rs framework implementation
- TRACE protocol instruction and practice
- Daily regulation check-ins and strategy access
- Garden-based experiential learning
- Differentiated instruction within the general classroom
- Weekly growth journal and formative assessment

**Progress Monitoring at Tier 1:**
- TRACE reasoning move tracking (weekly, rotating observation)
- CBM probes (biweekly for reading and math computation, monthly for other areas)
- Daily regulation check-in data
- Quarterly SEL competency assessment

**Decision Rule for Tier 2 Referral:**
A student is referred for Tier 2 support when ANY of the following are met:
- CBM scores fall below the 25th percentile on two consecutive biweekly probes
- TRACE reasoning move levels remain at "Beginning" (1) in three or more steps after 6 weeks of Tier 1 instruction
- Regulation data shows co-regulation needs exceeding 5 episodes per day for more than 2 consecutive weeks
- SEL competency assessment shows "Beginning" in 3 or more competencies at the quarterly review
- Teacher professional judgment, documented with specific observational evidence

### Tier 2: Targeted Supports (Approximately 15-20% of Students)

**What Students Receive (in addition to Tier 1):**
- Small-group intervention sessions (3-4 students, 20-30 minutes, 3-4 times per week)
- Additional TRACE practice with scaffolded prompts and increased teacher modeling
- Individualized regulation plan with identified strategies and check-in schedule
- Modified garden tasks that provide additional practice on target skills
- More frequent family communication (biweekly progress snapshots)

**Progress Monitoring at Tier 2:**
- CBM probes weekly (instead of biweekly) for areas of concern
- TRACE reasoning move tracking twice per week (instead of once)
- Daily regulation data with specific strategy effectiveness tracking
- Biweekly progress review with intervention team

**Decision Rules:**

| Data Pattern | Decision |
|-------------|----------|
| CBM scores rise above the 25th percentile for 4 consecutive weeks | Return to Tier 1 with monitoring period |
| CBM scores remain flat or decline after 6-8 weeks of Tier 2 intervention | Refer for Tier 3 evaluation |
| TRACE reasoning move levels improve by at least 1 level in 2+ steps within 6 weeks | Continue Tier 2 with current approach |
| Regulation co-regulation needs decrease to fewer than 3 per day for 2 consecutive weeks | Begin fading Tier 2 regulation supports |
| Student shows progress in some areas but not others | Adjust intervention focus; do not change tier |

### Tier 3: Intensive Supports (Approximately 3-5% of Students)

**What Students Receive (in addition to Tiers 1 and 2):**
- Individualized intervention with daily 1:1 or 1:2 sessions (15-20 minutes)
- Comprehensive evaluation to determine root causes of persistent difficulty
- Individualized regulation and safety plan developed in collaboration with school counselor and family
- Modified academic expectations with clearly defined grade-level access points
- Weekly family communication and monthly family conference
- Referral for additional services as needed (school psychologist, counseling, community resources)

**Progress Monitoring at Tier 3:**
- CBM probes twice per week for areas of concern
- Daily TRACE observation with full documentation
- Continuous regulation monitoring with 30-minute interval data during school day
- Weekly progress review with intervention team including family representative
- Monthly review with full MTSS team

**Decision Rules:**

| Data Pattern | Decision |
|-------------|----------|
| Consistent growth toward aimline over 8-12 weeks | Continue Tier 3; consider gradual fade to Tier 2 |
| No growth after 8-12 weeks despite intervention adjustments | Refer for comprehensive special education evaluation |
| Regulation crisis frequency increases | Convene emergency team meeting; assess safety plan; contact family |
| Student demonstrates readiness for reduced support | Develop a structured transition plan from Tier 3 to Tier 2 with monitoring period |

### MTSS Data Review Schedule

| Meeting | Frequency | Participants | Focus |
|---------|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Classroom Data Review | Weekly | Classroom teacher, instructional coach | Review Tier 1 data; identify students approaching Tier 2 thresholds |
| Grade-Band MTSS Meeting | Biweekly | Grade-band teachers, instructional coach, counselor | Review Tier 2 students; make tier movement decisions |
| School MTSS Team | Monthly | All teachers, administrators, counselor, school psychologist, family liaisons | Review Tier 3 students; examine school-wide data trends; allocate resources |
| District MTSS Review | Quarterly | School representatives, district specialists | Examine cross-school patterns; share effective interventions; align resources |
