# Portfolio Assessment

## Overview

The FG2G Growth Portfolio is the cornerstone assessment of the curriculum. Unlike traditional portfolios that simply collect "best work," the FG2G Growth Portfolio documents the full arc of student development across academic reasoning, social-emotional growth, and garden-based learning. The portfolio is a living document that students curate throughout the school year, providing the richest and most authentic evidence of learning in the program.

The portfolio system is designed to be trauma-informed: students have agency over what they include, reflection prompts are regulation-aware, and portfolio conferences prioritize strengths and growth narratives over deficit-based evaluation.

---

## Portfolio Structure

Every FG2G Growth Portfolio contains four sections, regardless of grade band.

| Section | Purpose | Required Contents | Student Choice Contents |
|---------|---------|-------------------|------------------------|
| **My Roots** (Identity and Safety) | Documents the student's connection to the garden community and sense of belonging | Garden autobiography (updated annually); community map showing connections | Personal artifacts, drawings, photos of garden space |
| **My Growth** (Academic Evidence) | Demonstrates academic learning and reasoning development | One TRACE protocol sample per quarter; two reasoning move demonstrations per semester | Any additional academic work the student is proud of |
| **My Seasons** (SEL and Regulation) | Tracks social-emotional development and regulation growth | Regulation self-assessment logs (monthly); one growth mindset reflection per quarter | Journal entries, regulation strategy descriptions, peer feedback |
| **My Harvest** (Application and Community) | Shows transfer of learning to community and real-world contexts | One collaborative project reflection per semester; one community connection artifact | Service learning documentation, family garden photos, creative projects |

## Student Self-Selection Process

A distinctive feature of the FG2G portfolio is the emphasis on student self-selection. Beyond required components, students choose additional artifacts that represent their learning journey.

### Selection Protocol

1. **Review**: At the end of each unit or garden cycle, students review their work folder (physical or digital) containing all completed work from the period.
2. **Select**: Students choose 1-3 additional pieces to add to their portfolio based on the selection criteria.
3. **Reflect**: For each selected piece, students complete a brief reflection tag answering: "I chose this because..." and "This shows my growth in..."
4. **Conference**: Students share their selections with their educator in a brief (5-10 minute) portfolio check-in, explaining their choices.

### Selection Criteria (Posted in Classroom)

Students are taught to select work that shows:
- Something they worked hard on (effort and persistence)
- Something that shows how their thinking changed (growth)
- Something they are proud of (personal significance)
- Something that connects to their community or family (relevance)

## Garden Documentation

The garden itself is a primary source of portfolio evidence. Students document their garden learning through multiple modalities.

| Documentation Method | Description | Grade Band | Tools Needed |
|---------------------|-------------|------------|-------------|
| **Photo journals** | Photographs of garden work with captions explaining what was happening and what was learned | All | Camera or tablet; photo printing capability |
| **Growth measurement logs** | Quantitative tracking of plant growth over time with graphs | 3-12 | Rulers, graph paper or digital graphing tools |
| **Time-lapse sequences** | Sequential photos showing change over days or weeks | 3-12 | Fixed camera position; time-lapse app |
| **Soil and weather data records** | Systematic recording of environmental conditions | 4-12 | Soil test kits, thermometers, rain gauges |
| **Harvest inventories** | Quantified records of what was harvested, when, and how much | All | Scales, measuring tools, recording sheets |
| **Garden design sketches** | Annotated drawings of garden layout with rationale | 2-12 | Graph paper, colored pencils, or digital design tools |

## Digital Portfolio Platform

### Platform Features

The FG2G digital portfolio system (integrated within the RootGuide platform) provides:

- **Student interface**: Drag-and-drop artifact upload; reflection prompt templates; visual timeline of portfolio growth; regulation log integration.
- **Educator interface**: Class-wide portfolio progress dashboard; individual student views; rubric scoring tools; comment and feedback functions.
- **Family interface**: Read-only access to child's portfolio; ability to add family comments and encouragement; conference scheduling.
- **Privacy controls**: Students can mark artifacts as "private" (visible only to themselves and their educator); all data is FERPA/COPPA compliant; no student data is shared with third parties.

### Offline Alternative

For sites without reliable technology access, the FG2G portfolio can be maintained as a physical folder system:

- Use a three-ring binder with tabbed dividers for each portfolio section.
- Print photographs and tape into the binder.
- Use handwritten reflection tags attached to each artifact.
- Store binders in a secure, student-accessible location in the classroom or garden shed.

## Portfolio Conferences

Portfolio conferences replace traditional parent-teacher conferences in the FG2G model. They are student-led whenever possible.

### Conference Structure

| Conference Element | Duration | Description |
|-------------------|----------|-------------|
| **Welcome and grounding** | 2 minutes | Student welcomes family; brief garden-based grounding activity (e.g., passing an herb bundle) |
| **Portfolio tour** | 10 minutes | Student guides family through each portfolio section, explaining selections and growth |
| **Growth highlight** | 3 minutes | Student identifies the single piece they are most proud of and explains why |
| **Goal setting** | 5 minutes | Student, family, and educator collaboratively set 1-2 goals for the next period |
| **Closing** | 2 minutes | Educator affirms student growth; family shares a strength they noticed |

### Conference Preparation

- Students practice their portfolio tour with a peer before the family conference.
- Educators prepare a brief written strength summary for each student (2-3 sentences highlighting specific growth).
- Conference spaces include garden-related sensory elements (plants, soil samples, harvest products) to ground the experience.
- Provide interpreters or translated materials for families whose home language is not English.

### Trauma-Informed Conference Practices

- Never surprise families with negative information during a portfolio conference. Address concerns separately and privately.
- If a family member becomes dysregulated during the conference, the educator models co-regulation strategies.
- Offer flexible conference scheduling (evening, weekend, virtual) to accommodate diverse family situations.
- If a student does not have a family member available, pair them with a school staff mentor for the conference.

## Assessment of Portfolios

Portfolios are assessed holistically using the following criteria:

| Criterion | Weight | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| **Completeness** | 20% | All required components are present and current |
| **Reflection quality** | 30% | Reflection tags and narratives demonstrate genuine metacognitive engagement |
| **Growth evidence** | 30% | Portfolio demonstrates measurable growth over time in academic and SEL dimensions |
| **Curation and presentation** | 20% | Portfolio is organized, selections are purposeful, and presentation demonstrates care |
