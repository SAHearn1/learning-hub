# Foreword

## The Intersection of Healing and Learning

For decades, education and mental health have operated in parallel -- two systems serving the same children but rarely speaking the same language. Teachers were trained to deliver content; counselors were trained to address trauma. The assumption was that these were separate endeavors: first heal, then learn. Or worse: learn regardless of whether you have healed.

The From Garden to Growth (FG2G) curriculum challenges this assumption at its foundation. Dr. Shawn A. Hearn and the team at Community Exceptional Children's Services have built something that the field has needed for a long time: a curriculum that treats healing and learning as a single, integrated process rather than competing priorities.

## Why Gardens Matter

The choice of the garden as the curriculum's central learning environment is not decorative. It is strategic. Decades of research in therapeutic horticulture demonstrate that garden-based activities produce measurable reductions in cortisol, improvements in mood and attention, and increased prosocial behavior -- precisely the outcomes we seek for students who have experienced adverse childhood experiences.

But the garden does something else that a traditional classroom cannot: it externalizes abstract learning concepts into tangible, living systems. When a student watches a seed germinate, they are not just learning botany. They are witnessing a metaphor for their own capacity to grow through difficulty. When they compost a failed planting, they are practicing the Restore phase of the 5Rs -- learning that mistakes are not endpoints but raw material for future growth.

## The Science Behind the Framework

The 5Rs Framework is grounded in converging evidence from neuroscience, developmental psychology, and education research. Polyvagal theory tells us that safety is a prerequisite for learning. The window of tolerance model explains why dysregulated students cannot access higher-order cognition. Growth mindset research demonstrates that how we frame errors determines whether students persist or withdraw. Metacognitive research shows that teaching students to think about their thinking is one of the most powerful interventions available to educators.

Dr. Hearn has woven these strands into a practical, implementable framework that does not require a graduate degree in psychology to understand or deploy. The 5Rs give educators a common language and a predictable sequence that respects the science while remaining accessible and actionable.

## What Makes This Curriculum Different

Many curricula claim to be trauma-informed. Few deliver on that promise at the level of daily instructional practice. The FG2G curriculum is different because:

1. **It is not an add-on.** The 5Rs are not a module bolted onto existing instruction. They are the structure through which all instruction flows.
2. **It operationalizes regulation.** Rather than telling teachers to "be aware of dysregulation," the curriculum provides specific assessment tools, co-regulation strategies, and transition protocols.
3. **It connects to academic standards.** This is not a social-emotional curriculum that ignores academics. Every garden-based lesson maps to Georgia Standards of Excellence, Common Core, and NGSS.
4. **It integrates technology thoughtfully.** The RootGuide AI tutor is designed to extend the 5Rs into digital learning environments, complete with dysregulation detection -- a genuinely novel application of educational technology.
5. **It is designed for the students who need it most.** The curriculum was developed at Community Exceptional Children's Services, serving students with significant trauma histories. It was built for the hardest cases and refined through daily practice.

## An Invitation

I encourage you to read this curriculum not just as a set of lesson plans but as a vision for what school could be. The garden at the center of this curriculum is both literal and metaphorical -- a space where children who have been told they cannot grow are given the soil, the light, and the patient care they need to prove otherwise.

The seeds are in your hands.
