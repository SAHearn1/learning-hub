# Acknowledgments

A curriculum of this scope does not emerge from a single mind. *From Garden to Growth* is the product of years of collaboration, experimentation, honest failure, and collective learning. The following acknowledgments represent only a fraction of the gratitude owed to those who made this work possible.

---

## The Students

Above all, this curriculum was shaped by the young people who trusted us enough to try something different. The students who planted their first seeds with skepticism and harvested their first vegetables with pride. The students who told us when a lesson felt condescending, when an activity triggered rather than healed, when the garden was the only place in school where they felt safe enough to breathe. Your honesty was the most rigorous form of peer review this curriculum ever received. Your voices are woven into every page, and your growth -- measured not in test scores but in moments of self-regulation, connection, and courage -- remains the truest evidence of this work's value.

## Educators and School Staff

To the classroom teachers who piloted early versions of these lessons, often with incomplete materials and more questions than answers: your willingness to experiment, to sit with discomfort, and to reflect on your own practice with humility made the difference between a theoretical framework and a living curriculum. Special recognition is owed to the educators at our pilot school sites who spent countless hours providing feedback, adapting lessons to their students' needs, and documenting what worked, what did not, and what surprised them. Your practitioner expertise is the backbone of this guide.

To the school counselors and social workers who reviewed our trauma-informed protocols and ensured that our language honored the complexity of children's lived experiences without pathologizing them. Your clinical perspective kept this work grounded in ethical practice.

To the paraprofessionals, teaching assistants, and behavioral support staff who are so often the first adults to notice a child in distress and the last to receive professional development. This curriculum was written with your critical role in mind.

## Researchers and Academic Partners

This curriculum stands on the shoulders of decades of research in trauma-informed education, therapeutic horticulture, social-emotional learning, and developmental neuroscience. We are indebted to the scholars whose work forms the theoretical foundation of the 5Rs Framework, including the foundational contributions of Dr. Vincent Felitti and Dr. Robert Anda on Adverse Childhood Experiences, Dr. Stephen Porges on Polyvagal Theory, Dr. Daniel Siegel on interpersonal neurobiology and the Window of Tolerance, Dr. John Bowlby and Dr. Mary Ainsworth on attachment theory, Dr. Carol Dweck on growth mindset, Dr. Barry Zimmerman on self-regulated learning, and Dr. David Kolb on experiential learning.

We also thank the university research partners who helped design our assessment protocols, reviewed our data collection methods, and challenged us to hold our outcomes claims to rigorous standards of evidence.

## Garden Volunteers and Master Gardeners

To the master gardeners, community garden coordinators, and volunteers who taught us how to build raised beds, compost with intention, manage seasonal planting schedules, and troubleshoot the inevitable failures that come with any growing season. You reminded us that the garden is not a metaphor -- it is a real place with real demands, and respecting those demands is what makes garden-based learning authentic rather than decorative. Your horticultural expertise ensured that the garden activities in this curriculum are botanically sound, seasonally appropriate, and genuinely educational.

## Families and Caregivers

To the families who trusted us with their children, who shared their own stories of adversity and resilience, and who participated in family engagement nights even when the demands of daily survival made attendance a genuine sacrifice. Your partnership reminds us that healing-centered education cannot stop at the school door. Several family members served on our advisory board, reviewed parent-facing materials, and offered perspectives that no amount of academic research could replace. This curriculum is stronger because of your involvement.

## Community Partners

To the community organizations, nonprofits, local businesses, and faith communities that provided garden space, donated supplies, offered mentorship, and connected our schools to the broader ecosystem of support that children and families need. Your generosity demonstrated that healing-centered education is a community endeavor, not merely a school program.

## Community Exceptional Children's Services

To the leadership and staff of Community Exceptional Children's Services, whose organizational commitment to serving exceptional children through innovative, evidence-informed programming created the conditions for this curriculum to move from concept to reality. Your institutional support -- including funding, staffing, and the freedom to take creative risks -- is a model for how organizations can invest in transformative educational practices.

## Editorial and Production Support

To the editors, graphic designers, and production team who transformed a sprawling collection of lesson plans, research summaries, and field notes into a coherent, navigable, and visually accessible curriculum guide. Your attention to detail, commitment to accessibility, and patience with an author who kept revising ensured that the final product honors the content it contains.

## A Final Note

To everyone who asked a hard question, offered a dissenting perspective, or pushed back when something did not sit right: thank you. A healing-centered curriculum must itself be developed through a healing-centered process -- one that welcomes critique, sits with uncertainty, and trusts that the work will be stronger for having been questioned. Every challenge made this curriculum more honest, more nuanced, and more worthy of the children and educators it serves.
