# Acknowledgments

## Research and Development

The From Garden to Growth curriculum represents years of iterative development at Community Exceptional Children's Services. This work would not have been possible without the contributions of many individuals and organizations.

### Curriculum Development Team

- **Dr. Shawn A. Hearn** -- Curriculum author, 5Rs Framework architect, and principal investigator
- The educators, counselors, and support staff at Community Exceptional Children's Services who piloted early versions of this curriculum and provided invaluable feedback from daily practice
- The students whose resilience, honesty, and growth shaped every revision of this framework

### Research Foundations

This curriculum builds on the foundational work of researchers whose contributions to trauma-informed education, therapeutic horticulture, and cognitive science made this framework possible:

- **Dr. Stephen Porges** -- Polyvagal Theory and the neuroscience of safety
- **Dr. Daniel Siegel** -- Window of Tolerance and interpersonal neurobiology
- **Dr. Carol Dweck** -- Growth Mindset and motivation research
- **Dr. Barry Zimmerman** -- Self-Regulated Learning theory
- **Dr. David Kolb** -- Experiential Learning Theory
- **Dr. Vincent Felitti and Dr. Robert Anda** -- Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs) study
- **Dr. Bessel van der Kolk** -- Trauma and the body
- **Sharon Simson and Martha Straus** -- Therapeutic horticulture research

### Professional Organizations

Gratitude is extended to the following organizations for their resources, frameworks, and professional development opportunities that informed this work:

- **Collaborative for Academic, Social, and Emotional Learning (CASEL)** -- SEL framework and implementation guidance
- **National Child Traumatic Stress Network (NCTSN)** -- Trauma-informed practice resources
- **American Horticultural Therapy Association (AHTA)** -- Therapeutic horticulture standards and research
- **National Farm to School Network** -- Garden-based education advocacy and resources
- **KidsGardening (National Gardening Association)** -- School garden program support

### Community Partners

The curriculum's emphasis on community engagement reflects the real partnerships that supported its development:

- Local master gardener associations who volunteered expertise and mentorship
- University education and horticulture departments that provided research collaboration
- County extension services that supported soil testing, pest management, and seasonal planning
- Community mental health organizations that informed the trauma-informed practice components
- Family advocates who ensured parent and caregiver voices were represented throughout

### Technology Development

The digital components of this curriculum -- including the RootGuide AI tutor, the data collection platform, and the AI lesson plan generator -- were developed with input from educators, students, and families to ensure that technology serves pedagogy rather than the reverse.

## A Note on the Students

Above all, this curriculum exists because of the students at Community Exceptional Children's Services. Their willingness to try, to trust, to step into the garden even when they were uncertain -- that is the evidence that matters most. Every phase of the 5Rs, every prompt in the TRACE protocol, every regulation strategy in this curriculum was tested first in the lived experience of young people who deserved better than what traditional education was offering them.

They taught us how to teach them. This curriculum is our attempt to share what they showed us.
