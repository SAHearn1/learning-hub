# How to Use This Guide

## Navigating the FG2G Curriculum

This curriculum is organized into 13 sections plus appendices, designed to be read sequentially for first-time users and referenced selectively for experienced practitioners. Each section builds on the previous, but any section can stand alone as a reference document.

| Section | Title | Purpose |
|---------|-------|---------|
| **01** | Front Matter | Orientation, copyright, acknowledgments |
| **02** | Introduction | The "why" behind the curriculum -- rationale, narrative, and vision |
| **03** | Theoretical Foundation | Research base: ACEs, trauma-informed pedagogy, therapeutic horticulture |
| **04** | 5Rs Framework | The pedagogical core: Root, Regulate, Reflect, Restore, Reconnect |
| **05** | Grade Bands | Grade-specific implementation: K-2, 3-5, 6-8, 9-12 |

| **11** | Community Partnerships | Partnership models, family engagement, grants |
| **12** | Implementation | Step-by-step guide from readiness assessment to long-term sustainability |
| **13** | Conclusion | Vision and call to action |

## Reading Paths

### For New Adopters (First Read)

Follow the sections in order. The curriculum is designed so that each section provides the context needed for the next:

1. Start with **Section 02 (Introduction)** to understand the curriculum's purpose and origin.
2. Read **Section 03 (Theoretical Foundation)** to ground yourself in the research.
3. Study **Section 04 (5Rs Framework)** thoroughly -- this is the pedagogical backbone.
4. Move to your **grade band in Section 05** for specific implementation guidance.
5. Review **Section 07 (Living Learning Labs)** for garden design and management.
6. Complete with **Section 12 (Implementation)** for your launch roadmap.

### For Experienced Practitioners

Jump to the sections most relevant to your current needs:

- **Planning a lesson?** Section 05 (Grade Bands) + Section 06 (Instructional Strategies)
- **Designing a garden?** Section 07 (Living Learning Labs)
- **Assessing student growth?** Section 08 (Assessment)
- **Training new staff?** Section 09 (Professional Development)
- **Seeking funding?** Section 11 (Community Partnerships) + Section 12 (Implementation)
- **Student is dysregulated?** Section 04, Regulate phase

### For Administrators

Focus on the implementation and systems-level sections:

- **Section 02 (Introduction)** -- for communicating the program's value to stakeholders
- **Section 08 (Assessment)** -- for understanding outcome measurement
- **Section 09 (Professional Development)** -- for planning staff training
- **Section 12 (Implementation)** -- for budgeting, timelines, and scaling

## Key Concepts to Understand First

Before engaging with the curriculum content, ensure familiarity with these foundational concepts:

### The 5Rs Framework

Every learning interaction follows the five phases:

```
Root --> Regulate --> Reflect --> Restore --> Reconnect
```

- **Root**: Grounding and safety assessment ("Am I safe and ready to learn?")
- **Regulate**: Co-regulation and stabilization ("Can I return to my window of tolerance?")
- **Reflect**: Active learning and metacognition ("What am I thinking, and why?")
- **Restore**: Error recovery and growth ("What can I learn from this mistake?")
- **Reconnect**: Application and integration ("How does this connect to my world?")

### The TRACE Protocol

The cognitive engagement framework embedded within the Reflect phase:

- **T**hink -- Observe and activate prior knowledge
- **R**eason -- Develop strategic thinking
- **A**rticulate -- Explain thinking through discourse
- **C**heck -- Verify through alternative methods
- **E**xtend -- Transfer and generalize learning

### Regulation Levels

Students are assessed on a 0-100 regulation scale:

| Range | State | Instructional Response |
|-------|-------|----------------------|
| 81-100 | Optimal | Proceed to Reflect phase |
| 61-80 | Adequate | Brief grounding, then Reflect |
| 41-60 | Borderline | Extended grounding, reassess |
| 21-40 | Dysregulated | Move to Regulate phase |
| 0-20 | Crisis | Escalation protocol |

## Document Conventions

Throughout this curriculum, the following conventions are used:

- **Bold text** indicates key terms, framework components, or emphasis
- *Italic text* indicates quotes from students, educators, or researchers
- `Code blocks` contain technical specifications, data structures, or digital platform details
- Tables present structured information for quick reference
- Blockquotes contain educator language models -- specific phrases to use with students
- Garden metaphors are woven throughout; they are both instructional tools and organizational structures

## Using This Curriculum with the Digital Platform

The FG2G digital platform (described in Section 10) provides:

- **RootGuide AI Tutor**: Implements the 5Rs and TRACE protocol in digital learning sessions with real-time dysregulation detection
- **AI Lesson Plan Generator**: Creates standards-aligned, garden-based lesson plans from curriculum content
- **Data Collection Dashboard**: Tracks student regulation levels, reasoning move deployment, and academic progress
- **Portfolio System**: Digital portfolio management for student work samples and growth documentation

Curriculum documents are ingested into the platform's RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) system, ensuring that the AI tutor and lesson plan generator draw on the full depth of this curriculum when interacting with students and educators.
