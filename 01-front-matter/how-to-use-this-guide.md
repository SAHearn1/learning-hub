# How to Use This Guide

*From Garden to Growth* is designed to be a practical, flexible, and comprehensive resource for educators, counselors, administrators, and community partners. Whether you are implementing the full curriculum across a school or adapting individual lessons for a single classroom, this guide provides the structure you need while respecting the professional judgment you bring.

This section explains how the curriculum is organized, how to navigate its components, and how to make the most of its features in both digital and print formats.

---

## Overview of Curriculum Sections

The curriculum is organized into the following major sections, each serving a distinct purpose in the implementation process:

### Front Matter (Section 01)
The section you are currently reading. Includes the title page, copyright information, dedication, acknowledgments, foreword, and this navigation guide. Start here for context and orientation.

### Introduction (Section 02)
Provides the rationale for the curriculum, including the research base on ACEs and education, the theoretical foundations that inform the 5Rs Framework, and a narrative account of how the curriculum was developed. Read this section to understand the *why* behind the work before moving to the *how*.

### Theoretical Foundation (Section 03)
A deeper examination of the research literature supporting this curriculum, including ACEs and education impact, trauma-informed pedagogy, therapeutic horticulture, a comprehensive literature review, and the concept of dual-purpose pedagogy. This section is especially valuable for administrators seeking evidence for adoption decisions, grant writers documenting the research base, and educators pursuing deeper understanding of the science behind the practices.

### The 5Rs Framework (Section 04)
The heart of the curriculum. This section presents the five phases of the instructional framework in detail:

| Phase | Focus | Core Question |
|-------|-------|---------------|
| **Root** | Grounding and safety | "Where am I, and am I safe?" |
| **Regulate** | Nervous system regulation | "What is my body telling me?" |
| **Reflect** | Metacognition and meaning-making | "What am I learning about myself?" |
| **Restore** | Repair and reintegration | "How do I come back to myself and others?" |
| **Reconnect** | Community and purpose | "How do I belong, and what can I contribute?" |

Each phase includes a detailed overview, theoretical grounding, garden-based activities, classroom integration strategies, and assessment guidance.

### Grade Bands (Section 05)
Lesson plans and activities organized by developmental level:

- **K--2 (Ages 5--8):** Sensory-rich, play-based, and routine-oriented activities emphasizing safety, co-regulation, and concrete garden experiences.
- **3--5 (Ages 8--11):** Activities building autonomy, cooperative learning, and beginning metacognitive reflection through garden journaling, group projects, and seasonal observation.
- **6--8 (Ages 11--14):** Lessons addressing identity development, peer relationships, and abstract thinking through garden design, data collection, and community-facing projects.
- **9--12 (Ages 14--18):** Advanced activities incorporating leadership, advocacy, career exploration, and independent research connected to garden-based entrepreneurship and community engagement.

Each grade band contains five parts corresponding to the five phases of the 5Rs Framework, ensuring developmental continuity across the full K--12 spectrum.

### Instructional Strategies (Section 06)
Detailed guidance on teaching methodologies that align with trauma-informed, garden-based instruction, including differentiation strategies, Universal Design for Learning (UDL) integration, and culturally responsive adaptations.

### Living Learning Labs (Section 07)
Practical specifications for creating and maintaining garden spaces as learning environments, including design specifications, RootWork Regulation Pods, seasonal planning calendars, garden management protocols, and safety procedures.

### Assessment (Section 08)
A comprehensive assessment system that includes portfolio-based assessment, social-emotional learning measures, progress monitoring tools, rubrics aligned to each phase of the 5Rs Framework, and guidance on documenting growth in ways that honor the complexity of healing.

### Professional Development (Section 09)
Training materials for educators and staff, including a training overview, session plans for professional learning communities, a facilitator guide, a video library index, and credentialing pathways for educators seeking formal recognition of their trauma-informed, garden-based teaching expertise.

### Technology Integration (Section 10)
Guidance on using digital tools to support curriculum implementation, including the RootWork Learning Hub digital platform, AI-assisted lesson plan generation, and data collection tools for tracking student outcomes.

### Community Partnerships (Section 11)
Models for building and sustaining partnerships with families, community organizations, and funding sources, including partnership frameworks, family engagement strategies, connections to community organizations, and grant resources.

### Implementation (Section 12)
A step-by-step guide for school-wide and district-wide adoption, including readiness assessments, phased implementation timelines, common challenges and solutions, and sustainability planning.

### Conclusion (Section 13)
Reflections on the journey of implementation and a vision for the future of healing-centered, garden-based education.

### Appendices
Supplementary materials including reproducible templates, assessment forms, garden planning worksheets, plant guides, standards alignment charts, and additional resources.

## How to Use the 5Rs Framework

The 5Rs Framework is the structural spine of the entire curriculum. It can be used in three primary ways:

### 1. As a Lesson Sequence
Each individual lesson follows the 5Rs as a progression:
- **Root (5--7 minutes):** Begin with a grounding activity that establishes safety and presence. In the garden, this might be a sensory check-in with soil, plants, or weather observation.
- **Regulate (5--10 minutes):** Guide students through a regulation practice -- breathing exercises, mindful movement, or a garden-based body scan -- to bring the nervous system into the Window of Tolerance.
- **Reflect (10--15 minutes):** Introduce the core learning content with embedded metacognitive prompts. Students engage with garden-based activities while reflecting on their thinking, feelings, and learning processes.
- **Restore (5--10 minutes):** Provide time and structure for processing, repairing, and integrating the experience. This may include journaling, partner sharing, or quiet individual work in the garden.
- **Reconnect (5--7 minutes):** Close the lesson by reconnecting to the learning community, sharing insights, expressing appreciation, and linking the day's work to larger purposes.

### 2. As a Unit Architecture
Multi-week units can be organized so that entire lessons or lesson clusters correspond to a single phase of the 5Rs, allowing for deeper engagement with each phase over time.

### 3. As a Whole-School Framework
Schools implementing the curriculum at scale can adopt the 5Rs as a shared language for all adults and students, applying the framework to classroom transitions, restorative conversations, staff meetings, and family engagement events.

## Integrating with Existing School Programs

*From Garden to Growth* is designed to complement, not replace, existing school programs. Here are specific integration pathways:

### Standards Alignment
All lessons are aligned to Common Core State Standards, Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS), and Georgia Standards of Excellence. Standards alignment charts are provided in the appendices and within each grade band section. Educators can map lessons to their existing scope and sequence.

### Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) Programs
The 5Rs Framework aligns with the five core competencies of the Collaborative for Academic, Social, and Emotional Learning (CASEL): self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, and responsible decision-making. A CASEL alignment guide is included in the appendices.

### Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports (PBIS)
The curriculum's emphasis on regulation, reflection, and restoration aligns with Tier 1 and Tier 2 PBIS practices. The 5Rs can serve as the instructional component that teaches the self-regulation skills PBIS frameworks expect but do not always explicitly teach.

### Individualized Education Programs (IEPs)
The curriculum includes guidance for adapting lessons to meet the needs of students with IEPs, including specific accommodations for sensory sensitivities, motor differences, communication needs, and executive function support. The garden environment naturally offers multiple modalities of engagement, making it inherently compatible with individualized instruction.

### Existing Garden Programs
Schools that already have garden programs can adopt the 5Rs Framework as a trauma-informed overlay, adding intentional regulation, reflection, and restoration practices to existing garden activities without discarding the work already in place.

6. **Educator Reflection Prompts** -- Questions for the educator's own reflective practice

## Icons and Formatting Conventions

Throughout this curriculum, the following conventions are used to help you navigate content quickly:

### Section Markers

- **[ROOT]** -- Content related to the Root phase (grounding and safety)
- **[REGULATE]** -- Content related to the Regulate phase (nervous system regulation)
- **[REFLECT]** -- Content related to the Reflect phase (metacognition and meaning-making)
- **[RESTORE]** -- Content related to the Restore phase (repair and reintegration)
- **[RECONNECT]** -- Content related to the Reconnect phase (community and purpose)

### Content Type Indicators

- **Garden Note:** Practical horticultural information or gardening tips relevant to the activity
- **Trauma-Informed Reminder:** A brief note highlighting a trauma-informed consideration for the activity, such as sensory triggers, choice-giving language, or co-regulation strategies
- **Research Spotlight:** A callout box summarizing a key research finding that supports the practice described
- **Educator Reflection:** A prompt for the educator's own reflective practice and professional growth
- **Family Connection:** A suggestion for extending the activity to the home environment or engaging families
- **Adaptation Alert:** Specific guidance for modifying the activity for students with diverse needs, including IEP accommodations, English language learner support, and cultural responsiveness considerations
- **Assessment Checkpoint:** An embedded formative assessment opportunity with guidance on what to observe and how to document it

### Formatting Standards

- **Bold text** indicates key terms, framework phases, and critical concepts.
- *Italic text* indicates direct quotes, publication titles, and reflective prompts.
- Block quotes are used for student voices, research citations, and extended reflective passages.
- Tables summarize key information for quick reference.
- Numbered lists indicate sequential steps that should be followed in order.
- Bulleted lists indicate non-sequential items or options.

## Digital vs. Print Use

### Digital Format
The digital version of this curriculum, available through the RootWork Learning Hub platform, offers several advantages:

- **Searchability:** Full-text search across all sections and grade bands
- **Hyperlinked Navigation:** Click any cross-reference to jump directly to the referenced content
- **Interactive Assessment Tools:** Digital versions of assessment rubrics and progress monitoring forms that can be completed and saved electronically
- **Multimedia Integration:** Embedded links to video demonstrations, audio guided practices, and supplementary digital resources
- **Regular Updates:** Digital subscribers receive curriculum updates, new lesson plans, and revised assessment tools as they are developed
- **AI-Assisted Lesson Planning:** Access to the AI lesson plan generator that creates customized lesson plans based on your grade level, available garden resources, and specific student needs

### Print Format
The print version is designed for ease of use in the classroom and garden:

- **Lay-flat binding** allows the guide to remain open on a desk or garden table
- **Tab dividers** separate each major section for quick access
- **Reproducible pages** are clearly marked and may be photocopied for classroom use within the purchasing school
- **Weather-resistant cover** accommodates outdoor use in the garden setting
- **Wide margins** provide space for handwritten notes, adaptations, and reflections

### Recommended Approach
For most educators, a hybrid approach works best: use the digital version for planning and preparation, and keep the print version accessible in the classroom or garden for real-time reference during instruction. The appendices are designed to be particularly useful in print format, as they contain reproducible templates and worksheets meant for student use.

## Getting Started: A Recommended Path

If you are new to this curriculum, we recommend the following sequence:

1. **Read the Introduction (Section 02)** to understand the rationale, theoretical foundations, and origin story of the curriculum.
2. **Study the 5Rs Framework Overview (Section 04)** to internalize the framework before attempting to implement individual lessons.
3. **Select your grade band (Section 05)** and read the Part 1 (Root Phase) lessons first, as these establish the safety and grounding practices that all subsequent phases depend upon.
4. **Review the Living Learning Labs section (Section 07)** to assess your garden readiness and plan any necessary setup.
5. **Engage with the Professional Development materials (Section 09)** individually or, ideally, with a team of colleagues.
6. **Begin implementation** with the Root Phase lessons, allowing at least two to three weeks for students to internalize grounding routines before introducing Regulate Phase activities.
7. **Use the Assessment tools (Section 08)** from the first day to establish baselines and document growth over time.

There is no single correct way to use this curriculum. The recommended path above is designed for educators beginning a comprehensive implementation, but the modular structure allows you to enter at any point that serves your students' needs.
