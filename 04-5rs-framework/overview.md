# 5Rs Framework Overview

## Introduction

The 5Rs Framework is the pedagogical backbone of the RootWork curriculum, developed by Dr. Shawn A. Hearn at Community Exceptional Children's Services. Rooted in trauma-informed practice and therapeutic horticulture, the 5Rs provide a structured yet flexible sequence that guides every learning interaction -- whether in the classroom, the garden, or with the AI-powered RootGuide tutor.

The framework takes its name from the natural growth cycle: just as a plant must root before it can regulate its resources, reflect sunlight, restore after stress, and reconnect through fruiting and seeding, students move through developmental phases that honor both their emotional and cognitive needs.

## The Five Phases

| Phase | Focus | Core Question |
|-------|-------|---------------|
| **Root** | Grounding & Foundation | "Am I safe and ready to learn?" |
| **Regulate** | Co-Regulation & Stabilization | "Can I return to my window of tolerance?" |
| **Reflect** | Active Learning & Metacognition | "What am I thinking, and why?" |
| **Restore** | Error Recovery & Growth | "What can I learn from this mistake?" |
| **Reconnect** | Application & Integration | "How does this connect to my world?" |

## Theoretical Foundations

The 5Rs draw on several converging bodies of research:

### Polyvagal Theory (Porges, 2011)
Learning requires a felt sense of safety. The Root and Regulate phases directly address the autonomic nervous system's need for environmental and relational safety before higher-order cognitive processes can engage.

### Window of Tolerance (Siegel, 1999)
Students operate within a zone of optimal arousal. When pushed outside this zone -- into hyperarousal (fight/flight) or hypoarousal (freeze/shutdown) -- academic engagement becomes physiologically impossible. The Regulate phase provides co-regulation strategies to bring students back within their window.

### Growth Mindset and Error Reframing (Dweck, 2006)
The Restore phase operationalizes growth mindset by treating errors not as failures but as neurological events -- moments when synapses form new connections. This reframing reduces the shame response that often accompanies mistakes in traditional educational settings.

### Metacognition and Self-Regulated Learning (Zimmerman, 2002)
The Reflect phase explicitly develops metacognitive skills through the TRACE protocol (Think, Reason, Articulate, Check, Extend), teaching students to observe and direct their own thinking processes.

### Experiential Learning and Transfer (Kolb, 1984)
The Reconnect phase ensures that learning transfers beyond the immediate context, connecting academic concepts to real-world applications -- especially through garden-based and community-oriented activities.

## How the Phases Flow

The 5Rs are not strictly linear. While a typical session begins with Root and progresses through the sequence, the framework is responsive and cyclical:

```
                    ┌─────────────┐
                    │    ROOT     │
                    │ Grounding & │
                    │ Foundation  │
                    └──────┬──────┘
                           │
              ┌────────────┼────────────┐
              ▼                         ▼
     ┌────────────────┐       ┌────────────────┐
     │   REGULATE     │       │   REFLECT      │
     │ Co-Regulation  │◄─────►│   Active       │
     │ & Stabilization│       │   Learning     │
     └────────────────┘       └───────┬────────┘
                                      │
                              ┌───────┼────────┐
                              ▼                ▼
                     ┌────────────────┐  ┌────────────────┐
                     │   RESTORE     │  │  RECONNECT     │
                     │ Error Recovery │  │  Application   │
                     │  & Growth     │  │  & Integration │
                     └───────┬───────┘  └────────────────┘
                             │                ▲
                             └────────────────┘
```

**Key transition rules:**
- A student can move from Root directly to Reflect when grounding indicates readiness (regulation level > 60).
- At any point during Reflect, if dysregulation is detected (regulation level < 40), the student transitions to Regulate.
- From Regulate, students return to Root for a fresh grounding check before re-entering Reflect.
- When an error occurs during Reflect, the student moves to Restore for error analysis and reframing.
- After Restore, the student returns to Reflect to continue learning with new understanding.
- When learning objectives are met, the student moves to Reconnect for application and integration.
- Reconnect completes the cycle, setting up the next Root phase.

## The TRACE Protocol

Embedded within the Reflect phase but applicable across all phases, the TRACE protocol structures cognitive engagement:

- **Think**: "What do you notice first?" -- Activates observation and prior knowledge.
- **Reason**: "What's your plan of attack?" -- Develops strategic thinking.
- **Articulate**: "Talk me through your thinking." -- Builds mathematical/scientific discourse.
- **Check**: "How do you know you're right?" -- Develops self-verification habits.
- **Extend**: "What else could you do with this?" -- Promotes transfer and generalization.

## Reasoning Moves

The framework explicitly teaches seven reasoning moves that students learn to identify and deploy:

1. **DECOMPOSE** -- Break complex problems into manageable parts
2. **COMPARE** -- Identify similarities and differences between concepts
3. **QUESTION** -- Challenge assumptions and probe deeper
4. **VERIFY** -- Check work through alternative methods
5. **GENERALIZE** -- Identify patterns and form rules
6. **TRANSFORM** -- Reframe or represent a problem differently
7. **CONNECT** -- Link ideas across domains and to real-world contexts

## Garden Metaphor Integration

The 5Rs naturally align with the garden growth cycle, providing students with concrete metaphors for abstract learning processes:

| Phase | Garden Metaphor |
|-------|----------------|
| Root | Preparing the soil, planting seeds -- establishing the conditions for growth |
| Regulate | Watering and tending -- providing the right resources at the right time |
| Reflect | Photosynthesis -- actively converting inputs into growth |
| Restore | Pruning and composting -- turning setbacks into nourishment |
| Reconnect | Harvesting and sharing -- bringing the growth into the wider community |

## Grade Band Adaptations

The 5Rs are implemented differently across grade bands while maintaining fidelity to the framework's core principles:

- **K-2**: Emphasis on sensory grounding, co-regulation with adults, and concrete garden experiences. Phases are shorter and more structured.
- **3-5**: Introduction of metacognitive language, increased student ownership of regulation strategies, and beginning abstract reasoning.
- **6-8**: Full TRACE protocol implementation, peer regulation support, error analysis as a distinct skill, and community application projects.
- **9-12**: Student-directed phase transitions, advanced reasoning moves, mentorship roles, and real-world problem posing connected to garden-based enterprises.

## Alignment with Standards

The 5Rs framework is designed to work alongside, not replace, academic content standards. Each phase creates the conditions for standards-aligned learning:

- **Georgia Standards of Excellence (GSE)** -- Primary alignment framework
- **Common Core State Standards (CCSS)** -- Mathematical practices and literacy standards
- **Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS)** -- Science and engineering practices

The framework's emphasis on reasoning, metacognition, and real-world application directly supports the higher-order thinking demands embedded in modern academic standards.
