# Reconnect Phase

## Application & Integration

The Reconnect phase is the culmination of the learning cycle, where students apply what they have learned to real-world contexts, connect new knowledge to prior understanding, and integrate their academic growth with their broader lives. This phase ensures that learning does not remain abstract or confined to the classroom -- it becomes meaningful, transferable, and personally relevant.

Drawing on David Kolb's experiential learning theory, the Reconnect phase closes the learning loop by connecting academic content to lived experience, especially through garden-based and community-oriented applications.

## Purpose

1. **Apply learning** -- Transfer academic concepts to real-world contexts, especially garden-based and community settings.
2. **Integrate knowledge** -- Connect new learning to prior knowledge, other subjects, and personal experience.
3. **Celebrate growth** -- Acknowledge effort, reasoning development, and persistence, not just correct answers.
4. **Set forward intentions** -- Establish goals and questions for the next learning cycle.

## Key Activities

### Real-World Application

Students apply the session's learning to a concrete, meaningful context:

**Mathematics applications:**
- "We learned about ratios today. How could you use ratios in the garden to mix soil amendments?"
- "You figured out area calculation. Let's design a new raised bed for the spring garden."
- "We worked on percentages. What percentage of our seeds germinated this season?"

**Science applications:**
- "We studied photosynthesis. Let's design an experiment to test which location in the garden gets the best light."
- "You learned about the water cycle. How does our irrigation system connect to what we discussed?"
- "We explored ecosystems. Let's map the ecosystem of our school garden."

**Language Arts applications:**
- "We worked on persuasive writing. Write a letter to the principal about why the garden program matters."
- "You practiced observation skills. Write a detailed description of the changes in your plant this week."
- "We studied narrative structure. Tell the story of your garden plot this season -- beginning, middle, and where we are now."

### Cross-Curricular Connections

The Reconnect phase deliberately bridges subject boundaries:

- A math lesson on measurement connects to science through soil testing and to language arts through recording observations.
- A science lesson on plant biology connects to math through data collection and to language arts through research reporting.
- A language arts lesson on procedural writing connects to science through lab reports and to math through step-by-step problem solving.

### Community Connection

Learning extends beyond the individual to the community:

- **Sharing achievements**: Students present what they learned to peers, younger students, or family members.
- **Teaching others**: Students who have mastered a concept teach it to a peer, reinforcing their own understanding.
- **Community projects**: Garden harvests donated to local food banks, garden tours for community members, student-led workshops.
- **Family engagement**: Students bring garden produce home with attached recipes and math/science connections.

### Reflection and Metacognition

Students reflect on their learning process, not just their learning product:

- "What reasoning move was most helpful to you today?"
- "What was the hardest part, and how did you get through it?"
- "What question do you still have?"
- "What would you do differently if you solved this problem again?"

### Growth Journaling

Students document their growth in a personal journal:

- What they learned (content)
- How they learned it (process)
- How they felt about the learning (regulation)
- What they want to explore next (intention)

## Celebration of Growth

The Reconnect phase celebrates effort and reasoning rather than achievement:

- "You used DECOMPOSE and VERIFY today -- those are powerful reasoning moves."
- "I noticed you caught your own error before I said anything. That's real mathematical thinking."
- "You tried three different approaches before finding one that worked. That persistence is exactly what scientists do."
- "You explained your thinking so clearly that anyone could follow your reasoning."

Celebration is specific and process-oriented, avoiding empty praise ("Good job!" "You're so smart!") in favor of concrete recognition of thinking behaviors.

## Intention Setting

Each session ends with forward-looking intention setting:

- "What do you want to work on next time?"
- "Is there a question from today you'd like to explore further?"
- "What goal do you want to set for this week in the garden?"

This gives students agency and ownership over their learning trajectory, which is particularly important for students whose lives may otherwise feel unpredictable or out of their control.

## Garden Connection

The Reconnect phase aligns with harvesting and sharing -- the stage of the growth cycle where the results of planting, tending, and nurturing become visible and are distributed to the community.

Students can literally harvest produce from the garden during this phase, connecting the metaphorical harvest of knowledge to the physical harvest of food. Garden produce can be:

- Measured and weighed (mathematics)
- Analyzed for growth patterns (science)
- Described and documented (language arts)
- Prepared and shared (culinary arts and community engagement)
- Donated to those in need (social-emotional learning and civic responsibility)

## Grade Band Implementation

### K-2
- Show-and-tell: Share one thing learned with a partner
- Garden walk: Point out something in the garden connected to today's lesson
- Drawing: Illustrate the concept in their growth journal
- Simple goal: "Next time, I want to try..."

### 3-5
- Partner teaching: Explain the concept to a classmate
- Garden application: Complete a hands-on garden task connected to the lesson
- Written reflection: 3-5 sentences about what they learned and how
- Goal setting: Written intention for next session

### 6-8
- Peer presentation: Share reasoning with a small group
- Cross-curricular project: Connect to another subject area
- Extended journal reflection: Process, content, and regulation reflections
- Research question: Identify a question to investigate further

### 9-12
- Community application: Plan or execute a project connecting learning to the community
- Mentorship: Teach a concept to a younger student
- Portfolio documentation: Add session work to a growth portfolio
- Self-directed goal: Identify next steps in their learning trajectory

## Transition to Next Cycle

The Reconnect phase completes the current 5Rs cycle and sets up the next Root phase. When the session ends, the student carries forward:

- A documented learning experience in their growth journal
- A specific intention or question for the next session
- A sense of accomplishment grounded in process, not just product
- A strengthened connection between academic learning and their lived world

The next session begins again with Root -- grounding and checking in -- creating the predictable rhythm that trauma-informed practice requires.
